2.3Venous Thromboembolism

1. Definition

VTE = deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and its complication, pulmonary embolism (PE). Pregnancy raises the risk about 5 times; the puerperium even more. PE is a leading direct cause of maternal death, and most fatal PEs happen after birth.

2. Risk factors
Pre-existingPrevious VTE · thrombophilia · age > 35 · BMI > 30 · parity ≥ 3 · smoker · gross varicose veins · malignancy · SLE, heart or kidney disease, sickle cell
ObstetricCaesarean section (above all in labour) · pre-eclampsia · multiple pregnancy · prolonged labour · induction · PPH / APH · IUFD · PROM / PPROM · IVF
TransientImmobility · surgery in pregnancy · hyperemesis, dehydration · infection · long travel
Thrombophilia · congenital

Antithrombin III deficiency (highest risk) · protein C or S deficiency · factor V Leiden (commonest) · prothrombin gene variant.

Thrombophilia · acquired

Antiphospholipid syndrome: lupus anticoagulant or anticardiolipin antibodies + thrombosis or recurrent miscarriage.

3. Pathophysiology
VIRCHOW'S TRIAD · Pregnancy switches on all three. Why left? The right common iliac artery crosses and compresses the left iliac vein.
4. Clinical features

Exam trap

Leg swelling is common in normal pregnancy, but it is bilateral and painless. One painful swollen leg = DVT until proven otherwise: start LMWH, then scan.
