  • Atony after oxytocin, ergometrine and misoprostol → Bimanual compression, then balloon
  • Atony not controlled, wants more children → B-Lynch suture
7Tissue, trauma and thrombin
  • Retained placenta: not out 30 min after active management (60 min physiological) → manual removal in theatre + antibiotics; will not separate: suspect accreta
  • Trauma: good light, repair tears; rupture → laparotomy
  • Inversion: shock out of proportion, no fundus felt: push it back at once, then oxytocin
  • Thrombin: FFP 4 units per 6 units of red cells, platelets if under 50, cryoprecipitate if fibrinogen under 2 g/L
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Shock after cord traction, no fundus felt → Uterine inversion
8Secondary PPH
  • Causes: endometritis, retained products; rarely choriocarcinoma
  • Clues: pain, bulky uterus, open os, fever, offensive lochia
  • FBC, cultures, scan, β-hCG if it persists
  • IV cephalosporin + metronidazole, uterotonics, evacuation if tissue retained
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Bleeding weeks later with high β-hCG → Choriocarcinoma
9Prevention
  • Treat anaemia; high-risk women deliver where blood and theatre exist; partograph
  • Active third stage for every birth: oxytocin 10 IU IM within 1 min (or misoprostol 600 µg oral)
  • Delayed cord clamping 1–3 min
  • Controlled cord traction
  • Check the placenta is complete; uterine tone checks every 15 min for 2 h (WHO)
  • Watch every mother for the first 4 h
