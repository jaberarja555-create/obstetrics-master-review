13. Admission

History: onset and frequency of pains, show, leaking liquor, fetal movements, antenatal record (risk factors, previous CS). Examination: pulse, BP, temperature, pallor · fundal height, lie, presentation, fifths palpable, contractions, FHR · vaginal exam: dilatation, effacement, station, position, membranes, liquor, pelvis. Decide: low or high risk?

14. Management of the 1st stage
ProgressPartogram from 4 cm · vaginal exam every 4 h (every 2 h on oxytocin) · contractions every 30 min
FetusLow risk: intermittent auscultation (Pinard or hand Doppler) every 15–30 min, for 1 min after a contraction · high risk: continuous CTG
MotherPulse every 30 min · BP 4-hourly · temperature 2-hourly · urine: volume, protein, ketones
ComfortCompanion · move freely, upright · oral fluids, light food · empty bladder 2-hourly
AnalgesiaSupport, breathing → paracetamol → pethidine 50–100 mg IM (not near birth: neonatal depression) → epidural
Exam trap

Do not do routine ARM, enema, shaving or oxytocin in normal labour. ARM ± oxytocin is for slow progress, not for every woman.

15. Management of the 2nd stage

Diagnosis: full dilatation on vaginal exam; clues: urge to push, anus gaping, head visible. Fetus: FHR every 5 min (low risk) or continuous CTG (high risk). Mother: pulse, BP, exhaustion, bladder.

Primi2 h · 3 h with epidural
Multi1 h · 2 h with epidural
Not deliveredDelay: instrumental or CS
  1. Push with contractions only, in a position she likes; lithotomy for instruments.
  2. Crowning: one hand keeps the head flexed and slow, the other supports the perineum (Ritgen). Decide on episiotomy now.
  3. Head born → feel for cord round the neck (loosen, or clamp and cut if tight) → wait for restitution.
  4. Next contraction: gentle downward traction for the anterior shoulder, then upward for the posterior; body follows.
  5. Baby on mother's chest, dry, Apgar at 1 and 5 min.
