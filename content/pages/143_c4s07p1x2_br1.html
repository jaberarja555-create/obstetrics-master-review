4. Aetiology
  • Idiopathic: most term cases
  • Prematurity: the commonest association
  • Uterus: bicornuate, septate · fibroid (submucous, cervical)
  • Liquor: poly- or oligohydramnios
  • Placenta: praevia · cornual-fundal
  • Fetus: twins · hydrocephalus, anencephaly · neck goitre · IUFD
  • Pelvis: contracted · pelvic tumour
  • Previous breech · lax uterus (multipara)
5. Pathophysiology

Before 28 wk there is plenty of liquor and the fetus turns freely. Near term the pear-shaped uterus fits the fetus best head down: the bulky breech and legs fill the wide fundus. Anything that changes the space or shape of the uterus, or stops the fetus kicking round, leaves it breech.

6. Clinical features

History: discomfort under the ribs (hard head in the fundus) · kicks felt low. About 1 in 3 breeches are missed until labour.

7. Investigations
  • Ultrasound: confirms it; the standard
  • Type of breech · EFW
  • Head flexed or hyperextended
  • Placenta site · liquor volume
  • Anomaly scan (hydrocephalus, anencephaly)
  • Pelvimetry: no role
