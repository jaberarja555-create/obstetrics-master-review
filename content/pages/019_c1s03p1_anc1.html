1.3Antenatal Care

1. Introduction

Antenatal care (ANC) is the planned care given to a pregnant woman and her fetus from conception until the onset of labour. It is one of the most effective ways of reducing maternal and perinatal deaths, because most deaths from pre-eclampsia, anaemia, malaria and haemorrhage can either be prevented or detected early during antenatal visits.

Antenatal care is not only a series of examinations. At each contact the health worker prevents disease, looks for early signs of complications, decides whether the woman is at low or high risk, and teaches her and her family what to expect and when to seek help.

2. Aims of antenatal care
Medical aims
  • Prevention of disease by supplements, vaccines and protection against malaria
  • Early detection of hypertension, anaemia, diabetes, abnormal fetal growth and malpresentation
  • Risk classification, so that the place and method of birth can be planned
Educational and social aims
  • Health education about danger signs, nutrition, breastfeeding and family planning
  • Birth preparedness: the place of birth, transport, money, a blood donor and a companion
  • Building a relationship of trust, so that the woman returns for her visits
3. The booking visit

The first antenatal visit is called the booking visit. It should ideally take place before 12 weeks of pregnancy. Early booking allows accurate dating of the pregnancy, early start of folic acid, early detection of anaemia and infection, and screening tests to be done while there is still time to act on the results. It is the longest of the antenatal visits.

HistoryA full obstetric history is taken (see 7.2): the dates, parity, details of previous pregnancies, medical diseases, drugs, family history and social circumstances. This information is used for the risk assessment.
ExaminationHeight, weight and body mass index (BMI); blood pressure (BP); pallor; examination of the heart, chest and thyroid; the breasts if there is a complaint; the abdomen; and the legs.
Treatment startedIron and folic acid, the tetanus–diphtheria (Td) vaccine, and advice on malaria prevention and nutrition. Low-dose aspirin and calcium are added when indicated.
RecordsThe woman is given a hand-held antenatal card on which the dates, results and plan of care are written. She brings it to every visit and to the hospital in labour.
