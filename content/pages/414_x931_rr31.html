3Antiepileptic drugs and anomalies
  • Major anomaly risk about 6–8% with one drug, 8–16% with several
  • Valproate: neural tube defect (1–2%), heart, cleft, low IQ: avoid if she may conceive
  • Carbamazepine: NTD about 1%, cleft; enzyme inducer
  • Phenytoin: fetal hydantoin syndrome (cleft, nail and finger hypoplasia)
  • Phenobarbitone: heart, cleft, neonatal drowsiness
  • Lamotrigine: lowest risk
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Cleft lip, small nails, short fingers → Phenytoin
  • Spina bifida, mother on antiepileptics → Valproate
4Pre-pregnancy and antenatal care
  • Plan pregnancy; ideally fit-free for 2 years; one drug, lowest dose; switch from valproate before conception
  • Folic acid 5 mg from 3 months before conception
  • Never stop drugs suddenly: status epilepticus is more dangerous
  • Continue the same drugs; levels not routine
  • Anomaly scan, fetal echo, growth scans; BP and urine each visit
  • Enzyme inducers: vitamin K 1 mg IM to the baby at birth
🩺 Exam scenario
  • 7 wk pregnant on carbamazepine, fit-free → Continue it; folic acid 5 mg
5Status epilepticus
  • A fit over 5 min or repeated fits without recovery
  • IV lorazepam; hypoxia threatens mother and fetus
6Labour and after birth
  • Hospital birth; induction about 38 wk; vaginal birth (CS for obstetric reasons)
  • Continue drugs (IV if vomiting); avoid exhaustion and dehydration
  • Early epidural; avoid pethidine (its metabolite causes fits)
  • Breastfeeding encouraged; a dose raised in pregnancy returns to the old dose within 10 days
  • Screen for depression
  • Baby safety: feed and change on the floor, never bathe alone
