🩺 Exam scenario
  • High ALP only, no itch at 34 wk → Normal (placental ALP)
4Complications
  • Mother: itch, poor sleep, vitamin K deficiency → PPH; no lasting liver damage
  • Fetus: sudden stillbirth (risk rises with bile acids ≥ 100), meconium, preterm birth, neonatal respiratory distress
⚠️ Exam trap
  • A normal CTG does not exclude sudden fetal death in cholestasis: plan birth by the bile acid level
5Antenatal management
  • Ursodeoxycholic acid 8–12 mg/kg/day in 2 doses: itch improves in 1–7 days; no proven fetal benefit
  • Vitamin K (menadiol 10 mg daily) only if PT prolonged or steatorrhoea (RCOG 2022)
  • Emollients (aqueous cream with menthol); chlorphenamine only helps sleep
  • LFT and bile acids weekly; the peak level sets the birth timing
6Timing of birth
  • Peak bile acids 19–39: by 40 wk
  • 40–99: at 38–39 wk
  • ≥ 100: at 35–36 wk
  • Induction; CS for usual obstetric reasons; steroids if before 35 wk
  • Continuous CTG, active third stage
  • Local practice: fasting bile acids over 10, birth at 39 or 38 wk, vitamin K for all
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Bile acids 25 µmol/L at 34 wk → UDCA, birth by 40 wk
  • Bile acids 130 µmol/L at 34 wk → Plan birth at 35–36 wk
7After birth
  • Itch goes within days; check LFT before discharge or at 6 wk
  • Still abnormal: look for chronic liver disease
  • Recurs in 45–70% of later pregnancies
  • Avoid oestrogen pills: use progestogen-only or IUCD
