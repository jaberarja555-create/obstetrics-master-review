4. Investigations: making the diagnosis (continued)
Exam trap

Absent heart sounds on a Pinard or hand Doppler do not prove death: obesity, polyhydramnios or a bad angle mislead. Only a real-time scan confirms. Signs on X-ray are late and no longer needed.

Clinical scenarios
32 wk, no fetal movements for 2 days, no heart on hand Doppler. Next step?
Real-time ultrasound to confirm.
Gas in the fetal heart and great vessels on X-ray?
Robert sign: earliest X-ray sign of death.
5. Investigations: looking for the cause
  • Mother now: FBC, clotting + fibrinogen, urine protein, LFT, RFT
  • Glucose / OGTT · malaria film · TORCH, syphilis
  • Blood group; Rh-negative → Kleihauer · TFT if symptoms
  • At 6 wk: thrombophilia, APS, lupus anticoagulant
  • Fetus before birth: detailed scan: anomaly, growth, liquor, twins, retroplacental clot
  • Karyotype (amniocentesis, or cord or skin after birth)
  • After birth: autopsy (MRI, X-ray if refused) · placental histology
  • Parental karyotype if abnormal
6. Complications

DIC (hypofibrinogenaemia) = most serious: thromboplastin leaks from the dead fetus; ≈ 10% in the first 3 wk, ≈ 30% if retained > 3 wk · infection, sepsis (especially after ROM) · PPH · psychological: grief, depression.

7. Management
InductionMifepristone 200 mg oral, then 36–48 h later misoprostol
< 27 wkMisoprostol 200 µg PV or SL 4–6-hourly
27–28 wkMisoprostol 100 µg PV 4-hourly
> 28 wkMisoprostol 25 µg PV 6-hourly or 25 µg oral 2-hourly
Previous CSLower doses, watch for rupture · ≥ 2 CS or classical scar: Foley + oxytocin, or CS

Expectant: most labour within 3 wk. Check clotting 2–3 times a week; induce if clotting falls, infection appears or she changes her mind. Most women and doctors prefer induction: shorter grief, no DIC risk.

Avoid ARM (infection) · caesarean only for a usual indication (e.g. praevia, transverse lie) · good analgesia, epidural only if clotting is normal.
