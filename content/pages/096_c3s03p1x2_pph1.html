4. Risk factors
ToneOverdistension: twins, polyhydramnios, macrosomia · grand multipara · fibroids · long or fast labour · oxytocin · chorioamnionitis · MgSO₄, nifedipine, halothane · anaemia
TraumaInstrumental delivery · precipitate labour · macrosomia · malposition · VBAC
TissuePlacenta accreta · succenturiate lobe · previous CS or myomectomy · pulling the cord before separation
ThrombinDIC: abruption, pre-eclampsia, HELLP, IUFD, sepsis, amniotic fluid embolism · von Willebrand · anticoagulants
Clinical scenarios
Twins, 18 h labour on oxytocin. Bleeds 900 mL after birth. Likeliest cause?
Uterine atony (overdistension + long, augmented labour).
Months after a massive PPH she cannot breastfeed and has no periods. Diagnosis?
Sheehan syndrome (pituitary necrosis).
5. Pathophysiology

The placental bed takes 500–800 mL/min at term. After separation the criss-cross fibres clamp the vessels: "living ligatures". A tired, stretched uterus cannot.

Exam trap

Loss is underestimated: blood hides as clot in a large, soft uterus. BP holds until late: tachycardia comes first; low BP = ≥ 30% of volume gone.

6. Investigations

FBC, cross-match 4–6 units · PT, aPTT, fibrinogen · RFT, LFT · bedside clot test: no clot in 7–10 min = coagulopathy. Do not wait for results.

7. Complications

Early: shock · DIC · AKI, liver, heart failure · death. Of treatment: transfusion reactions, overload · hysterectomy. Late: anaemia · sepsis · Sheehan.
