33. Classification & risk factors

Type I = velamentous cord insertion · type II = vessels joining the lobes of a bilobed placenta or a succenturiate lobe. Risk factors: these, low-lying placenta or praevia, IVF, twins.

34. Pathophysiology

A term baby has only about 250–300 mL of blood (80 mL/kg). A tear of one vessel drains it in minutes, so about 100 mL can kill. The mother stays well because she loses nothing. The presenting part can also press the vessels and cause fetal distress before any bleed.

35. Clinical features

Triad: ROM + painless, small bleed + fetal distress (bradycardia, or a sinusoidal CTG from fetal anaemia). Mother stable, uterus soft. Vessels may be felt pulsating in the membranes on VE.

36. Investigations

Antenatal: transvaginal colour Doppler if the 20 wk scan shows a risk factor. In a bleed: Apt test or Kleihauer, but never wait for it.

37. Management
Known before labour
  • Admit at 30–32 wk, steroids
  • Caesarean at 34–36 wk, before ROM
  • No ARM, no sweep
Bleeding in labour
  • 1st stage: immediate caesarean
  • Fully dilated, head low: instrumental (fastest)
  • Neonatal team, O-neg blood
