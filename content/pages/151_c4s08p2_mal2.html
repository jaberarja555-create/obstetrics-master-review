7. Clinical features

Abdomen: head high · deep groove between head and back; the head's prominence is on the same side as the back.

PV is diagnostic, usually in labour. Facial oedema can make a face feel like a breech: look for gums and the triangle of mouth and malar bones.

8. Management
Face
  • Mento-anterior: await vaginal birth; chin passes under the pubis, head born by flexion
  • Mento-posterior: most rotate to MA; wait if progress is good
  • Persistent MP → caesarean: the neck is already fully extended
  • Low forceps allowed for MA · no ventouse, no scalp electrode or FBS on the face
Brow
  • Early labour: may flex to vertex or extend to face; watch a short time
  • Persistent brow → caesarean
  • No forceps, ventouse or oxytocin
  • Never leave in labour: obstruction, rupture, fistula

Complications: prolonged or obstructed labour · perineal tears · PPH · fetal: facial bruising and oedema (warn the parents; it fades in days), hypoxia.

Abnormal Lie and Compound Presentation

9. Definition

Transverse lie: fetal long axis across the uterus; presents by the shoulder (denominator = acromion; commonest position left dorso-anterior). Oblique lie: axis at an angle, a lower pole in an iliac fossa. Unstable lie: lie keeps changing after 36 wk. Incidence: ≈ 1 in 300 at term.
