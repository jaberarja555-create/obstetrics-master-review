Breastfeeding

Do not stop breastfeeding. Sertraline and paroxetine pass into milk in tiny amounts. Avoid fluoxetine (long half-life, builds up in the baby) and doxepin.

Breastfeeding mother, 7 wk postpartum, moderate depression. Drug of choice?
Sertraline; continue breastfeeding.

Puerperal Psychosis

15. Definition

An acute psychotic illness starting within the first 2–4 weeks after delivery, in 1–2 per 1000 births. Most cases are an episode of bipolar disorder; a few are severe depression with psychosis or schizophrenia.

16. Risk factors
  • Previous puerperal psychosis
  • Bipolar disorder
  • Previous severe non-postpartum depression or psychosis
  • Family history (1st or 2nd degree) of bipolar or puerperal psychosis
  • Primigravida · stopping a mood stabiliser in pregnancy
17. Clinical features

Sudden onset, often after a few nights of no sleep: restless, agitated, confused and perplexed, rapidly changing mood (mania, depression or mixed), delusions (e.g. the baby is evil or swapped) and hallucinations, bizarre thoughts, inappropriate response to the baby, neglect. She is unaware she is ill. Life-threatening to mother and baby.

18. Differential diagnosis

Rule out organic causes before calling it psychiatric: sepsis delirium and malaria, eclampsia, cerebral vein thrombosis, hypoglycaemia, low Na, drugs or withdrawal. Severe blues or depression have no delusions and insight is kept.
