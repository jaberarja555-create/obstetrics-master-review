5. Expected date of delivery and gestational age

By convention, gestational age is counted from the first day of the last normal menstrual period (LMP), and not from the day of conception, which occurs about two weeks later. A pregnancy at term lasts on average 280 days (40 weeks) from the LMP. Accurate dating is essential, because many decisions, such as giving steroids before preterm birth or inducing labour after 41 weeks, depend on the gestational age.

Naegele's ruleEDD = LMP + 7 days − 3 months + 1 year (equivalent to LMP + 9 months + 7 days). For example, if the LMP was 10 January 2026, the expected date of delivery (EDD) is 17 October 2026.
Long or short cyclesThe rule assumes a 28-day cycle. For a longer cycle, add the extra days (a 35-day cycle: add 7 days); for a shorter cycle, subtract the difference.
When the LMP is unreliableThe woman is unsure of the date, her cycles are irregular, she used the pill or an injectable in the previous 3 months, she conceived while breastfeeding, or she bled in early pregnancy. In these cases the pregnancy is dated by ultrasound.
Gestational age todayCount the days from the LMP to today and divide by 7, or use a pregnancy wheel. Gestational age is written as weeks plus days (for example, 32⁺³ weeks).

Dating by ultrasound. The earlier the scan, the more accurate it is, because early fetuses grow at the same rate, while later fetuses differ in size.

If the early scan and the LMP differ by more than 5 days, the scan date is used. Once a pregnancy has been dated by a reliable early scan, it is never re-dated by a later scan: a fetus that is small later in pregnancy has growth restriction, not "wrong dates".
