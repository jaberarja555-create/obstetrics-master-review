10. Risk factors the history must find
  • Previous CS, myomectomy, uterine surgery
  • Previous PPH, retained placenta, abruption
  • Previous pre-eclampsia, stillbirth, preterm birth
  • Recurrent miscarriage, previous baby with anomaly
  • Medical disease: heart, diabetes, hypertension, epilepsy, sickle cell
  • Age < 18 or ≥ 35; grand multipara; short interval
  • Rh negative with antibodies; HIV, hepatitis B
  • Twins now; infertility treatment

History in an emergency

A bleeding or fitting woman cannot give a full history: resuscitate first, and ask the companion in a few lines: gestation, parity, previous CS, what happened and when, bleeding amount, fits or headache, fetal movements, allergies, drugs, last meal. Complete the history once she is stable.

12. Presenting the history
Presenting the history: one opening line

"Huda, 28, from Shendi, G3 P2+0, at 32⁺³ weeks by a sure LMP confirmed by an early scan, presented with painless vaginal bleeding for 3 hours. Her previous two births were by caesarean section." Then say only what is relevant, ending with a summary and your top differential.

Common mistakes in the long case
  • Parity written wrongly (twins, stillbirths, current pregnancy)
  • No gestational age, or GA not checked against the scan
  • Complaint not analysed (no amount of bleeding, no fetal movements)
  • Previous CS without the indication and the incision type
  • Ending without a summary and a diagnosis
