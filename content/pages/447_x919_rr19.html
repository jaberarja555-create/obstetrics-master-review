2.4Diabetes in pregnancy
Diabetes in pregnancy is either pre-existing (type 1 or 2), gestational (glucose intolerance first found in pregnancy, gone after birth; 85–90%), or overt diabetes first found in pregnancy at diabetic levels (treated as pre-existing). Good glucose control prevents most of its harm to mother and baby.
1Risk factors for GDM
  • Strong (screen with OGTT): previous GDM, BMI over 30, previous macrosomic baby, first-degree relative with diabetes
  • Others: age 35 or more, PCOS, multiparity, previous unexplained stillbirth, steroids
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • High glucose at 26 wk, normal 6 wk after birth → GDM
  • Fasting 7.8 at 10 wk → Overt diabetes: treat as pre-existing
2Pathophysiology
  • From 20 wk placental hormones (hPL, cortisol) cause insulin resistance
  • Pedersen hypothesis: glucose crosses the placenta, insulin does not → fetal hyperinsulinaemia → macrosomia, RDS, neonatal hypoglycaemia
  • Anomalies need high glucose in weeks 3–8: pre-existing diabetes only, not GDM
  • Features: often none; candida, UTI, large for dates, polyhydramnios (fetal polyuria)
  • Glycosuria is a poor test (low renal threshold)
3Screening and the 75 g OGTT
  • Previous GDM: OGTT at 16 wk; normal → repeat at 24–28 wk
  • Other risk factors: OGTT at 24–28 wk
  • Pre-existing: no OGTT; HbA1c at booking
  • Normal diet 3 days, overnight fast, 75 g glucose, 2 h sample
  • GDM: fasting ≥ 5.6 mmol/L (100 mg/dL) or 2 h ≥ 7.8 (140): any one value
  • Overt: fasting ≥ 7.0 (126) or 2 h ≥ 11.1 (200)
  • Mg/dL ÷ 18 = mmol/L
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Fasting 5.8, 2 h 7.0 → GDM (one value is enough)
  • Glycosuria 1+, OGTT normal → Low renal threshold: normal
