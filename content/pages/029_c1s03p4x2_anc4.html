14. Minor complaints of pregnancy

Minor complaints are symptoms caused by the normal physiological changes of pregnancy (see 1.1). They are not dangerous, but they can be distressing. The health worker should explain the cause, give simple advice, and make sure that a serious condition is not being missed.

ComplaintCauseManagement
Nausea and vomitingHuman chorionic gonadotrophin (hCG), which peaks at 8–12 weeksSmall, dry, frequent meals and ginger; an antiemetic if needed. Ketones in the urine or weight loss suggest hyperemesis gravidarum (see 2.1).
HeartburnProgesterone relaxing the oesophageal sphincter, and pressure on the stomachSmall meals, avoiding late meals, raising the head of the bed, and antacids.
Constipation and haemorrhoidsSlow bowel movement and raised venous pressurePlenty of fluids and fibre. Iron tablets may make it worse. A laxative such as lactulose may be given.
Backache and pelvic painRelaxin loosening the joints, and increased lumbar lordosisGood posture, flat shoes, physiotherapy and paracetamol.
Leg cramps and varicose veinsSlow venous flow in the legsStretching exercises, raising the legs, and support stockings.
Frequency of micturitionPressure of the uterus on the bladderThis is normal. Pain on passing urine, fever or loin pain suggest infection and need a urine culture.
Vaginal dischargeIncreased secretions; candida infectionA white discharge without smell or itching is normal. An itchy, curd-like discharge is candidiasis and is treated with a clotrimazole pessary.
Fainting; tingling of the handsLow BP and compression of the inferior vena cava; carpal tunnel syndromeAvoid lying flat on the back or standing for long, and check the haemoglobin. A wrist splint at night helps carpal tunnel syndrome.
Clinical scenario
A woman has heartburn at 30 weeks. What is the first advice?
Small, frequent meals, no late meals and raising the head of the bed; an antacid if needed.
