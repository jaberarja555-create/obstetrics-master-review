2.9Obstetric Cholestasis

1. Definition

Obstetric cholestasis (intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy) = itching without a rash + raised bile acids and/or abnormal LFTs, with no other cause, that resolves after delivery. Usually the 3rd trimester. A diagnosis of exclusion.

Clinical picture
Obstetric cholestasis
Itchy palms + soles+no rash+bile acids ↑=Cholestasis
2. Risk factors
  • Family history in 1 of 3 women · previous obstetric cholestasis
  • More common in women of South Asian origin · twins (more hormone)
3. Pathophysiology
PATHOPHYSIOLOGY · Pregnancy hormones slow bile flow in a susceptible liver. The mother itches; the fetus is the one at risk.
4. Clinical features
  • Pruritus without a rash: palms and soles first, then trunk and limbs
  • Worse at night: poor sleep, tiredness
  • Anorexia, malaise
  • Less often: epigastric discomfort, dark urine, pale stool, steatorrhoea
  • Mild jaundice in a few
  • Exam: only scratch marks (excoriations)
5. Investigations
Bile acids> 10 µmol/L (fasting). The key test; ≥ 100 = high fetal risk
LFTALT, AST, GGT 2–3 × normal. ALP is not useful (made by the placenta)
ClottingPT: vitamin K deficiency
Exclude othersUS liver and biliary tree · viral serology (hepatitis A, B, C, E, CMV, EBV) · antimitochondrial and anti-smooth muscle antibodies
Best next step

Itching + normal tests: repeat LFT and bile acids every 1–2 wk. The itch can come weeks before the blood tests turn abnormal.
