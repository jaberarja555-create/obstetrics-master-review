4. The schedule of visits: eight contacts

The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends a minimum of eight contacts during pregnancy. Most of them are in the third trimester, when pre-eclampsia, fetal growth problems and malpresentation usually appear.

The WHO eight-contact model · one contact in the first trimester, two in the second and five in the third. Women with complications are seen more often.

Each follow-up visit has the same three parts: the health worker asks about symptoms, checks the mother and fetus, and then acts on the findings.

AskAbout her general wellbeing and fetal movements, and about bleeding, headache or visual disturbance, swelling, fever, leaking of fluid and contractions. Ask whether she is taking her iron tablets.
CheckBlood pressure and urine for protein (and glucose); weight when useful; pallor and oedema; the symphysis–fundal height (SFH) from 24 weeks; the fetal heart; and the fetal presentation from 36 weeks.
ActReview the results, give the next doses of drugs and vaccines, reassess the risk, teach one health topic, and book the next visit.

5. Booking investigations

Routine investigations are offered to every woman at the booking visit. Their purpose is to detect conditions that cause no symptoms but can harm the mother or baby, such as anaemia, infections that pass to the newborn, and blood group incompatibility.

TestPurposeAction if abnormal
Complete blood count (CBC)To detect anaemia (haemoglobin below 11 g/dL) and a low platelet countRequest iron studies and a malaria film, and treat the cause (see 2.2). The haemoglobin is repeated at 28 weeks.
Blood group and Rhesus (Rh) factorTo identify RhD-negative women and red cell antibodiesAntibody screen at booking and at 28 weeks; plan anti-D prophylaxis.
Urine examinationProtein, glucose, and asymptomatic bacteriuria (by urine culture)Bacteriuria is treated, because this prevents pyelonephritis and preterm labour.
Blood glucoseTo detect pre-existing diabetes (fasting or random blood sugar)An oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) is done at 24–28 weeks in women with risk factors (at 16 weeks if she had gestational diabetes before).
