8. Prevention of malaria

Malaria is more severe in pregnancy because of the reduced cell-mediated immunity, and the parasites collect in the placenta. It causes maternal anaemia, miscarriage, low birth weight and stillbirth. In areas where malaria is common, three measures are used together: intermittent preventive treatment, insecticide-treated bed nets, and early diagnosis and treatment of every fever.

Intermittent preventive treatment in pregnancy (IPTp)

Sulfadoxine–pyrimethamine (SP), 3 tablets (500/25 mg), is swallowed in the clinic under observation at the first visit after quickening (16–20 weeks). A second dose is given at least 4 weeks later. SP is not given in the first trimester or to women taking cotrimoxazole. Women receiving SP should take folic acid 0.4 mg, not 5 mg, because high-dose folic acid reduces the effect of SP.

Bed nets and early treatment

The woman should sleep under an insecticide-treated net every night from her first visit. Any fever in pregnancy requires a malaria blood film or rapid diagnostic test (RDT) on the same day, and a positive result is treated according to the national protocol (see 2.6).

9. Health advice

Advice is given gradually over the antenatal visits, one or two topics at a time, in simple language that the woman and her family can understand.

DietA varied diet containing protein, vegetables, fruit and beans. Only about 300 extra kilocalories per day are needed, and only in the second half of pregnancy; the idea of "eating for two" is a myth. Meat should be well cooked and vegetables washed. Strong tea and coffee should not be taken with meals, because they reduce iron absorption.
Harmful substancesSmoking, shisha and toombak (oral tobacco) and alcohol should be avoided. No drugs or herbal remedies should be taken without medical advice; examples of harmful drugs are non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) after 30 weeks, tetracycline and warfarin. X-rays are avoided unless necessary, and the abdomen is shielded.
Daily lifeNormal work and moderate exercise such as walking are encouraged. Late in pregnancy she should rest lying on her left side. Sexual intercourse is safe unless there is bleeding, placenta praevia or ruptured membranes. A seat belt should be worn below the abdomen.
BreastfeedingThe baby should be breastfed exclusively for 6 months, starting within the first hour after birth. Colostrum alone is enough for the newborn in the first days.
