6.1Normal Puerperium

1. Definition

Puerperium = the time from delivery of the placenta until the genital organs return to the non-pregnant state: about 6 weeks (42 days). Immediate = first 24 h (PPH, eclampsia) · early = to day 7 · remote = to 6 wk. Its main dangers: sepsis, haemorrhage, VTE, mood disorders.

2. Physiological changes: the genital tract
Involution · the fundus falls about 1 cm (one finger) a day; a uterus still felt above the pubis after day 14 = subinvolution.
OrganWhat happens
UterusFirm, at the umbilicus after birth. Contraction kinks the vessels ("living ligatures") and stops bleeding. Muscle cells shrink by autolysis; they are not lost. Oxytocin from suckling speeds it (after-pains)
EndometriumDecidua necroses and sheds as lochia; new lining grows from the basal layer by day 10. The placental site heals last (≈ 6 wk) by exfoliation, without a scar
CervixAdmits 2–3 fingers for 4–6 days, 1 finger by day 10–14. External os stays a transverse slit (parous os)
Vagina, perineumOedema settles in days; rugae return by 4 wk (less than before). Hymen tags = carunculae myrtiformes
Clinical picture
Normal involution
Umbilicus day 1+− 1 cm a day+gone by day 14=Normal
Clinical scenarios
Day 7 after a normal delivery. Where should the fundus be?
About midway between umbilicus and pubis.
From which layer does the endometrium regenerate after birth?
The basal layer of the decidua.
