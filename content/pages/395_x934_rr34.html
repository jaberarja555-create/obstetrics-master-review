2.10Acute fatty liver of pregnancy
Acute fatty liver of pregnancy is acute liver failure from microvesicular fat in liver cells, in the third trimester or just after birth. It is rare (about 1 in 10 000) but deadly: an obstetric emergency treated by stabilising and delivering.
1Risk factors and pathophysiology
  • Twins, male fetus (about 3 : 1), coexisting mild PE, first pregnancy, low BMI
  • Fault in mitochondrial fatty-acid oxidation, often a fetal LCHAD deficiency
🩺 Exam scenario
  • AFLP mother; newborn later has low glucose → Test the baby for LCHAD deficiency
2Clinical features
  • Days of nausea, vomiting, malaise
  • Epigastric or RUQ pain
  • Jaundice, headache
  • Polyuria, polydipsia (transient diabetes insipidus)
  • Confusion → coma
  • Mild hypertension ± proteinuria
3Investigations
  • Hypoglycaemia: check often
  • PT/INR raised, fibrinogen low → DIC
  • Bilirubin raised; ALT/AST moderately raised (usually under 1000)
  • Marked hyperuricaemia, raised creatinine
  • WBC raised; platelets low (milder than HELLP)
  • Acidosis, raised ammonia and lactate
  • Ultrasound: bright liver; mainly to exclude others
  • Swansea criteria: 6 or more of 14 features
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Jaundice, glucose 40 mg/dL, INR 2 at 35 wk → AFLP
  • RUQ pain, platelets 40, BP 170/110 → HELLP
