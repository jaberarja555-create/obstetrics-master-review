🩺 Exam scenario
  • Scalp swelling limited by suture lines, growing on day 2 → Cephalhaematoma
6First stage care
  • Admission: history, vitals, abdomen (fifths, contractions, FHR), VE
  • Partogram from 4 cm; VE every 4 h (2 h on oxytocin); contractions every 30 min
  • Low risk: intermittent auscultation every 15–30 min for 1 min after a contraction; high risk: continuous CTG
  • Pulse 30 min, BP 4-hourly, temperature 2-hourly, urine
  • Companion, mobile, fluids, empty bladder
  • Pain relief ladder to epidural; pethidine not near birth
⚠️ Exam trap
  • No routine ARM, enema, shaving or oxytocin in normal labour
7Second stage care
  • Full dilatation on VE; urge to push, head visible
  • FHR every 5 min (low risk)
  • Push with contractions
  • Control the head at crowning, support the perineum
  • Feel for cord round the neck; clamp and cut if tight
  • Gentle downward traction for the anterior shoulder, then upward
  • Episiotomy not routine: mediolateral for instruments, fetal distress, shoulder dystocia, breech, tight perineum
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Multi, fully dilated 1 h, no epidural → Delay: assess for instrumental birth
8Third stage
  • Separation through the spongy decidua; crossed fibres act as living ligatures
  • Schultze (80%, fetal surface first) or Matthews Duncan (20%, edge first, more bleeding)
  • Signs: fundus rises and firms, cord lengthens, gush of blood
  • Active management (AMTSL): oxytocin 10 IU IM within 1 min (misoprostol 600 µg oral if none) + controlled cord traction with counter-pressure; then check uterine tone (WHO)
