8. Status epilepticus

A fit > 5 min, or repeated fits without recovery: hypoxia threatens mother and fetus.

Clinical picture
Status epilepticus
Fit > 5 min+BP normal=Lorazepam IV
9. Management: intrapartum
  • Plan induction at 38 wk in hospital; aim for vaginal birth (caesarean for obstetric reasons only).
  • Continue drugs (IV if vomiting) · good hydration · continuous CTG.
  • Avoid triggers: exhaustion, no sleep, dehydration, hyperventilation. Early epidural; avoid pethidine (its metabolite causes fits).
10. Management: postpartum
  • Breastfeed: encouraged. Watch baby for drowsiness, poor feeding (phenobarbitone)
  • Dose raised in pregnancy? Return to the old dose within 10 days (toxicity)
  • Screen for depression (risk about 29%)
  • Baby safety: feed and change diapers on the floor; never bathe baby alone; sleep when baby sleeps
  • Family: put her in left lateral during a fit
Exam trap · contraception

Enzyme inducers (carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbitone, topiramate) make the combined pill, POP and implant fail. Use copper IUD, LNG-IUS or DMPA injection. Combined pill also lowers lamotrigine levels: fits.

Distinguishing features
Known epileptic fits at 36 wk, BP 160/110Treat as eclampsia: MgSO₄
Fit, fever 39 °C, BP normalCerebral malaria
Fit on insulin, sweatyHypoglycaemia: dextrose
On carbamazepine, wants the pillCopper IUD / DMPA
