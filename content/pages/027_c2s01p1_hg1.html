2.1Hyperemesis Gravidarum

1. Definition
Morning sicknessNausea on getting up; about half of pregnant women. Starts 4–6 wk, gone by about 12 wk
Emesis gravidarumActual vomiting, mostly mornings; she stays hydrated
Hyperemesis gravidarumVomiting all day until she cannot keep up fluids and nutrition: dehydration, ketonuria, electrolyte upset, weight loss > 5%
Clinical picture
Hyperemesis
Vomiting < 20 wk+dehydration+ketonuria+weight loss > 5%=HG
2. Aetiology & risk factors

Cause: unclear. Main theory: high hCG (peaks at 9–12 wk, like the vomiting). Others: oestrogen, psychological.

  • Multiple pregnancy · molar pregnancy (more hCG)
  • Primigravida · previous HG
  • Thyrotoxicosis
Distinguishing features
Vomiting at 9 wk, uterus large for datesMolar pregnancy / twins
Vomiting + fever, loin painPyelonephritis
Vomiting, low TSH, high T4, no goitreGestational thyrotoxicosis
Vomiting starts at 24 wkNot HG: find the cause
3. Pathophysiology
Clinical scenario
10 wk, vomiting all day, ketones 3+, Na 128, K 2.9. Acid–base picture?
Hypochloraemic hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis.
