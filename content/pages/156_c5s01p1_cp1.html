5.1Cord Prolapse

1. Definition
TypeMembranesCord position
Cord presentationIntactCord lies below the presenting part, felt through the membranes
Cord prolapse (overt)RupturedCord below the presenting part, in the vagina or out of the vulva
Occult prolapseEitherCord beside the presenting part, not felt; suspect from FHR dips

About 1 in 300–500 deliveries. A true emergency: the fetus can die in minutes.

Clinical scenario
Intact membranes, cord felt below the head on VE. Name it.
Cord presentation.
2. Risk factors

Anything that leaves the presenting part not fitting the pelvis, so the cord can slip past it when the membranes rupture.

  • Malpresentation: breech (footling most; prolapse in about 15% of footling, 5% of complete and 0.5% of frank breeches), transverse, oblique, unstable lie
  • Preterm, low birth weight · PPROM
  • Twins: second twin after the first is born
  • Polyhydramnios: gush at ROM washes the cord down
  • Multiparity: lax uterus, high head
  • High head: CPD, contracted pelvis, praevia
  • Fetal anomaly (anencephaly) · long cord
  • Procedures: ARM with a high head, ECV, internal version, balloon induction
3. Pathophysiology
Why the fetus suffers

Two things stop flow in the cord: compression between the presenting part and the pelvis, and vasospasm of the umbilical arteries from cold, drying and handling. Result: acute fetal hypoxia; death or brain injury within minutes if birth is delayed.
