Abruptio Placentae

15. Definition

Premature separation of a normally sited placenta after 28 wk, before birth.

16. Classification
Clinical picture
Concealed abruption
Pain+woody uterus+shock > loss=Abruption
17. Risk factors
  • Previous abruption
  • Pre-eclampsia, hypertension
  • Trauma · smoking, cocaine
  • Polyhydramnios with sudden ROM
  • Thrombophilia · multiparity
18. Pathophysiology
  • Decidual vessel bursts → clot
  • Clot strips placenta → fetal hypoxia
  • Blood into muscle → Couvelaire
  • Thromboplastin into blood → DIC (abruption is the commonest cause of DIC in pregnancy)
19. Clinical features
  • Constant pain; dark bleed or none
  • Shock > visible loss
  • Uterus hard, "woody", tender
  • Fetal distress or death
20. Investigations
  • Clinical: scan misses most clots
  • CTG · FBC, cross-match
  • Clotting, fibrinogen, clot test
  • Creatinine · Kleihauer
21. Complications

Maternal: shock · DIC · Couvelaire uterus → PPH · AKI · Sheehan. Fetal: hypoxia, death, prematurity.

Couvelaire uterus

Blood tracks into the myometrium: the uterus looks blue-purple and bruised at caesarean and contracts poorly → PPH. Not itself a reason for hysterectomy: uterotonics, massage, compression sutures first.
