4. Ultrasound in early pregnancy

Transvaginal ultrasound (TVS) shows the structures of early pregnancy about one week earlier than abdominal ultrasound. The structures appear in a fixed order, so their presence or absence at a given gestation is used to diagnose normal and abnormal pregnancy.

Finding on transvaginal scanWeeks from LMPSignificance
Gestational sac4.5–5Lies inside the uterus, to one side of the cavity, within the decidua. A sac alone is not proof: it may be the fluid collection (pseudosac) seen with an ectopic pregnancy.
Yolk sac5–5.5The first definite proof of an intrauterine pregnancy.
Fetal pole with heartbeat6Proves that the pregnancy is alive (viable). A crown–rump length (CRL) of 7 mm or more with no heartbeat means a missed miscarriage.
Abdominal scanabout 1 week laterNeeds a full bladder; each finding appears about one week later than on TVS.
Information from the early scan
  • Site: intrauterine or ectopic
  • Viability: presence of a heartbeat
  • Number of fetuses and their chorionicity (best assessed before 14 weeks)
  • Gestational age: dating by CRL
  • Major anomalies, and nuchal translucency at 11–14 weeks
The discriminatory zone

When the serum hCG is 2000 IU/L or more, an intrauterine sac should be visible on transvaginal ultrasound (about 6000 IU/L for an abdominal scan). If the uterus is empty above this level, the pregnancy must be treated as ectopic until proven otherwise (see 3.1).
