5Investigations
  • Diagnosis is clinical: replace first
  • At the same time: CBC, coagulation, cross-match 4–6 units
  • Ultrasound only if unclear (subacute, chronic)
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Ultrasound before replacing an acute inversion wastes the minutes that matter
6Differential diagnosis
  • Atonic PPH: soft fundus felt, shock matches the loss
  • Uterine rupture: shock with a fast pulse, scar, labour pain
  • Amniotic fluid embolism: cyanosis, fits, then bleeding; uterus in place
  • Mass in the vagina with a normal fundus: fibroid polyp, prolapse, vaginal haematoma
7Management: the drill
  • Call help
  • Replace at once, everything else in parallel: the ring tightens every minute
  • Leave the placenta on until the uterus is back
  • IV lines, fluids, blood
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Removing the placenta first makes the raw bed bleed torrentially
8Methods of replacement
  • Johnson manoeuvre: palm on the fundus, push up through the cervix toward the umbilicus; the part out last goes in first; hold 3–5 min
  • Relax the ring: terbutaline 0.25 mg SC, MgSO₄ 4 g IV over 15 min, or GA with a volatile agent
  • O'Sullivan (hydrostatic): 2–5 L warm saline into the sealed vagina; exclude rupture first
  • Huntington (laparotomy): clamps on the round ligaments, pull up step by step
  • Haultain (laparotomy): posterior incision through a tight ring
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Ring tight, uterus will not go up → Terbutaline or GA, then Johnson
  • Manual replacement fails, no rupture → O'Sullivan
  • Ring too tight at laparotomy → Haultain incision
