🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Soft isthmus, the fingers almost meet → Hegar's sign
  • One cornu of the uterus larger → Piskacek's sign
2Amenorrhoea that is not pregnancy
  • Stress, weight loss, heavy exercise, breastfeeding, PCOS, thyroid disease, high prolactin, DMPA, menopause
  • Bleeding does not exclude pregnancy: implantation bleed, threatened miscarriage, ectopic
  • Pseudocyesis: firm belief of pregnancy, hCG negative, empty uterus; often linked to infertility: explain gently, counselling
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Breastfeeding, 10 wk amenorrhoea, positive test → Pregnant: arrange a dating scan
3Pregnancy tests (hCG)
  • Made by the syncytiotrophoblast; keeps the corpus luteum
  • Tests measure the β-subunit (α is shared with LH, FSH, TSH)
  • Serum positive 8–11 days after conception; peak about 100 000 IU/L at 8–10 wk
  • Viable pregnancy: rises ≥ 35% in 48 h (about doubles)
  • Urine positive at about the missed period; early-morning urine best; stays positive 2–4 wk after a loss
  • High: molar, multiple, wrong dates, Down syndrome
  • Low or not rising: ectopic or failing pregnancy
  • False negative: too early, dilute urine, hook effect
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • HCG rises only 20% in 48 h with pain → Ectopic pregnancy
  • Very high hCG, severe vomiting, uterus large for dates → Molar pregnancy
4Ultrasound in early pregnancy
  • TVS sees each structure about 1 wk before an abdominal scan
  • Gestational sac 4.5–5 wk: not proof alone (pseudosac of ectopic)
  • Yolk sac 5–5.5 wk: first definite proof of an intrauterine pregnancy
  • Fetal pole with heartbeat 6 wk: viable
