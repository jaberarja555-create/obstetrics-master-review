3. Clinical features & investigations

Weight loss despite good appetite · tremor · persistent tachycardia > 100 (resting) · eye signs · goitre. Palpitations and heat intolerance also occur in normal pregnancy. TFTs: low TSH + high free T4. TRAb in Graves'. Ultrasound: size, nodules.

4. Complications
Maternal
  • Subfertility · miscarriage
  • Pre-eclampsia
  • Heart failure, AF · thyroid storm
Fetal
  • Preterm birth · IUGR · stillbirth
  • Fetal / neonatal thyrotoxicosis
  • Fetal goitre, hypothyroidism (drugs)
Exam trap

Past Graves' treated by surgery or radioiodine, now euthyroid: still check TRAb. The antibodies persist, cross the placenta and can make the fetus thyrotoxic (tachycardia > 160, goitre, IUGR).

Clinical scenario
Thyroidectomy for Graves' 3 years ago, on thyroxine. Fetal heart 175. Cause?
Fetal thyrotoxicosis from persisting TRAb.
5. Management of hyperthyroidism
  • PTU 150–400 mg/day in the 1st trimester (less placental and milk transfer), then carbimazole 15–40 mg/day. Carbimazole embryopathy: aplasia cutis, choanal / oesophageal atresia. PTU: liver failure.
  • Propranolol 10–40 mg 8-hourly for symptoms, a few weeks only (long use: IUGR).
  • Aim: lowest dose keeping free T4 at the upper limit of normal (both drugs cross: fetal goitre). TFT every 4–6 wk. Surgery: dysphagia, stridor, suspected cancer, allergy to both drugs.
  • Fetus: TRAb, fetal heart rate, growth scans. Labour: watch for storm; not a reason for caesarean. Baby: TFTs.
  • Breastfeeding safe: PTU < 150 mg/day, carbimazole < 15 mg/day; above this, check the baby's TFTs.
Thyroid storm · emergency

Poorly controlled disease + trigger (labour, caesarean, infection). Fever, confusion, tachycardia / AF, heart failure. HDU: fluids, cooling, propranolol, PTU, iodine 1 h after PTU, IV hydrocortisone; treat the trigger, monitor the fetus.
