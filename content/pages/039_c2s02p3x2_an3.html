11. Parenteral iron
  • Indications: intolerant of oral iron, non-adherent, malabsorption, or Hb 7–9.9 after ~34 wk. Not in the 1st trimester.
  • Iron sucrose 200 mg IV over 30 min, 2–3×/wk: safest. Iron dextran: total dose over 4–6 h after a test dose; adrenaline ready (anaphylaxis).
12. Blood transfusion
SituationAction
Hb < 7, before 36 wk, no failureAdmit, find the cause, oral or IV iron, treat malaria and worms
Hb < 7 after 36 wkTransfuse: no time for iron
Heart failure or Hb < 5Transfuse at any gestation
Bleeding, sepsis, labour or CS dueTransfuse
  1. Sit her up, O₂, IV line. Cross-match 2 units of packed cells (less volume).
  2. Slowly: 1 unit over 3–4 h, furosemide 20–40 mg IV with the unit if overload.
  3. Watch RR, pulse, BP, temperature, chest, JVP every 15 min at first. Breathless, crackles or fever: stop.
  4. Treat the cause, then iron. Target is safety (Hb about 8), not full correction.

13. Labour and puerperium
  • Hospital birth, 2 units cross-matched, IV line, semi-sitting, careful fluids. Shorten the 2nd stage (ventouse, forceps) if tired.
  • Active 3rd stage: oxytocin 10 IU IM; replace loss early. Asepsis, antibiotics, iron for 3 months, family planning.
