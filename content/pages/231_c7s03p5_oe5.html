4 · Pelvic grip (2nd pelvic) القبضة الحوضية
4Face her feet. Both hands flat on the lower abdomen, fingers slide down and in towards the pelvis. Hands converge = head above the brim (not engaged) · diverge = engaged. Count the fifths felt (next page). Cephalic prominence (sinciput) on the side opposite the back = flexed; on the same side as the back = extended (face)ثني ركبك أكتر شوية، وحأضغط تحت البطن فوق العظم.
9. Abnormal lie or presentation: consider why

Uterus: lax (grand multipara), fibroid, septate · Liquor: polyhydramnios · Placenta: praevia · Baby: preterm, twins, anomaly · Pelvis: contracted. Any of these after 36 wk → scan.

Distinguishing features · What your hands find
Hard round mass in the fundusBreech presentation
Fundus and pelvis empty, head in a flankTransverse lie
Limbs everywhere, back not foundOP position
Head ballotable at 39 wk, primiNot engaged: CPD? praevia?
Two heads, three polesTwins
Viva questions
Which grip tells you if the head is flexed?
Pelvic grip: sinciput felt higher, on the side opposite the back.
Pawlik grip is very painful at term. Why gentle?
Hurts and tightens the wall; skip if head is deep, use pelvic grip.
10. Terms to use correctly
LieLong axis of the fetus vs long axis of the uterus: longitudinal, transverse, oblique
PresentationPart over the pelvic brim: cephalic, breech, shoulder
PositionDenominator (occiput) vs her pelvis; by abdomen: side of the back, e.g. LOA
EngagementWidest diameter past the brim: ≤ 2/5 palpable
Viva questions
Why can the head be high at term in a primi?
CPD, placenta praevia, wrong dates, OP, polyhydramnios.
Lie vs presentation in a transverse lie?
Lie transverse; presentation shoulder.
