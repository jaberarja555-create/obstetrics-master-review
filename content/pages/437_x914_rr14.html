🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Pale, fever, splenomegaly → Malaria
  • Pale, on phenytoin, large cells → Folate deficiency
3Clinical features
  • Often none
  • Tired, dizzy, breathless; pica
  • Pallor, tachycardia, flow murmur
  • Iron lack: smooth tongue, koilonychia
  • Jaundice and spleen in haemolysis; S3 in failure
4Investigations
  • First line: FBC + MCV, film, ferritin, malaria film or RDT, stool for ova, urine
  • No response: Hb electrophoresis, B12, folate, G6PD, renal, liver, thyroid, HIV, TB
  • Iron deficiency: low MCV, pencil cells, ferritin below 15, TIBC raised
  • Megaloblastic: high MCV, macro-ovalocytes, hypersegmented neutrophils
  • β-thal trait: MCV very low for the Hb, target cells, normal ferritin, HbA2 over 3.5%
  • Sickle: sickle cells, HbS
  • Mentzer index (MCV ÷ RBC): under 13 thal, over 13 iron
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • MCV 62, ferritin 8 → Iron deficiency
  • No rise after 4 wk of iron, MCV 64, ferritin normal → Hb electrophoresis (thal trait)
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Small cells with normal ferritin = thal trait: do not give iron for months; ferritin also rises with infection
5Types and their treatment
  • Megaloblastic: folic acid 5 mg + iron; low B12: hydroxocobalamin 1 mg IM first
  • Sickle cell (HbSS): crises, infection, VTE, FGR; folic acid 5 mg, no iron unless ferritin low, malaria prophylaxis, growth scans; crisis: fluids, O₂, analgesia, antibiotics, LMWH
  • Sickle trait: more UTIs
  • β-thal trait: folic acid 5 mg, iron only if ferritin low; two carriers: 25% major
  • G6PD: avoid sulphonamides, nitrofurantoin, primaquine
