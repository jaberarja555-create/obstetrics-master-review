6Complications
  • Mother: heart failure, PPH poorly tolerated, APH, sepsis, VTE
  • Baby: preterm birth, FGR, stillbirth, low iron stores
  • NTD from folate lack (not iron)
7Prevention
  • Folic acid 0.4 mg before conception to 12 wk; 5 mg if high risk (previous NTD, anti-epileptics, diabetes, obesity, sickle cell, thal, twins)
  • Iron 30–60 mg + folate daily from booking to 3 months after birth
  • Hb at booking, 28 and 36 wk
  • Treat malaria and hookworm, bed nets, space births
8Oral and parenteral iron
  • Ferrous sulphate 200 mg (65 mg iron) 2–3 times daily: first choice, black stool
  • Take 1 h before food with vitamin C; not with tea, milk or antacids
  • Response: reticulocytes rise at 7–10 days, Hb rises about 1 g/dL a week; recheck at 3 wk; continue 3 months after Hb is normal
  • Parenteral iron: intolerance, non-adherence, malabsorption, or Hb 7–9.9 after about 34 wk; not in the first trimester
  • Iron sucrose 200 mg IV 2–3 times a week: safest
  • Iron dextran: test dose, adrenaline ready
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Hb 8.5 at 35 wk, vomits oral iron → IV iron sucrose
  • No rise on iron, takes it with tea → Correct how she takes it
9Blood transfusion
  • Hb below 7 before 36 wk, no failure: admit, find the cause, oral or IV iron
  • Hb below 7 after 36 wk: transfuse (no time for iron)
  • Heart failure or Hb below 5: transfuse at any gestation
  • Also with bleeding, sepsis, labour or CS due
  • Packed cells, 1 unit over 3–4 h, furosemide 20–40 mg IV if overload risk
  • Observe every 15 min at first; stop for breathlessness, crackles or fever
  • Aim for safety (Hb about 8), not full correction
