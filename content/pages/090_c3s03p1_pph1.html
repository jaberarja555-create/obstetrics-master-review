3.3Postpartum Haemorrhage

1. Definition

Primary PPH = blood loss from the genital tract ≥ 500 mL after vaginal birth or ≥ 1000 mL at caesarean, within 24 h of birth, or any loss that makes her unstable. Secondary PPH = abnormal or heavy bleeding from 24 h to 12 wk after birth.

3. Aetiology: the 4 Ts
CAUSES OF PRIMARY PPH · Tone is commonest. Uterus hard but still bleeding → consider trauma.
Distinguishing features · Which T?
Uterus soft, boggy, above umbilicusTone: atony
Uterus hard, bright red trickle after forcepsTrauma: tear
Placenta delivered incompleteTissue: retained
Oozing from drip sites, no clotThrombin: DIC
4. Risk factors
ToneOverdistension: twins, polyhydramnios, macrosomia · grand multipara · fibroids · long or fast labour · oxytocin · chorioamnionitis · MgSO₄, nifedipine, halothane · anaemia
TraumaInstrumental delivery · precipitate labour · macrosomia · malposition · VBAC
TissuePlacenta accreta · succenturiate lobe · previous CS or myomectomy · pulling the cord before separation
ThrombinDIC: abruption, pre-eclampsia, HELLP, IUFD, sepsis, amniotic fluid embolism · von Willebrand · anticoagulants
