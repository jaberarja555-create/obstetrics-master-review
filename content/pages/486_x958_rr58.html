3.7Abnormal amniotic fluid
After 20 wk amniotic fluid is mostly fetal urine, removed by fetal swallowing; it peaks at about 900 mL at 34–36 wk. AFI adds the deepest pool of four quadrants (normal about 9–25 cm); DVP is the single deepest pool.
1Polyhydramnios: definition and causes
  • AFI over 25 cm or DVP over 8 cm
  • Chronic (common, gradual) or acute (rare, under 20 wk, usually monozygotic twins)
  • Causes: idiopathic (commonest), diabetes (fetal polyuria), GI atresia (oesophageal, duodenal), anencephaly, myotonic dystrophy, twins and TTTS, hydrops
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Polyhydramnios; newborn drools and chokes, NG tube coils → Oesophageal atresia with TOF
  • Polyhydramnios with a double bubble → Duodenal atresia (Down syndrome)
2Polyhydramnios: features and investigations
  • Uterus large for dates, tense; fetal parts and heart hard to find; fluid thrill
  • Discomfort, breathlessness
  • Unstable lie
  • Detailed scan, OGTT, TORCH, syphilis, blood group and antibodies, karyotype if anomaly
3Polyhydramnios: complications and management
  • Preterm labour, malpresentation
  • At ROM: cord prolapse and abruption
  • PPH (atony)
  • Treat the cause (glucose control, laser for TTTS)
  • Severe and symptomatic (AFI over 40): slow amnioreduction
  • Indomethacin 25 mg 6-hourly under 32 wk for no more than 72 h (ductus closure)
  • Labour: check the lie, controlled ARM, oxytocin ready for PPH
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Polyhydramnios: membranes rupture, sudden pain, dark bleed → Abruption
