29Complications

Maternal: massive PPH, DIC · hysterectomy · bladder, ureter injury · death. Fetal: preterm birth.

30Management
  • Specialist centre, team: obstetrician, anaesthetist, urologist, blood bank
  • Consent for hysterectomy · cross-match 6–8 units · cell salvage if available
  • Planned caesarean at 36 wk; uterine incision away from the placenta
  • Do not pull the placenta: hysterectomy with placenta in place
31Prevention

Avoid caesareans that are not needed: every scar raises the next risk.

Vasa Praevia

32Definition

Fetal vessels run in the membranes, unprotected by cord or placenta, over or near the internal os. They tear at ROM: the blood lost is fetal, not maternal. If it is not found before labour, about 60% of the babies die.

33Classification & risk factors

Type I = velamentous cord insertion · type II = vessels joining the lobes of a bilobed placenta or a succenturiate lobe. Risk factors: these, low-lying placenta or praevia, IVF, twins.

34Pathophysiology

A term baby has only about 250–300 mL of blood (80 mL/kg). A tear of one vessel drains it in minutes, so about 100 mL can kill. The mother stays well because she loses nothing. The presenting part can also press the vessels and cause fetal distress before any bleed.
