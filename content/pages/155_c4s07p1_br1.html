4.7Breech Presentation

1. Definitions
LieFetal long axis to the mother's: longitudinal, transverse, oblique
PresentationPart lowest in the birth canal: vertex, breech, face, brow, shoulder
AttitudeFetal parts to each other: flexion (normal) or extension
DenominatorLandmark of the presenting part: occiput, sacrum, mentum, acromion
PositionDenominator to the pelvis (sacrum, pubis, SI joints, ilio-pectineal eminences): LOA, ROA, LOP, LSA
MalpresentationLongitudinal lie, presenting part not the vertex: breech, face, brow, shoulder, compound, cord
MalpositionVertex presents, occiput not anterior: OT (LOT, ROT) or OP
Clinical picture
Malpresentation
Longitudinal lie+not vertex=Malpresentation
Malposition
Vertex+occiput not anterior=Malposition
2. Definition

Breech = longitudinal lie with the buttocks or feet presenting and the head in the fundus. Denominator = sacrum; commonest position LSA. The most common malpresentation.

Incidence: 25% before 28 wk → 3–4% at term. Most turn by themselves before 36 wk, so breech before 36 wk matters only in labour.
