Obstetric Emergencies
5.1Cord prolapse
The umbilical cord lies below the presenting part. A true emergency: cord compression and vasospasm can kill the fetus within minutes. About 1 in 300–500 deliveries.
1Definition
  • Cord presentation: membranes intact, cord felt through them
  • Cord prolapse (overt): membranes ruptured, cord in the vagina or at the vulva
  • Occult prolapse: cord beside the presenting part, not felt; suspect from FHR dips
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Intact membranes, cord felt below the head on VE → Cord presentation
2Risk factors
  • Anything that leaves the presenting part not fitting the pelvis
  • Malpresentation: footling breech (about 15%), complete 5%, frank 0.5%; transverse, oblique, unstable lie
  • Preterm, low birth weight, PPROM
  • Second twin
  • Polyhydramnios: gush at ROM
  • Multiparity, high head (CPD, praevia)
  • ARM with a high head, ECV, internal version, balloon induction
3Pathophysiology
  • Compression between the presenting part and the pelvis
  • Vasospasm of the umbilical arteries from cold, drying and handling
  • Result: acute fetal hypoxia; death or brain injury within minutes
