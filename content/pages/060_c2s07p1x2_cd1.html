3. Classification

NYHA functional class: assess at booking and every visit; often worsens by one class as pregnancy advances.

Class INo symptoms
Class IISymptoms on moderate effort
Class IIISymptoms on mild effort
Class IVSymptoms at rest
High risk · pregnancy contraindicated

Pulmonary hypertension and Eisenmenger syndrome (maternal death 40–60%) · severe MS or AS · LV ejection fraction < 30% or NYHA III–IV · previous peripartum cardiomyopathy with poor recovery · Marfan with aortic root > 4 cm. Advise against pregnancy; offer termination if pregnant. Stenotic lesions are worse than regurgitant: fixed output cannot rise.

4. Clinical features

History: known lesion, rheumatic fever, previous admissions, last echo, drugs (warfarin, penicillin). Breathlessness out of proportion, orthopnoea, palpitations, haemoptysis. Examination: pulse rhythm, JVP, murmurs, basal crackles, oedema, cyanosis.

5. Investigations
  • Echo: the key test. Valve area, ejection fraction, pulmonary pressure, aortic root
  • ECG: AF, ischaemia, chamber strain
  • FBC each visit and at 28 wk: anaemia strains the heart
  • Urine (UTI), INR if on warfarin · chest X-ray (shielded) if oedema
6. Complications
MaternalHeart failure and pulmonary oedema (24–32 wk, labour, first hours after birth) · AF → stroke · thromboembolism, valve thrombosis · infective endocarditis · pre-eclampsia, APH, PPH · death
FetalMiscarriage · growth restriction, low birth weight · preterm birth · stillbirth · congenital heart disease if mother has CHD (3–5%) · drug effects (warfarin)

7. Management: pre-pregnancy

Multidisciplinary team (MDT) care: cardiologist, obstetrician and anaesthetist. Echo + NYHA class. Explain risks; advise against pregnancy if high risk. Repair the valve before pregnancy if needed. Stop ACE inhibitors and statins; plan anticoagulation. Folic acid.
