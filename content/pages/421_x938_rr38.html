2.12HIV in pregnancy
Mother-to-child transmission happens in utero, in labour or through breast milk. Early lifelong ART with a suppressed viral load, infant prophylaxis and safe feeding reduce it to under 1%; untreated it is about 25%.
1Risk factors for transmission
  • High viral load: the strongest factor
  • Low CD4, advanced disease, no ART, new infection in pregnancy
  • Prolonged ROM, chorioamnionitis, preterm birth, early ARM, scalp electrode
  • Mixed feeding, cracked nipples
  • Complications: miscarriage, PROM, preterm labour, opportunistic infection, TB, FGR
2Screening and investigations
  • Opt-out testing at booking with syphilis and hepatitis B
  • Positive rapid test: confirm with a second, different test; retest late if at risk
  • Baseline CD4, viral load, Hb, creatinine, LFT, HBV, HCV, TB screen; test the partner
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Unbooked, in labour, status unknown → Rapid HIV test now
3Antenatal management
  • ART for all: start as soon as diagnosed (same day if ready), any CD4, any gestation, for life
  • TLD (tenofovir + lamivudine + dolutegravir) one tablet daily
  • Viral load 3 months after starting and at 34–36 wk (birth plan)
  • Cotrimoxazole 960 mg daily if CD4 ≤ 350 or WHO stage 3–4
  • Avoid amniocentesis and CVS
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Diagnosed at 16 wk, CD4 500 → Start ART now, lifelong
⚠️ Exam trap
  • On cotrimoxazole: no SP for IPTp (two sulfa drugs; cotrimoxazole already prevents malaria)
