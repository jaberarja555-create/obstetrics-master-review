8Placenta accreta spectrum
  • Deficient decidua; villi attach too deep
  • Accreta on the myometrium (about 75%), increta into it, percreta through the serosa into bladder or bowel
  • Risk with praevia + previous CS: none 3%, one 11%, two 40%, three or more 60%
  • Usually silent; percreta may cause haematuria
  • Colour Doppler in every praevia with a scar; MRI if unclear
  • Scan signs: lost clear zone, lacunae, thin myometrium, bridging vessels
  • Plan at a specialist centre, consent for hysterectomy, 6–8 units, CS at about 36 wk, incision away from the placenta
  • Do not pull the placenta: hysterectomy with it in place
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Anterior praevia with 2 previous CS → Colour Doppler ± MRI for accreta
9Vasa praevia
  • Fetal vessels in the membranes over the os; they tear at ROM: the blood lost is fetal
  • Types: velamentous insertion; vessels between lobes (bilobed, succenturiate)
  • Undiagnosed: about 60% of babies die; a term baby has only 250–300 mL of blood
  • Triad: ROM + painless small bleed + fetal distress (bradycardia or sinusoidal CTG); mother well
  • Known before labour: admit 30–32 wk, CS at 34–36 wk before ROM
  • Bleeding in labour: immediate CS; fully dilated with the head low: instrumental
  • Apt test or Kleihauer, but never wait
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Bleed at ROM, FHR 80, fully dilated, head at +2 → Instrumental delivery now
10Uterine rupture
  • Complete (all layers) or incomplete (dehiscence, serosa intact)
  • Risks: scar (about 0.5% after one lower-segment CS, 4–9% after a classical scar), obstructed labour in a multipara, oxytocin or misoprostol overdose, grand multiparity
  • Impending: Bandl's ring rising, tender lower segment, tachycardia, haematuria
  • Rupture: pain, contractions stop, presenting part rises, fetal parts under the skin, fetal bradycardia, shock
