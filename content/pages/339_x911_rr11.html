3.6Intrauterine fetal death
No fetal movements, no heart on real-time ultrasound at 34 weeks → IUFD
IUFD retained 4 weeks, bleeding gums → DIC: check clotting, deliver
IUFD with ruptured membranes and fever → Deliver now with antibiotics
Definition
Fetal death ≥ 28 wk or ≥ 1000 g (WHO) · commonest cause unexplained (≈ 50%)
Diagnosis
Real-time ultrasound · X-ray signs: Spalding (overlapping skull bones, 7 d), Robert (gas in vessels, 12 h), Deuel (halo)
Causes
PE, abruption, diabetes, infection (malaria, syphilis), cholestasis, FGR, cord accident, anomaly
Management
Most labour within 3 wk (85%) · expectant: clotting 2–3 times a week · mifepristone 200 mg, then misoprostol after 36–48 h (25 µg 6-hourly > 28 wk)
Investigations
CBC, clotting, Kleihauer, glucose, TORCH, syphilis, bile acids, placenta, post-mortem
After
Cabergoline 1 mg to suppress lactation · Rh-negative: Kleihauer + anti-D · follow-up at 6 wk
Exam traps
Absent heart on Pinard is not diagnostic · DIC in ≈ 30% if retained > 3 wk · CS only for obstetric reasons
3.7Abnormal amniotic fluid
Uterus large for dates, tense, fluid thrill, AFI 30 → Polyhydramnios: look for diabetes, anomaly
Polyhydramnios with a double bubble → Duodenal atresia
AFI 3 cm at 41 weeks → Oligohydramnios: induce
Physiology
After 20 wk liquor is fetal urine · peak 34–36 wk · normal AFI 9–25 cm
Polyhydramnios
AFI > 25 or DVP > 8 · causes: idiopathic, diabetes, GI atresia, anencephaly, twins (TTTS) · risks: malpresentation, cord prolapse, abruption at ROM, PPH
Oligohydramnios
AFI < 5 or DVP < 2 · causes: ROM (commonest), FGR, renal agenesis, post-term, ACE inhibitors
Investigations
Detailed scan · OGTT · speculum for ROM · Doppler
Treatment
Poly: treat cause · indomethacin < 32 wk, 72 h · amnioreduction · oligo: deliver at term
Fetal effects of oligo
Pulmonary hypoplasia (before 22 wk) · Potter sequence (renal agenesis) · limb contractures
Exam traps
ECV is contraindicated with oligohydramnios · ACE inhibitors cause oligohydramnios and hypocalvaria · controlled ARM in polyhydramnios
