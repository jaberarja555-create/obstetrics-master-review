Phase 1 · keep her alive
  • O₂ 15 L/min, intubate early; SpO₂ ≈ 100% protects the brain
  • Left tilt or push the uterus left, off the IVC
  • Fluids with care: right heart failing
  • Low BP after fluids: vasopressor (phenylephrine, noradrenaline) ± inotrope
  • Fits: protect the airway
Phase 2 · stop the bleeding
  • Blood + FFP 15 mL/kg
  • Cryo if fibrinogen < 2 g/L · platelets if < 50
  • TXA 1 g IV
  • Atony: oxytocin 40 IU/500 mL over 4 h, carboprost 250 µg IM
  • Still bleeding: hysterectomy

9. Management: perimortem caesarean
WhenCardiac arrest at ≥ 20 wk (fundus at or above the umbilicus), no output after 4 min of good CPR
TimingStart the knife at 4 min · baby out by 5 min of arrest
WhereWhere she lies: no transfer to theatre, no consent, CPR continues during the operation
WhyUterus off the IVC and aorta → venous return ↑ → CPR works: mainly for the mother

Not arrested, baby not yet born: CTG; deliver quickly once she is stable. ICU after: ventilation, monitoring, obstetrics + anaesthesia + haematology.

Exam trap

"Move her to theatre for the caesarean": wrong, transfer wastes minutes and stops CPR. "Give 3 L of fluid fast": wrong, the failing right heart floods the lungs. "Wait for clotting results": wrong, use the bedside clot test and give products.

Hospital protocol

No ICU on site: CPR, O₂, two cannulae, left tilt, call the theatre team; perimortem CS in the labour room. Ask relatives to donate early: fresh whole blood gives clotting factors and platelets. Transfer only once stable.

10. Prognosis

Maternal death 13–80%; up to 85% of survivors have hypoxic brain damage; perinatal death high. Debrief, document, report for maternal death review. Recurrence rare; next delivery in a hospital with ICU.
