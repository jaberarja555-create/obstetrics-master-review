5.4Shoulder Dystocia

1. Definition

The head is delivered but the shoulders do not follow with gentle downward traction, so extra manoeuvres are needed; or a head-to-body interval > 60 s. About 1 in 200 vaginal births. Mostly unpredictable: about half happen in babies under 4 kg.

2. Pathophysiology
  • Normally the shoulders enter the pelvis in the oblique diameter, then rotate
  • In SD they stay antero-posterior: the anterior shoulder sticks behind the symphysis pubis
  • Rarely the posterior shoulder sticks on the sacral promontory (bilateral)
  • A bony problem: episiotomy alone does not free it
  • Large trunk, normal head: in diabetes fat is laid on shoulders and chest
  • Cord and chest are squeezed → no breathing, poor blood flow
3. Why it is an emergency

60 sNo shoulders: SD

5 minHypoxic injury risk

Five minutes is about the most a baby tolerates; aim to deliver well before. Hard pulling to "beat the clock" causes the injuries.
