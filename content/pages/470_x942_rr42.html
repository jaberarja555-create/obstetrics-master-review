⚠️ Exam trap
  • Amenorrhoea after repeated D&C → Asherman syndrome (intrauterine adhesions)
7Septic miscarriage
  • Usually after an unsafe abortion or unclean instruments
  • Fever, rigors, offensive loss, tender uterus
  • Clostridium perfringens: haemolysis with jaundice and dark urine = severe sepsis
  • Complications: septic shock, AKI, DIC, ARDS, pelvic abscess, tubal block
  • Treat: resuscitate, cultures, ceftriaxone + metronidazole IV, anti-tetanus
  • Evacuate gently after antibiotics start (the soft uterus perforates easily)
8Recurrent miscarriage
  • Definition: ≥ 3 miscarriages, not necessarily consecutive (RCOG 2023)
  • First trimester: antiphospholipid syndrome, parental translocation, PCOS, poorly controlled diabetes or thyroid disease
  • TORCH does not cause recurrent loss: do not test
  • Second trimester: cervical weakness (painless, each loss earlier), uterine anomaly (painful, each loss later), syphilis
  • Investigate: lupus anticoagulant and anticardiolipin twice, 12 wk apart, parental karyotype, pelvic scan, HbA1c, TSH
  • APS: aspirin 75 mg + enoxaparin 40 mg daily from a positive test
  • Cervical weakness: cerclage at 12–14 wk, removed at 36–37 wk or when labour starts
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • 3 losses at 8–10 wk, lupus anticoagulant positive → Aspirin + LMWH in the next pregnancy
  • Painless second-trimester losses, each earlier than the last → Cervical weakness: cerclage
9Anti-D in RhD-negative women
  • Miscarriage or ectopic: no anti-D before 12 wk (NICE 2025); from 12 wk 250 IU IM within 72 h
  • From 20 wk: 500 IU + Kleihauer test; 1500 IU if Kleihauer is not available
  • Local practice: often given after any loss from 10 wk, a partial mole or a surgical evacuation
