2.9Obstetric cholestasis
Obstetric cholestasis is itching without a rash with raised bile acids and/or abnormal liver tests and no other cause, usually in the third trimester, that resolves after birth. The mother itches; the fetus is at risk of sudden death.
1Risk factors and features
  • Family history in 1 of 3, previous cholestasis, South Asian origin, twins
  • Pruritus of the palms and soles, then trunk; worse at night
  • Only scratch marks on examination; mild jaundice in a few; dark urine, pale stool, steatorrhoea
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Itchy palms and soles, no rash, bile acids raised → Obstetric cholestasis
2Investigations
  • Bile acids ≥ 19 µmol/L on a random sample (RCOG 2022): the key test; ≥ 100 = high fetal risk
  • ALT, AST, GGT 2–3 times normal; ALP not useful (placental)
  • PT for vitamin K deficiency
  • Exclude others: liver ultrasound, viral serology, autoantibodies
  • Itching with normal tests: repeat in 1–2 wk (itch can precede abnormal tests)
🩺 Exam scenario
  • 32 wk, itchy at night, normal LFT and bile acids → Repeat LFT and bile acids in 1–2 wk
3Differential diagnosis
  • Itch with a rash: eczema, scabies, polymorphic eruption, pemphigoid gestationis (blisters around the umbilicus)
  • Abnormal LFT: HELLP (BP, platelets), AFLP (vomiting, low glucose), viral hepatitis, gallstones, PBC (AMA), autoimmune hepatitis (ASMA)
