Normal Pregnancy and Antenatal Care
1.1Physiology of pregnancy
Almost every organ system changes in pregnancy, mainly under progesterone (relaxes smooth muscle) and oestrogen (growth, plasma proteins, clotting factors). The changes supply the fetus and prepare the mother for blood loss at delivery, and they return to normal within about 6 weeks after birth. Knowing them stops normal findings being mistaken for disease.
1Genital tract
  • Uterus 50–70 g → about 1000 g at term; hypertrophy early, stretching later
  • Braxton Hicks contractions from mid-pregnancy: painless, do not open the cervix
  • Isthmus forms the lower uterine segment in the third trimester
  • Cervix soft (Goodell's sign), mucus plug
  • Vagina bluish (Chadwick's sign, about 8 wk); leucorrhoea; candida more common
2Cardiovascular changes
  • Cardiac output +40%: stroke volume first, then heart rate +10–15
  • Most of the rise by 20–24 wk; highest just after delivery
  • Heart failure risk peaks at 24–28 wk, in labour and just after delivery
  • Uterine blood flow 500–750 mL/min at term: why PPH kills in minutes
  • Peripheral resistance falls: BP lowest in the second trimester, back to booking level by term
  • IVC compression: ankle oedema, varicose veins, haemorrhoids
  • Supine hypotension after 20 wk: prevent with a 15° left lateral tilt
  • Apex in the 4th space, ejection systolic murmur and third sound are normal
  • A diastolic murmur is never normal
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Feels faint lying flat at 32 wk → Supine hypotension: left lateral tilt
  • Mid-diastolic murmur with breathlessness → Mitral stenosis
