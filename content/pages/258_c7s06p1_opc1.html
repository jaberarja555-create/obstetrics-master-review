7.6Partogram & CTG Stations

1. The station

Data station, 5 min, no patient. Two forms: (1) "Plot": the examiner gives a labour story with findings at set times and a blank WHO partogram; you plot it. (2) "Read": a filled partogram; you describe it, give the diagnosis and the plan. Bring a pen and a ruler; write clearly.

2. The symbols
3. Plotting: step by step
  1. Patient details: name, age, G/P, GA, number, date, admission time, time of ROM.
  2. Open it in the active phase, at 4 cm. Put the first X on the alert line at the dilatation found; write the clock time under that hour. Why: the alert line starts at 4 cm and the woman starts on it.
  3. Alert line: from 4 cm, rising 1 cm/h to 10 cm at 6 h. Action line: parallel, 4 h to the right.
  4. Cervix X every 4 h by VE (every 2 h on oxytocin) · descent O in fifths palpable by abdominal exam, before each VE · join the Xs and the Os.
  5. Fetal heart dot every 30 min · liquor letter at each VE: I intact, C clear, M meconium, B blood, A absent · moulding 0, +1, +2, +3.
  6. Contractions every 30 min: shade one box per contraction in 10 min; dots < 20 s, lines 20–40 s, solid > 40 s.
  7. Oxytocin (U/L and drops/min), drugs and IV fluids in their rows at the time given.
  8. Mother: pulse every 30 min · BP every 4 h · temp every 2 h · urine protein, acetone, volume at each void.
Viva questions
Admitted at 6 cm. Where do you put the first X?
On the alert line, at the 6 cm point.
How do you record descent on the partogram?
O = fifths of head palpable above the brim.
