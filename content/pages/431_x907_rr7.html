7Uterine size and the dates
  • From about 20 wk the SFH in cm ≈ weeks
  • First step in any mismatch: check the dates
  • Larger than dates: multiple pregnancy, polyhydramnios, macrosomia, molar pregnancy, fibroids, ovarian mass, full bladder
  • Smaller than dates: FGR, oligohydramnios, IUFD, missed miscarriage, transverse lie, deeply engaged head
  • Late booking with unknown LMP: combine quickening (add 20–22 wk in a first pregnancy, 22–24 wk later), earliest fundal height and first scan; record the dates as uncertain
1.3Antenatal care
Antenatal care is planned care from conception to labour. It prevents disease, detects complications early, classifies each woman as low or high risk, and teaches her and her family. Most deaths from pre-eclampsia, anaemia, malaria and haemorrhage can be prevented or detected early at these visits.
1Booking visit
  • Ideally before 12 wk: accurate dating, early folic acid, tests done in time
  • Full obstetric, medical, drug, family and social history for the risk assessment
  • Height, weight, BMI, BP, pallor, heart, chest, thyroid, abdomen, legs
  • Start iron and folic acid, Td, malaria prevention; aspirin and calcium when indicated
  • Hand-held antenatal card brought to every visit
2Schedule of visits
  • WHO: at least 8 contacts: 1 in the first trimester, 2 in the second, 5 in the third
  • Each visit: ask (movements, bleeding, headache, visual change, swelling, fever, leaking, contractions, iron), check (BP, urine protein, pallor, oedema, SFH from 24 wk, fetal heart, presentation from 36 wk), act
3Booking investigations
  • CBC: anaemia below 11 g/dL; repeat at 28 wk
  • Blood group and Rh, antibody screen at booking and 28 wk
  • Urine culture: treat asymptomatic bacteriuria (prevents pyelonephritis and preterm labour)
  • Blood glucose; OGTT at 24–28 wk with risk factors (16 wk after previous GDM)
