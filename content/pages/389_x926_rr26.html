4Differential diagnosis
  • Fever: pyelonephritis, typhoid, chorioamnionitis, brucellosis
  • Fits or coma: eclampsia, meningitis, hypoglycaemia, epilepsy
  • Jaundice: hepatitis (E is severe in pregnancy), HELLP, AFLP
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Fit, fever, BP 110/70, film positive → Cerebral malaria
  • Fit at 34 wk, fever 39 °C and BP 160/110 → Treat both: MgSO₄ + IV artesunate
5Complications
  • Maternal: anaemia (commonest), hypoglycaemia, cerebral malaria, renal failure, pulmonary oedema, shock, preterm labour, PPH
  • Fetal: miscarriage, preterm birth, stillbirth, FGR and low birth weight, congenital malaria
6Drug treatment
  • Decide by severity, not trimester
  • Uncomplicated: artemether–lumefantrine 4 tablets twice daily for 3 days in every trimester, with fatty food or milk; repeat a dose vomited within 30 min
  • Second line in the second and third trimester: DHA–piperaquine; no ACT: oral quinine 7 days
  • Severe: IV artesunate 2.4 mg/kg at 0, 12 and 24 h, then daily (IM if no vein)
  • Second line IV quinine 10 mg/kg 8-hourly in dextrose, with glucose checks
  • After at least 24 h IV and able to swallow: full 3-day oral ACT
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Fever again on day 3 of AL → Repeat the film; treatment failure → change drug
  • Confused and sweating on IV quinine → Check blood glucose (quinine releases insulin)
