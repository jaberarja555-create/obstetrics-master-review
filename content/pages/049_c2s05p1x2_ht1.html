3. Risk factors
Major risk · aspirin if 1
  • Previous PE or HTN in pregnancy
  • Chronic HTN · kidney disease
  • Diabetes · SLE, antiphospholipid
Moderate risk · aspirin if 2
  • First pregnancy · age ≥ 40
  • BMI > 30 · family history
  • Twins · interval > 10 years
4. Pathophysiology
PATHOPHYSIOLOGY · Starts in the placenta, shows in the mother's vessels: every sign is endothelial damage.
5. Clinical features
  • History: often none. Ask about headache, flashes, epigastric pain, vomiting, fetal movements
  • Examination: BP, sudden oedema of face and hands, hyperreflexia / clonus, RUQ tenderness, small fundus
OrganSevere features · any one = severe PE
BP≥ 160/110 (vasospasm: risk of brain bleed)
BrainSevere headache, flashes, blurred vision, clonus
LiverEpigastric / RUQ pain, vomiting, ↑ AST/ALT
Blood · kidneyPlatelets < 100, haemolysis · oliguria, ↑ creatinine
Lungs · fetusPulmonary oedema · severe growth restriction
