4. Pathophysiology
  1. Relaxed fundus (placental site) dimples in
  2. Cord traction or fundal push pulls it down
  3. Cervix closes as a constriction ring
  4. Veins blocked: fundus swells
Why shock is worse than the bleed

Traction on the ovaries, tubes and peritoneum pulled into the ring stimulates the vagus: neurogenic shock, often with bradycardia (pulse below 60). Bleeding adds hypovolaemia on top.

5. Clinical features
  • Severe lower abdominal pain in the 3rd stage
  • PPH in about 94%
  • Shock out of proportion to the blood loss
  • Bearing-down feeling, vaginal fullness
  • Abdomen: fundus absent, or a cup at the top (1st, 2nd degree)
  • VE: soft, purple-red mass in vagina or at vulva
  • Placenta may still be stuck on it
Clinical picture
Diagnosis
Abdomen: no fundus+VE: purple mass=Clinical, treat now
6. Investigations
Diagnosis is clinical
  • Do not wait for tests: replace first
  • At the same time: CBC, coagulation screen, group and cross-match 4–6 units
  • Ultrasound (empty fundus, inverted "U") only if unclear, e.g. subacute or chronic
Clinical scenarios
Suspected acute inversion. Ultrasound first?
No: clinical diagnosis; replace at once.
Cup-shaped dent at the fundus, mass felt inside the cervix. Degree?
First degree (incomplete).
