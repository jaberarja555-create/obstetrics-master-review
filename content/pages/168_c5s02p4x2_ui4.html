14. Prevention
Do: active 3rd stage
  • Oxytocin 10 IU IM after the baby
  • Wait for signs of separation: lengthening cord, gush of blood, firm rising uterus
  • CCT with counter-pressure above the pubis
  • Previous inversion: deliver in hospital
Do not
  • Pull the cord on a relaxed uterus
  • Pull before the placenta separates
  • Push on the fundus (Credé) to expel it
  • Rush manual removal on a soft uterus
Clinical picture
Prevention
Signs of separation+counter-pressure=No inversion
15. Prevention: the next pregnancy
  • Inversion can recur: book early and plan delivery in hospital
  • 3rd stage by a skilled attendant: active management, no early cord pull
  • IV line, cross-matched blood and oxytocin ready; anaesthetist aware
Exam case 1

Para 3, home birth. The traditional midwife pulled hard on the cord. Now: severe pain, pulse 52, BP 70/40, little bleeding, no fundus felt, red mass at the vulva. Answer: acute uterine inversion, vagal shock. First: help + immediate replacement, placenta left on.

Exam case 2

Para 4, 6 weeks after a home birth: irregular bleeding, offensive discharge, Hb 7 g/dL. Speculum: a red mass in the vagina, no stalk felt. Bimanual: no fundus, a cup at the top. Answer: chronic inversion, not a fibroid polyp. Next: ultrasound, correct anaemia, antibiotics, then laparotomy (Haultain).
