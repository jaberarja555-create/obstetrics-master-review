8. Management
Mild · at home
  • Small, frequent, dry, low-fat meals; avoid triggers
  • Reassure: usually settles by 12–16 wk
  • Oral antiemetic if needed
Admit if
  • Cannot keep down oral fluid or drugs
  • Ketonuria, weight loss, ↓ K⁺ / ↓ Na⁺
  • Coexisting illness (UTI, diabetes)
  1. IV fluids: 0.9% saline (or Hartmann's) with KCl by daily U&E. No dextrose until thiamine given; no hypotonic fluids (worsen ↓ Na⁺).
  2. Thiamine 100 mg IV before any dextrose, then oral; add pyridoxine (B6).
  3. Antiemetics by the ladder below, regular, IV/IM until vomiting stops, then oral.
  4. VTE risk assessment: stockings + LMWH prophylaxis while admitted.
  5. Nil by mouth 24–48 h if vomiting everything, then light diet. Monitor fluid chart, weight, U&E, ketones. Psychological support.
9. Antiemetic ladder
Exam trap

Do not correct low sodium fast: ≤ 10 mmol/L per 24 h. Rapid rise → pontine myelinolysis. Metoclopramide: ≤ 5 days (dystonia).

Termination of pregnancy.
