4.3Preterm labour
Preterm labour is regular contractions with cervical change after 24 wk and before 37 completed weeks. It affects about 10% of births and is the leading cause of perinatal death and handicap. Management buys time for steroids and transfer, then plans a safe birth.
1Classification and causes
  • Extremely preterm 24–27⁺⁶
  • Very 28–31⁺⁶
  • Mild 32–36⁺⁶ wk
  • About a third each: after PPROM, spontaneous with intact membranes, iatrogenic
  • Threatened: contractions, cervix unchanged
  • Established: progressive dilatation
  • Four pathways: infection (commonest proven), bleeding, over-distension, cervical weakness
  • Risks: previous preterm birth, twins, polyhydramnios, uterine anomaly, cone biopsy, pyelonephritis, malaria, APH, age under 17 or over 35, short interval, smoking
2Features and diagnosis
  • Regular pains, backache, pelvic pressure, show, fluid, bleeding
  • The diagnosis is clinical; progressive cervical change is the proof
  • Sterile speculum first; digital VE only with intact membranes and praevia excluded
  • Fetal fibronectin negative: birth unlikely in 7–14 days
  • TV cervical length: over 15 mm labour unlikely; 15 mm or less birth likely within 7 days
  • Scan, CTG, HVS, urine culture, FBC, CRP
  • Differential: Braxton Hicks, pyelonephritis, red degeneration, abruption, appendicitis
🩺 Exam scenario
  • 31 wk, tightenings, fFN negative → Birth unlikely in 7–14 days: no tocolysis
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Contractions alone are not preterm labour: without cervical change most do not deliver
