5.2Uterine Inversion

1. Definition

The uterus turns inside out: the fundus falls into the cavity, through the cervix, or out of the vulva. Almost always in the 3rd stage of labour. About 1 in 2000–3000 deliveries; maternal death up to 15% if not treated fast.

2. Classification
DegreeFundus reaches
1stInto the cavity, not past the cervix
2ndThrough the cervix, in the vagina
3rdTo or beyond the vulva
4thUterus and vagina turned out
TimingFrom delivery
Acute< 24 h: most cases (diagnosis is clinical: no scan needed)
Subacute24 h – 4 wk: ring tight
Chronic> 4 wk: rare, often a fundal fibroid polyp

Incomplete = 1st and 2nd degree · complete = 3rd and 4th degree.

Clinical scenario
Fundus has passed the cervix but not the vulva. Degree?
Second degree (incomplete).
3. Aetiology
  • Mismanaged 3rd stage: cord traction before separation, no counter-traction
  • Fundal pressure (Credé) on a relaxed uterus
  • Fundal placenta · short cord
  • Atony: relaxed fundus dimples easily
  • Placenta accreta · fundal fibroid
  • Uterine anomaly · previous inversion
Clinical picture
Uterine inversion
3rd stage pain+no fundus+mass in vagina=Inversion
