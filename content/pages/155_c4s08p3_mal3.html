Abnormal Lie and Compound Presentation

9. Definition

Transverse lie: fetal long axis across the uterus; presents by the shoulder (denominator = acromion; commonest position left dorso-anterior). Oblique lie: axis at an angle, a lower pole in an iliac fossa. Unstable lie: lie keeps changing after 36 wk. Incidence: ≈ 1 in 300 at term.

10. Aetiology
  • Multiparity, lax uterus: commonest
  • Polyhydramnios · prematurity
  • Twins (often the 2nd twin)
  • Placenta praevia · pelvic tumour
  • Uterine anomaly (arcuate, septate)
  • Contracted pelvis · fetal anomaly
11. Clinical features

Abdomen: uterus wide, fundal height less than dates · fundus empty · head in one flank, breech in the other · pelvic grip empty · FHS near the umbilicus. PV: high, empty pelvis; in labour a shoulder, ribs ("gridiron"), or a prolapsed arm or cord. Ultrasound confirms and looks for the cause.
