7. Types of anaemia
Iron deficiencyThe commonest. Ferritin ↓, small pale cells. Oral iron first
MegaloblasticNo folate, no DNA: cells grow, cannot divide. Folic acid 5 mg + iron. Low B12: hydroxocobalamin 1 mg IM first
Sickle cellHbSS: crises, infection, VTE, IUGR. Folic acid 5 mg, no iron unless ferritin low, malaria prophylaxis, growth scans. Crisis: fluids, O₂, analgesia, antibiotics, LMWH. Trait: more UTIs
β-thal traitFolic acid 5 mg; iron only if ferritin low. Two carriers: 25% major
G6PDAvoid sulphonamides, nitrofurantoin, primaquine
8. Complications
MotherHeart failure · PPH tolerated badly · APH · sepsis · VTE
BabyPreterm · IUGR · stillbirth · low iron stores · NTD (folate lack, not iron)
9. Prevention
  • Folic acid 0.4 mg daily from before conception to 12 wk. High risk (previous NTD, anti-epileptics, diabetes, obesity, sickle cell, thal, twins): 5 mg daily.
  • Iron + folate daily, booking to 3 months after birth (30–60 mg elemental). Hb at booking, 28, 36 wk. Treat malaria, hookworm (after 1st trimester); bed nets; space births.
10. Oral iron
DrugDoseKey point
Ferrous sulphate200 mg (65 mg Fe) 2–3× dailyFirst choice · black stool
Ferrous fumarate200 mg once or twice dailyAlternative
Ferrous gluconate300 mg (35 mg Fe) 2–3× dailyGentler, less iron
  • How: 1 h before food, with vitamin C. Not with tea, milk or antacids.
  • Response: reticulocytes ↑ at 7–10 days · Hb ↑ about 1 g/dL per week. Recheck at 3 wk. Continue 3 months after Hb is normal.
