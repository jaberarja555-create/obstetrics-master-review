35Clinical features

Triad: ROM + painless, small bleed + fetal distress (bradycardia, or a sinusoidal CTG from fetal anaemia). Mother stable, uterus soft. Vessels may be felt pulsating in the membranes on VE.

Clinical picture
Vasa praevia
ROM+painless bleed+fetal brady=Vasa praevia
Distinguishing features · After ROM, which one?
Shocked mother + pain + fetal distressAbruption
Mother well + bleed + fetal distressVasa praevia
Mother well, clear liquor, fetal distressCord prolapse
Sinusoidal CTG after the bleedFetal anaemia
36Investigations

Antenatal: transvaginal colour Doppler if the 20 wk scan shows a risk factor. In a bleed: Apt test or Kleihauer, but never wait for it.

37Management
Known before labour
  • Admit at 30–32 wk, steroids
  • Caesarean at 34–36 wk, before ROM
  • No ARM, no sweep
Bleeding in labour
  • 1st stage: immediate caesarean (decision to delivery < 30 min)
  • Fully dilated, head low: instrumental (fastest)
  • Neonatal team, O-neg blood (10–20 mL/kg for the baby)
Clinical scenarios
Test to show that vaginal blood is fetal?
Apt test (fetal Hb resists alkali).
Bleed at ROM, FHR 80, fully dilated, head at +2. Best delivery?
Instrumental delivery now: fastest route.
