10. Clinical features

History: watery leak (ROM) · reduced fetal movements. Examination: uterus small for dates · fetal parts easily felt, fetus moulded by the uterine wall · often breech.

11. Investigations
  • Sterile speculum: pool (ROM)
  • If ROM: FBC, CRP, high vaginal swab
  • Temp and pulse 4-hourly
  • Scan: AFI, growth, kidneys, bladder
  • UA Doppler if growth is poor
  • CTG in the 3rd trimester
12. Complications
From the causeROM → chorioamnionitis, preterm · FGR → stillbirth
From low volumePulmonary hypoplasia (if before ≈ 22 wk) · limb contractures, talipes · Potter facies · cord compression → hypoxia → meconium, fetal distress in labour
13. Management
ROM ≥ 34–36 wkInduce labour unless caesarean is needed for another reason
ROM < 34–36 wkErythromycin 250 mg oral 6-hourly × 10 days · dexamethasone 6 mg IM 12-hourly × 4 if < 34 wk · watch for infection
FGRTiming by umbilical artery Doppler + CTG (see 3.5)
Isolated, Doppler normalRecheck cause · serial CTG, AFI · deliver at term
Renal anomalyRefer to a fetal medicine centre
Exam trap

ECV is contraindicated: no fluid cushion. ACE inhibitors are banned in pregnancy: fetal renal failure, oligohydramnios, hypocalvaria, death.

Clinical scenarios
Breech at 37 wk, AFI 3 cm. Offer ECV?
No: oligohydramnios contraindicates ECV.
Newborn: flat nose, low-set ears, talipes, breathing failure. Cause?
Potter sequence: renal agenesis.
