  • Stop tocolysis, start oxytocin 40 IU in 500 mL over 4 h; fist in the fundus until it contracts
  • Then manual removal of the placenta in theatre; check for tears

10Subacute and chronic inversion
  • Ring fixed, uterus swollen: treat shock and infection first
  • Replacement under GA, usually at laparotomy (Huntington, Haultain)
  • Chronic: look for a fundal fibroid polyp; hysterectomy if necrotic
🩺 Exam scenario
  • 6 weeks after a home birth, red mass in the vagina, no fundus, a cup at the top → Chronic inversion: laparotomy (Haultain)
11Prevention
  • Active 3rd stage: oxytocin 10 IU IM
  • Controlled cord traction only after signs of separation, with counter-pressure above the pubis
  • No Credé, no pulling on a relaxed uterus
  • Previous inversion: it can recur; deliver in hospital
5.3Amniotic fluid embolism
Sudden collapse in labour, at delivery or soon after, when amniotic fluid and fetal cells enter the mother's blood. Rare (1 in 8 000–30 000), unpredictable, not preventable, often fatal. Timing: 70% in labour around ROM · 19% at caesarean · 11% after delivery.
1Risk factors
  • Induction, ARM, strong contractions (oxytocin)
  • Caesarean, instrumental delivery
  • Multiple pregnancy, polyhydramnios
  • Older mother, eclampsia
