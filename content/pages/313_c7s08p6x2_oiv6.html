Station: "Write the post-operative orders for this woman after an emergency LSCS for failure to progress" or "she is 6 h after CS: review her". Write clear, dated, signed orders: drug, dose, route, frequency, duration.

17. Post-operative orders
ObservationsPulse, BP every 30 min for 2 h, then hourly · urine output · fundus, wound and vaginal bleeding · pain
Fluids, feedingIV fluids (normal saline / dextrose 5%) until drinking · oxytocin 10 IU in the 2nd and 4th bottle · sips at 6–8 h, eat when bowel sounds return
AnalgesiaDiclofenac 75 mg IM 8-hourly (not in asthma or PE with renal impairment) · paracetamol 1 g 6-hourly
AntibioticsIf infection risk (long labour, long ROM): cefuroxime 750 mg IV 8-hourly + metronidazole 500 mg IV 8-hourly for 24–48 h, then oral to complete 5–7 days
VTEEarly mobilisation, stockings, fluids · enoxaparin 40 mg SC daily (weight-adjusted), first dose 4 h after the spinal, for 10 days after emergency CS or with another risk factor; 6 wk if high risk
Baby, breastSkin-to-skin and breastfeeding in recovery · help her lift and position the baby (wound pain)
Catheter, woundCatheter out at 12 h (after mobile) · dressing off at 24–48 h · stitches out day 5–7 if not absorbable
Viva questions
First LMWH dose after a spinal: when?
At least 4 h after the spinal.
Why oxytocin infusion after an emergency CS?
Prevent atonic PPH after a long labour.
18. Reviewing her on the ward
  1. Ask: pain, nausea, passing urine and flatus, leg pain, breathlessness, breastfeeding.
  2. Look: pulse, BP, temperature, RR, SpO₂ · pallor · chest · abdomen: fundus firm and at or below the umbilicus, distension, bowel sounds · wound · lochia · calves · catheter bag.
  3. Act: Hb on day 1–2 if loss was heavy · encourage walking and feeding · plan discharge on day 2–3 with wound care, danger signs, contraception and follow-up at 6 wk.
What to say · ward round
"Good morning. How is the pain? Have you passed urine and wind? Please walk a little every few hours and keep feeding the baby: it helps you heal and prevents clots."

Common errors

Forgetting thromboprophylaxis · NSAID in an asthmatic or a woman with severe PE and poor urine · ergometrine in a hypertensive woman · no oxytocin after a long labour (atony) · catheter left in for days.
