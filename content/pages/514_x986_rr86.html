2Pathophysiology
  • An anaphylactoid reaction, not a mechanical block
  • Phase 1 (minutes): pulmonary vasospasm, right heart failure, hypoxia, collapse
  • Phase 2 (within 4 h): DIC and massive bleeding
  • Only 12% have DIC at the start; almost all survivors develop it
3Clinical features
  • Warning: sudden breathlessness, chills, agitation, "something is wrong"
  • Severe dyspnoea, cyanosis, hypoxia → respiratory arrest
  • Hypotension, arrhythmia → cardiac arrest
  • Convulsions, coma
  • Fetal bradycardia
  • Then bleeding from uterus, wound, cannula sites: blood will not clot
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Breathless and cyanosed minutes after ARM on oxytocin, then a fit → Amniotic fluid embolism
4Investigations and diagnosis
  • None proves AFE: send while resuscitating
  • ABG: hypoxia, acidosis
  • Clotting: PT, aPTT up, fibrinogen down, D-dimer up
  • Bedside clot test: no clot in 7–10 min
  • CXR: pulmonary oedema
  • ECG, echo: right heart strain
  • Diagnosis is clinical, by exclusion
  • Squames in PA blood: supportive only
  • Definitive: fetal squames in lung vessels at post-mortem
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Never wait for clotting results: use the clot test and give blood products
