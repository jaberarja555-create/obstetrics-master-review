3Investigations and complications
  • FBC, cross-match 4–6 units, clotting, fibrinogen, RFT, LFT, bedside clot test
  • Do not wait for results
  • Complications: shock, DIC, organ failure, death; transfusion reactions; hysterectomy; anaemia, sepsis, Sheehan syndrome
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Cannot breastfeed and no periods after a massive PPH → Sheehan syndrome
4Emergency management
  • Call for help; all first steps at once
  • Two cannulae, bloods, warm crystalloid 20 mL/kg, then blood (O-negative if needed)
  • Tranexamic acid 1 g IV over 10 min within 3 h of birth
  • Empty the bladder (catheter)
  • Chart pulse, BP, SpO₂, urine and loss every 15 min
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Bleeding 4 h after birth, TXA not given → Too late for TXA
5Atony: uterotonics
  • Rub up the uterus, then drugs
  • Oxytocin 10 IU slowly then 40 IU over 4 h: first line; fast bolus drops BP
  • Ergometrine 0.5 mg IM or slow IV: not in hypertension, PE or heart disease
  • Misoprostol 800 µg sublingual or rectal: fever, shivering; no fridge
  • Carboprost 250 µg deep IM every 15 min, max 8 doses: not in asthma
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Atony, BP 165/110 → Avoid ergometrine
  • Atony, drowsy, cannot swallow → Rectal misoprostol
6Mechanical measures and surgery
  • Bimanual compression buys time
  • Balloon tamponade (Bakri or condom catheter, 300–500 mL saline, 12–24 h) with oxytocin and antibiotics
  • Laparotomy: repair tears, then B-Lynch suture (works in about 9 of 10) → uterine artery ligation → internal iliac ligation → hysterectomy (decide early if she is getting worse)
