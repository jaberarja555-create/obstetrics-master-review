10. Special situations
The RhD-negative mother

An antibody screen is done at booking and at 28 weeks. Anti-D immunoglobulin is given within 72 hours of any sensitising event: vaginal bleeding, miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy, external cephalic version (ECV), abdominal trauma, amniocentesis, and after birth if the baby is RhD positive. Routine anti-D is also given at 28 weeks where supplies allow. If antibodies are already present, the fetus is monitored in a specialist unit.

Late booking (after 20 weeks)

All booking tests are done on the same day, and the pregnancy is dated using every available clue (see 1.2). Iron and folic acid, two doses of Td 4 weeks apart, SP and a bed net are started. The health worker looks carefully for anaemia, hypertension, malpresentation and twins, and plans the birth early.

11. Danger signs

Every woman and her family must be taught the following danger signs and told to come to the hospital immediately if any of them appears.

  • Vaginal bleeding
  • Convulsions (fits), severe headache or blurred vision
  • Fever, or being too weak to get out of bed
  • Severe abdominal pain
  • Fast or difficult breathing
  • Reduced or absent fetal movements
  • Leaking of fluid from the vagina (rupture of the membranes)
  • Swelling of the face and hands
12. Risk assessment

At booking, and again at every visit, each woman is classified as low risk or high risk. A low-risk woman can be cared for by a midwife or at a health centre and should give birth in a health facility. A high-risk woman needs doctor-led care and should give birth in a hospital that has an operating theatre and blood for transfusion. Risk factors come from three sources: her previous pregnancies, her medical history, and the current pregnancy.

Previous pregnanciesPrevious caesarean section (CS) or other uterine surgery, postpartum haemorrhage (PPH), pre-eclampsia, stillbirth, preterm birth, three or more miscarriages, a baby with a congenital anomaly, and grand multiparity.
Medical historyHeart disease, hypertension, diabetes, epilepsy, kidney or thyroid disease, sickle cell disease, HIV infection and hepatitis B.
Current pregnancyAge under 18 or 35 years and over; BMI 30 or more, or under 18.5; height under 150 cm; multiple pregnancy; Rh antibodies; haemoglobin below 9 g/dL; BP 140/90 mmHg or higher; antepartum haemorrhage (APH); a fundal height that does not match the dates; malpresentation at 36 weeks; and prolonged pregnancy.
