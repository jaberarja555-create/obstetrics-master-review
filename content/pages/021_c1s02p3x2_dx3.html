7. Uterine size that does not match the dates

From about 20 weeks the height of the uterus (symphysis–fundal height) in centimetres roughly equals the gestational age in weeks. When the uterus is larger or smaller than expected, the first step is always to check the dates.

Uterus larger than dates
  • Wrong dates (the first cause to exclude)
  • Multiple pregnancy
  • Polyhydramnios; a large fetus (macrosomia, as in diabetes)
  • Molar pregnancy (in early pregnancy)
  • Fibroids, an ovarian mass, or a full bladder
Uterus smaller than dates
  • Wrong dates
  • Fetal growth restriction (FGR)
  • Oligohydramnios; intrauterine fetal death (IUFD)
  • Missed miscarriage (in early pregnancy)
  • Transverse lie, or a fetal head deep in the pelvis
Hospital protocol

Many women book late, without an early scan, and do not know their LMP. In this situation all available information is combined: the date of quickening (add about 20–22 weeks in a first pregnancy, or 22–24 weeks in a later pregnancy, to reach the EDD), the earliest recorded fundal height, and the first available scan. The dates should be recorded as uncertain, because this affects decisions about preterm and post-term care.
