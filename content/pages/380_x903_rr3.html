6Gastrointestinal and liver
  • Nausea peaks at 8–12 wk (hCG), settles by 16–20 wk
  • Heartburn; slow gastric emptying in labour: aspiration risk under general anaesthesia
  • Constipation, gingivitis, gallstones more common
  • ALP rises 2–4 times (placental): normal
  • Albumin falls; total calcium falls, ionised normal
  • ALT, bilirubin and bile acids do not rise: if raised, think obstetric cholestasis, AFLP, HELLP or hepatitis
🩺 Exam scenario
  • ALT 120 IU/L with itching at 34 wk → Obstetric cholestasis
7Placental hormones and endocrine changes
  • HCG (syncytiotrophoblast) keeps the corpus luteum until the placenta takes over at 8–10 wk; very high in molar and twin pregnancy
  • HPL rises with placental size: insulin resistance in the second half
  • Progesterone: heartburn, constipation, dilated ureters
  • Relaxin: loose pelvic joints
  • Thyroid: TBG doubles, total T4 rises, free T4 normal; TSH low in the first trimester (hCG)
  • Pituitary doubles (prolactin cells): Sheehan's syndrome after massive PPH
  • Gestational diabetes appears at 24–28 wk: screening time
  • Reduced cell-mediated immunity: severe malaria, varicella, hepatitis E
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Low TSH, normal free T4 in the first trimester → Normal (hCG effect): no treatment
  • High total T4, normal free T4 and TSH at 16 wk → Raised thyroxine-binding globulin
8Skin, breasts and musculoskeletal
  • Linea nigra, chloasma (worse in sunlight), striae gravidarum (red-purple now, silvery later)
  • Spider naevi, palmar erythema: fade after delivery
  • Breasts tender from 6 wk; Montgomery's tubercles; colostrum from mid-pregnancy
  • Symphysis pain, backache, lordosis, carpal tunnel syndrome
  • Hair falls out 2–4 months after delivery
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Itching of the palms and soles without a rash is not dry skin: check bile acids
