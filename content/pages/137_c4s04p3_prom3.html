10. Intrapartum management (PROM and PPROM)
  • Deliver in hospital; paediatrician called for the birth
  • Continuous CTG · MEOWS chart for sepsis
  • GBS prophylaxis (IV penicillin) if GBS positive, ROM > 24 h, or preterm
  • Induce now (no waiting) if meconium, GBS positive or any sign of infection
  • High head or malpresentation: check FHR at once for cord prolapse
  • Caesarean only for obstetric reasons; infection is not itself a reason for CS
11. Chorioamnionitis

Definition: infection of the membranes, liquor and placenta, usually ascending from the vagina after ROM (GBS, E. coli, anaerobes).

DiagnosisClinical criteria
MotherFever > 37.8 °C · tachycardia · uterine tenderness · offensive discharge or liquor
FetusTachycardia > 160/min, often the first sign
TestsWBC and CRP rising · HVS and blood cultures
1 · RecogniseFever, FHR ↑
2 · CulturesBlood, HVS
3 · TreatIV antibiotics
4 · DeliverAny gestation

Antibiotics: ampicillin 2 g IV 6-hourly + gentamicin 5 mg/kg IV daily + metronidazole 500 mg IV 8-hourly; continue after birth until fever settles. Delivery: induce or augment with oxytocin; caesarean only for obstetric reasons. No tocolysis; never delay birth for steroids.

Exam trap

Chorioamnionitis is an indication for delivery at any gestation, even 26 wk. Antibiotics alone cannot clear an infected uterus.

Complications: mother: sepsis, PPH (atony), endometritis, wound infection · baby: early-onset sepsis, pneumonia, meningitis, cerebral palsy.
