4.7Breech presentation
Hard round ballotable head in the fundus at 36 weeks → Breech: offer ECV
Footling breech in labour → CS (cord prolapse risk 15%)
Aftercoming head stuck → Mauriceau–Smellie–Veit, then Piper forceps
Facts
3–4% at term · frank (65%), complete (25%), footling (10%) · commonest association prematurity
Diagnosis
Head in fundus, FHS above umbilicus · PV: anus and ischial tuberosities in line · scan at 36 wk
ECV
At 36–37 wk · success ≈ 50% · terbutaline 0.25 mg SC · Rh-negative: anti-D 500 IU · contraindicated: APH, ROM, oligohydramnios, previous CS (relative)
Elective CS
39 wk · footling, star-gazing head, EFW > 3.8 kg, praevia, choice
Vaginal breech
Hands off · legs: Pinard · arms: Løvset · head: Mauriceau–Smellie–Veit · Burns–Marshall
Baby
Hip ultrasound at 6 wk (DDH)
Exam traps
Never pull on the breech · umbilicus to head should take < 4 min · footling breech has the highest cord prolapse risk
4.8Malpresentations & malpositions
Backache, early urge to push, flat lower abdomen → Occipito-posterior position
Head arrested at the spines with the sagittal suture transverse → Deep transverse arrest: rotate or CS
Persistent brow presentation in labour → Caesarean section
Diameters
Flexed vertex SOB 9.5 · deflexed OP OF 11.5 · brow MV 13.5 · face SMB 9.5
OP position
25% in early labour · 90% rotate forward (long rotation) · persistent OP: face to pubis · rotation (Kielland/manual/ventouse) or CS
Face
Mento-anterior: vaginal birth · mento-posterior persistent: CS · never ventouse · rule out anencephaly
Brow
Commonest largest diameter · persistent → CS
Transverse lie
Cause: multiparity, praevia, polyhydramnios · unstable lie at 37 wk: admit · in labour: CS
Compound
Hand beside the head: leave it
Exam traps
Ventouse is never used for face presentation · a transverse lie in labour with ROM risks cord/arm prolapse · brow does not deliver vaginally at term
