3The 3 Ps
  • Power: 3–5 contractions in 10 min, 40–60 s, fundal dominance
  • More than 5 in 10 min: fetal hypoxia, uterine rupture
  • Upper segment contracts and retracts; thin lower segment stretches (physiological retraction ring)
  • Passenger: vertex, OA, well flexed; posterior fontanelle (Y, 3 sutures) gives the position
  • Passage: gynaecoid 50%, android 20% (OP, deep transverse arrest), anthropoid 25% (face to pubis), platypelloid 5% (inlet delay)
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Six contractions in 10 min on oxytocin → Stop oxytocin: hypoxia and rupture risk
4Pelvic diameters and engagement
  • Inlet: AP 11 cm (true conjugate), transverse 13 cm: head enters transverse
  • Mid-cavity round 12 cm (spines 10.5): head rotates
  • Outlet: AP 13, transverse 11 cm: head leaves AP
  • Diagonal conjugate 12.5 cm (promontory not reached = adequate inlet)
  • Engaged: biparietal (9.5 cm) past the inlet = 2/5 or less palpable
  • Station: lowest bony point vs the spines (−5 to +5)
  • High head at term in a primi: think CPD, praevia, wrong dates
5Mechanism of labour
  • Engagement
  • Descent
  • Flexion (SOB 9.5 cm presents)
  • Internal rotation (occiput 1/8 forward at the pelvic floor)
  • Extension (born under the symphysis)
  • Restitution
  • External rotation (shoulders AP)
  • Expulsion (anterior shoulder first)
  • Deflexed OP: occipito-frontal 11.5 cm; brow: mento-vertical 13.5 cm (largest)
  • Caput crosses sutures, gone in days; cephalhaematoma stops at sutures and grows on day 2
  • Severe moulding + caput = obstruction
