11. External cephalic version (ECV)

Definition: turning the fetus from breech (or transverse) to cephalic through the abdominal wall, by a forward roll.

When36 wk primigravida · 37 wk multipara; can be tried until early labour with intact membranes
WhereLabour ward with theatre ready (≈ 0.5% need emergency CS)
BeforeScan (breech, liquor, placenta) · CTG · tocolytic terbutaline 0.25 mg SC
HowLift breech out of the pelvis → push it up to the side of the back → guide head forward. No force
AfterCTG 30 min · anti-D 500 IU IM if RhD-negative + Kleihauer
Success≈ 50% overall · 40% primigravida · 60% multipara · turns back to breech in 3%
12. ECV: contraindications and complications
Contraindications

Absolute: placenta praevia, APH · twins · CS needed anyway (≥ 2 scars, FGR) · ruptured membranes · severe oligohydramnios · fetal compromise, major anomaly · pre-eclampsia · Rh isoimmunisation.

Relative: one previous CS · uterine anomaly · hypertension.

Complications
  • FHR slowing (≈ 8%): usually recovers
  • Abruption · vaginal bleeding
  • Cord accident · cord prolapse
  • Feto-maternal bleed → Rh antibodies
  • ROM, labour · emergency CS

Better success: multipara, normal liquor, breech not engaged, head easy to feel, posterior placenta, tocolysis.
