🩺 Exam scenario
  • Breech in 3 pregnancies in a row → Uterine anomaly
3Diagnosis
  • Discomfort under the ribs; kicks felt low
  • Hard ballotable head in the fundus; FHS at or above the umbilicus
  • VE: ischial tuberosities and anus in one line, sacrum, genitalia, feet, meconium on the glove
  • Ultrasound: type, EFW, head flexion, placenta, liquor, anomalies (2–3 times more common)
  • About 30% found only in labour
  • Face vs breech: face has gums and a triangle of mouth and malar bones
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Thick meconium in a breech in the second stage → Often normal (compressed bowel)
4Complications
  • Fetal: cord prolapse (footling 15%, complete 5%, frank 0.5%), trapped aftercoming head, intracranial bleed, fractures, Erb palsy, liver and adrenal tears, higher perinatal death
  • Maternal: CS risks, tears, PPH, a scar for next time
⚠️ Exam trap
  • The body can slip through a cervix that is not fully dilated (preterm, footling); the bigger head is then trapped
5External cephalic version
  • Offer at 36 wk primigravida, 37 wk multipara
  • Labour ward with theatre; scan, CTG, terbutaline 0.25 mg SC
  • Forward roll, no force
  • After: CTG 30 min; anti-D 500 IU + Kleihauer if RhD-negative
  • Success about 50% (40% primi, 60% multi)
  • Contraindicated: praevia, APH, twins, CS needed anyway, ruptured membranes, severe oligohydramnios, fetal compromise
  • Complications: transient FHR slowing, abruption, cord accident, feto-maternal bleed
  • Knee-chest position does not work
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Primigravida, breech at 37 wk, normal liquor → ECV
  • Bleeding and FHR 80 after ECV → Abruption: emergency CS
