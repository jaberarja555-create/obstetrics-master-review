6.2Puerperal Pyrexia & Sepsis

1. Definition
TermDefinition
Puerperal pyrexiaTemperature ≥ 38 °C in the first 14 days after birth (any cause)
Puerperal morbidity≥ 38 °C on any 2 of the first 10 days, not counting the first 24 h
Puerperal sepsis (WHO)Genital tract infection from rupture of membranes or labour to day 42, with ≥ 2 of: pelvic pain · fever ≥ 38.5 °C · abnormal discharge · foul smell · slow involution
Maternal sepsis (WHO)Organ dysfunction caused by infection in pregnancy, birth, after abortion or up to day 42

Sepsis is the leading cause of maternal death in Sudan, ahead of haemorrhage. On the MEOWS chart, 1 red or 2 yellow triggers need urgent review. Infection is usually polymicrobial and ascends from the vagina to the raw placental site.

Clinical picture
Puerperal pyrexia
≥ 38 °C+first 14 days=Puerperal pyrexia
2. Causes and day of onset
Causes by day · chest first, then urine and womb, then wound, then breast and legs. Endometritis is the commonest cause. Also: retained products, infected haematoma, cannula site, epidural abscess, drug fever, and malaria or typhoid in Sudan.
