7. Investigations
  • Ultrasound: confirms it; the standard
  • Type of breech · EFW
  • Head flexed or hyperextended
  • Placenta site · liquor volume
  • Anomaly scan (hydrocephalus, anencephaly)
  • Pelvimetry: no role
Clinical scenario
Breech suspected on palpation at 36 wk. Next step?
Ultrasound: type, EFW, head flexion, placenta, anomalies. Anomalies are 2–3 times more common than with a cephalic baby, and about 30% of breech babies are first found in labour.

9. Complications
FetalCord prolapse: footling 15%, complete 5%, frank 0.5% · trapped aftercoming head → asphyxia · fast head delivery → intracranial bleed · fractured humerus, femur, clavicle · Erb palsy · liver, adrenal tear · prematurity, anomaly · higher perinatal death
MaternalMore caesareans and their risks · perineal, cervical tears · PPH · infection · a scar for the next pregnancy
Exam trap

The danger in breech is the head, not the buttocks. The body slips through a cervix not fully dilated (preterm, footling); the bigger head is trapped.
