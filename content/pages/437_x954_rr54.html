2Symmetrical and asymmetrical FGR
  • Symmetrical (20–30%): early onset; fetal cause (chromosomes, TORCH, drugs); HC, AC and FL all small; too few cells; poor outlook
  • Asymmetrical (70–80%): late onset; placental insufficiency (PE, HTN, malnutrition); AC small, head spared (raised HC:AC); abnormal Doppler, oligohydramnios; good catch-up if delivered in time
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • All measurements small from 20 wk → Symmetrical FGR: karyotype, TORCH
  • AC small at 34 wk, mother has PE → Asymmetrical FGR
3Causes and pathophysiology
  • Maternal: malnutrition, anaemia, HTN, renal or heart disease, diabetes with vasculopathy, APS, smoking, alcohol, drugs, interval under 6 months
  • Placental (commonest): pre-eclampsia, abruption, praevia, infarcts
  • Fetal: trisomy 13, 18, 21; infection (CMV, rubella, toxoplasma, malaria); twins; anomalies
  • Poor placental flow → brain sparing → low liver glycogen (AC falls first) and less renal flow (oligohydramnios)
4Screening and detection
  • Early dating scan comes first
  • SFH lag over 3 cm or reduced movements → growth scan + umbilical artery Doppler
  • Low PAPP-A; uterine artery Doppler at 20–24 wk
🩺 Exam scenario
  • 32 wk, SFH 28 cm, mother hypertensive → Growth scan + umbilical artery Doppler
5Investigations
  • Growth scan: HC, AC, FL, EFW; AC is the best single measure; every 3 wk (shorter gives false positives)
  • Liquor: falling AFI
  • Umbilical artery Doppler: raised PI → absent → reversed EDF; normal = FGR unlikely
  • MCA: low PI = brain sparing; high peak velocity = fetal anaemia
  • Ductus venosus absent or reversed a-wave = acidaemia (late)
  • CTG
  • Cause: anomaly scan, karyotype if early or symmetrical, TORCH, BP
