2.11Thyroid disease in pregnancy
Thyroid disease is common in pregnancy. Iodine needs rise, TBG doubles, and hCG stimulates the TSH receptor, so thyroid tests are read with free T4 and trimester ranges (TSH upper limit 2.5 in the first trimester, 3.0 later).
1Thyrotoxicosis: causes
  • Graves' disease about 95%
  • Others: toxic nodular goitre, adenoma, thyroiditis, amiodarone, lithium
  • Graves': TRAb positive, goitre, eye signs; improves in late pregnancy, relapses after birth; needs antithyroid drugs
  • Gestational transient thyrotoxicosis: high hCG (hyperemesis, mole, twins), TRAb negative, no goitre or eye signs, settles by 18–20 wk: no antithyroid drugs
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Low TSH at 10 wk with exophthalmos → Graves': start PTU
  • Low TSH at 10 wk, vomiting, no goitre → Gestational: no drug
2Features and complications
  • Weight loss despite appetite, tremor, resting pulse over 100, eye signs, goitre
  • TFTs: low TSH, high free T4; TRAb
  • Maternal: miscarriage, pre-eclampsia, heart failure, AF, thyroid storm
  • Fetal: preterm birth, FGR, stillbirth, fetal thyrotoxicosis, fetal goitre from drugs
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Past Graves' treated by surgery or radioiodine: still check TRAb; the antibodies cross the placenta (fetal heart over 160, goitre, FGR)
3Treatment of hyperthyroidism
  • PTU 150–400 mg/day in the first trimester, then carbimazole 15–40 mg/day
  • Carbimazole embryopathy: aplasia cutis, choanal and oesophageal atresia
  • PTU: liver failure
  • Propranolol for a few weeks only (FGR)
  • Lowest dose keeping free T4 at the upper limit of normal; TFT every 4–6 wk
  • Breastfeeding safe at PTU under 150 or carbimazole under 15 mg/day
