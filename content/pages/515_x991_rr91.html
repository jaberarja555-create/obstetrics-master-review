8Prevention
  • Elective caesarean: EFW > 4.5 kg in a diabetic or > 5 kg in a non-diabetic woman
  • Diabetes: good control; planned birth 37+0 – 38+6 wk in pre-existing diabetes, by 40+6 wk in gestational diabetes (NICE; local units: 38–39 wk)
  • Previous SD recurs in about 1 in 10
  • Known risk: deliver in hospital, no mid-cavity instrument
  • Scan weight can be wrong by 10–15%: every labour ward drills HELPERR
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Diabetic, EFW 4.6 kg before labour → Elective caesarean
  • GDM, EFW 4.2 kg, good control → Individual plan, not automatic caesarean

Puerperium and Maternal Health
6.1Normal puerperium
The puerperium runs from delivery of the placenta until the genital organs return to the non-pregnant state: about 6 weeks (42 days). Immediate = first 24 h · early = to day 7 · remote = to 6 wk. Main dangers: sepsis, haemorrhage, VTE, mood disorders.
1Physiological changes: the genital tract
  • Fundus at the umbilicus after birth, falls about 1 cm a day, not palpable by day 14 (still felt = subinvolution)
  • Uterus 1000 g → 60–100 g at 6 wk
  • Contraction closes the vessels ("living ligatures"); muscle shrinks by autolysis
  • New endometrium from the basal layer by day 10; placental site heals by 6 wk without a scar
  • Cervix 1 finger by day 10–14; os stays a transverse slit
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Day 7 after a normal delivery → Fundus midway between umbilicus and pubis
