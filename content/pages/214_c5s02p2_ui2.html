Clinical scenario
Fundus has passed the cervix but not the vulva. Degree?
Second degree (incomplete).
3Aetiology
  • Mismanaged 3rd stage: cord traction before separation, no counter-traction
  • Fundal pressure (Credé) on a relaxed uterus
  • Fundal placenta · short cord
  • Atony: relaxed fundus dimples easily
  • Placenta accreta · fundal fibroid
  • Uterine anomaly · previous inversion
Clinical picture
Uterine inversion
3rd stage pain+no fundus+mass in vagina=Inversion
Distinguishing features · PPH: which cause?
Bleed, soft fundus feltAtonic PPH
Bleed, no fundus, mass in vaginaUterine inversion
Bleed, firm uterus, bright redGenital tract tear
Shock + bradycardia, small lossInversion: vagal
4Pathophysiology
  1. Relaxed fundus (placental site) dimples in
  2. Cord traction or fundal push pulls it down
  3. Cervix closes as a constriction ring
  4. Veins blocked: fundus swells
Why shock is worse than the bleed

Traction on the ovaries, tubes and peritoneum pulled into the ring stimulates the vagus: neurogenic shock, often with bradycardia (pulse below 60). Bleeding adds hypovolaemia on top.
