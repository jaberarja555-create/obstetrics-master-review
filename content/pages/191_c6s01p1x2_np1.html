3. Lochia
TypeDaysColour and content
Rubra1–4Red: blood, decidua, vernix, lanugo
Serosa5–9Pink-brown: less blood, serum, leucocytes
Alba10 → 3–6 wkYellow-white: leucocytes, mucus, epithelial cells
Normal lochia

Smell is stale, not foul. Amount falls day by day. Total about 250 mL. Heavier on standing and when feeding.

Abnormal lochia

Foul → infection. Red again or persistent rubra → retained tissue, subinvolution. None + fever → blocked drainage (lochiometra).

4. Physiological changes: other systems
SystemChange and why it matters
Heart, circulationCardiac output rises immediately after birth (autotransfusion from the contracted uterus) → heart disease decompensates now. Diuresis brings plasma volume to normal by 2–3 wk
BloodWBC up to 20–25 × 10⁹/L in the first days: not infection by itself. Clotting factors stay high → hypercoagulable for 6 wk (VTE)
UrinaryDiuresis day 2–5. Oedematous, numb bladder (epidural, tears) → retention: void within 6 h. Ureters normal by 6–8 wk
TemperatureSlight rise in the first 24 h (exertion, dehydration) is normal; ≥ 38 °C is never "normal"
Bowel, weightConstipation: fasting, pain, fear of stitches, opioids · ≈ 5–6 kg lost at birth, more with diuresis
HormoneshPL, hCG gone by day 10 · oestrogen, progesterone non-pregnant by day 7 · prolactin high only if breastfeeding
Mood"Blues" in up to 80% on day 3–5; settles by 2 wk
