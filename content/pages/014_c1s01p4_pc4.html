9. Endocrine glands and metabolism
ThyroidThe gland enlarges slightly because more iodine is lost in the urine. Oestrogen doubles the level of thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG), so total T4 rises, but free T4 stays normal. hCG acts on the TSH receptor, so TSH is low in the first trimester. Thyroid tests must therefore be interpreted with free T4 and trimester-specific ranges.
PituitaryThe gland doubles in size, mainly because the prolactin-producing cells enlarge. Prolactin rises about ten times to prepare the breasts for lactation. The enlarged gland is easily damaged by low blood pressure, so massive PPH can cause pituitary necrosis (Sheehan's syndrome).
AdrenalCortisol rises, which contributes to striae and insulin resistance. Aldosterone rises through the renin–angiotensin system, so more salt and water are retained.
Glucose metabolismIn the first half of pregnancy the mother stores fat, and her fasting glucose is lower than normal. In the second half, hPL, cortisol and progesterone cause insulin resistance, so glucose stays higher after meals and more passes to the fetus. This is why gestational diabetes usually appears at 24–28 weeks, the time when screening is done.
ImmunityCell-mediated immunity is reduced so that the mother does not reject the fetus. As a result, malaria is more severe (parasites collect in the placenta), and some viral infections such as varicella and hepatitis E are more dangerous. Some autoimmune diseases, such as rheumatoid arthritis, often improve.
Lipids and calciumCholesterol and triglycerides rise. Calcium absorption from the gut increases (through vitamin D) to supply the fetal skeleton. Total calcium falls with albumin, but ionised calcium stays normal.
Clinical picture
Thyroid tests in early pregnancy
High hCG+low TSH+normal free T4=Normal: no treatment
Clinical scenario
At 16 weeks a woman's total T4 is high, while her free T4 and TSH are normal. What is the explanation?
Oestrogen doubles thyroxine-binding globulin, so the bound (total) T4 rises.
