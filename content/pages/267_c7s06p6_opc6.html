14. Example 4 · late decelerations
READ IT · Baseline 155, variability < 5, smooth dips with the lowest point after the peak of every contraction (L). Cause: placental insufficiency (PE, FGR, abruption, oxytocin). Pathological.
Presenting the findings · example 4
"A woman at 38 weeks with pre-eclampsia in labour. Three contractions in 10 minutes. Baseline 155, variability reduced below 5, no accelerations, repeated late decelerations. This is a pathological CTG. I would turn her to the left, stop oxytocin, give IV fluids, call the obstetric team and check for fetal acidosis or deliver."
«دقات قلب الجنين فيها هبوط متأخر بعد كل طلقة، ده معناه إنو الجنين ما واصلو أكسجين كفاية. حنقلبك على جنبك الشمال ونديك محاليل، ولو ما اتحسّن ممكن نولّدك بسرعة.»

Distinguishing features · Which dip?
Dip with contraction, 9 cmEarly: head
V-dips after ROM, scanty liquorVariable: cord
Shallow dips after peaks, PELate: placenta
Dips on every contraction, 6 in 10 minTachysystole: stop oxytocin
Viva questions
Commonest deceleration in labour?
Variable (cord compression).
Late decelerations with normal variability: worse or better than with low variability?
Better: low variability means the fetus is decompensating.
15. Example 5 · prolonged deceleration (bradycardia)
READ IT · Normal trace, then the rate drops to about 80 and stays for more than 3 min (B). Pathological, emergency. Consider: cord prolapse, abruption, uterine rupture, hypotension after spinal, tachysystole.
