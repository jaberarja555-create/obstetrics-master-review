🩺 Exam scenarios
  • BP 145/95, protein 1+, severe headache → Severe pre-eclampsia
  • RUQ pain, shock, falling Hb → Liver capsule rupture
4Investigations
  • Urine dipstick → PCR or 24 h protein
  • FBC (platelets), creatinine, uric acid (rises with severity), LFT, LDH, film; clotting if platelets low
  • Fetus: growth scan, liquor, umbilical artery Doppler, CTG
  • Absent or reversed end-diastolic flow = deliver soon
🩺 Exam scenario
  • PE at 32 wk, reversed EDF → Steroids, MgSO₄, deliver (usually CS)
5Fits in pregnancy and complications
  • Differential: eclampsia, epilepsy, hypoglycaemia, meningitis, cerebral venous thrombosis, cerebral malaria
  • Test glucose and malaria in every fit; unsure: MgSO₄ + IV artesunate
  • Maternal: eclampsia, intracranial haemorrhage (leading cause of death), HELLP, DIC, AKI, pulmonary oedema, abruption, PPH
  • Fetal: FGR, oligohydramnios, prematurity (mostly iatrogenic), stillbirth
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Fit, fever 39 °C, BP normal → Cerebral malaria
6Follow-up and antihypertensives
  • Mild: clinic, BP every 2 wk
  • Moderate: clinic or admit, BP 2–3 times a week, bloods weekly
  • Severe: admit, HDU, BP every 15 min
  • Labetalol first (200 mg 8-hourly; IV when severe; not in asthma)
  • Nifedipine 20 mg twice daily (max 60 mg/day)
  • Methyldopa 250 mg 8-hourly (change after birth: depression)
  • Severe: hydralazine 5 mg IV over 10–15 min every 15 min, with 500 mL saline first
  • Never ACE inhibitors, ARBs or diuretics
  • Target 135/85 (NICE)
