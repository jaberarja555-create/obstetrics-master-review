Inversion with placenta still attached. Remove it first?
No: replace the uterus, then remove it.
10. Management: methods of replacement
Johnson manoeuvre (manual)Palm on the fundus, fingers at the ring. Push up through the cervix toward the umbilicus; the part that came out last goes in first. Hold 3–5 min
Relax the ringterbutaline 0.25 mg SC or MgSO₄ 4 g IV over 15 min · or GA with a volatile agent (halothane)
O'Sullivan (hydrostatic)Warm saline run fast into the vagina, 2–5 L, labia sealed by hand or a silicone ventouse cup. Water pressure lifts the fundus. Exclude rupture first
Huntington (laparotomy)Clamps on the round ligaments inside the cup; pull up step by step, regrasping lower each time
Haultain (laparotomy)Ring too tight: posterior incision through the ring, replace, repair
Distinguishing features · Which method?
Inverted minutes ago, ring looseJohnson now
Ring tight, will not go upTerbutaline or GA
Manual fails, no ruptureO'Sullivan
Both fail, ring too tight at laparotomyHaultain incision
11. Management: after replacement
  • Stop tocolysis, start oxytocin 40 IU in 500 mL over 4 h; keep a fist inside the fundus until the uterus contracts
  • Then manual removal of the placenta in theatre · check cervix and vagina for tears
  • Antibiotics (manual handling, raw surface) · transfuse as needed
  • Watch closely for re-inversion and PPH · warn her: risk again next birth; deliver her next baby in hospital
12. Management: subacute and chronic inversion

Ring tight and uterus swollen: manual replacement rarely works. Treat shock and infection first, then replacement under GA, usually at laparotomy (Huntington, Haultain). Chronic cases: look for a fundal fibroid polyp pulling the fundus down.
