2.3Venous thromboembolism
Venous thromboembolism is DVT and its complication, pulmonary embolism. Pregnancy raises the risk about 5 times and the puerperium more; PE is a leading direct cause of maternal death, and most fatal PEs happen after birth.
1Risk factors
  • Pre-existing: previous VTE, thrombophilia, age over 35, BMI over 30, parity 3 or more, smoking, varicose veins, SLE, sickle cell
  • Obstetric: caesarean (above all in labour), pre-eclampsia, twins, prolonged labour, PPH, IUFD, IVF
  • Transient: immobility, surgery, hyperemesis, infection, long travel
  • Congenital thrombophilia: antithrombin III deficiency (highest risk), protein C or S deficiency, factor V Leiden (commonest)
  • Acquired: antiphospholipid syndrome
  • Left leg affected in about 75%: the right common iliac artery compresses the left iliac vein
2Clinical features
  • DVT: leg pain and swelling, usually left; calf more than 2 cm wider; tender, warm; iliofemoral clot may give abdominal or back pain
  • PE: breathlessness, pleuritic pain, haemoptysis, collapse; tachycardia, tachypnoea, low SpO₂
  • Massive: hypotension, raised JVP, cyanosis
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Both ankles swollen, painless, 36 wk → Physiological oedema
  • Day 5 after CS, breathless, pulse 120 → PE
