3. Measurement
Maternal mortality ratio (MMR)

MMR = maternal deaths ÷ live births × 100 000

  • Maternal mortality rate = maternal deaths per 100 000 women aged 15–49 per year (reflects fertility as well as risk).
  • Lifetime risk = chance that a 15-year-old girl will die of a maternal cause in her life (Sudan ≈ 1 in 91).
  • Sources: vital registration (best), hospital records, household surveys, sisterhood method (ask adults about their sisters), confidential enquiries. Deaths are often under-reported.

4. Morbidity and the near miss

Maternal morbidity = any ill health caused or made worse by pregnancy: for each death, many women live with fistula, prolapse, anaemia, infertility or depression. A maternal near miss = a woman who nearly died but survived a life-threatening complication in pregnancy, birth or within 42 days (e.g. shock, eclampsia, emergency hysterectomy). Near misses outnumber deaths and are reviewed to improve care.

Exam trap

The MMR denominator is live births, not total births or women; and it is a ratio, per 100 000, not per 1000 (perinatal mortality is per 1000).

6. Causes
Direct
  • Genital tract sepsis (often group A strep)
  • Haemorrhage: PPH, APH, rupture
  • Hypertensive disorders: stroke, liver, multi-organ failure
  • Unsafe abortion, ectopic
  • Obstructed labour → rupture, sepsis
  • VTE: PE, cerebral vein thrombosis
  • AFE · anaesthesia · acute fatty liver
Indirect
  • Cardiac disease: rheumatic, cardiomyopathy, MI, aortic dissection
  • Anaemia · malaria · HIV, TB, hepatitis E
  • Epilepsy, subarachnoid haemorrhage
  • Asthma · diabetes · sickle cell
  • Pancreatitis · malignancy
