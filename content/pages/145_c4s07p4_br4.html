13. Management: choosing the route
Criteria for assisted vaginal breech
  • Frank or complete breech, not footling
  • EFW 2.5–3.8 kg on scan
  • Flexed head, not hyperextended
  • No FGR, PE, diabetes, oligohydramnios · adequate pelvis
  • Trained staff, theatre, paediatrician
Elective CS at 39 wk
  • Footling or knee breech
  • EFW > 3.8 kg or < 2.5 kg
  • Hyperextended head ("star-gazing")
  • Other CS indication: praevia, scar, small pelvis
  • She prefers caesarean
  • First twin breech

Why CS today: lower risk for the baby (perinatal death ≈ 0.5% vs 2% after vaginal breech), at the cost of a higher risk for the mother now and a scar for the next pregnancy.

Breech found in labour
  • Scan if you can: type, EFW, head
  • Early labour, or criteria not met → caesarean
  • Advanced, buttocks low, criteria met → ABVD by a skilled person
Preterm breech
  • Small body slips through a cervix not fully dilated: head trapped
  • Caesarean often chosen; counsel
  • Steroids < 34 wk · MgSO₄ for neuroprotection

14. Mechanism of breech labour
  1. Buttocks engage with the bitrochanteric diameter (10 cm) in an oblique of the inlet, sacrum anterior (LSA).
  2. Anterior buttock meets the pelvic floor, turns 1/8 forward; lateral flexion of the trunk, anterior hip born first.
  3. Shoulders (bisacromial 12 cm) follow the same path; arms stay flexed on the chest if hands off.
  4. Head enters flexed, occiput turns under the symphysis; born by flexion: chin, face, then vertex. No time to mould.
