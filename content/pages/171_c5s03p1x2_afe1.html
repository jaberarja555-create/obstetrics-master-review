4. Clinical features
SystemWhat you see
WarningSudden breathlessness, chills, agitation, "something is wrong"
LungsSevere dyspnoea, cyanosis, hypoxia → respiratory arrest
HeartHypotension, shock, arrhythmia → cardiac arrest
BrainConvulsions, confusion, coma
FetusSudden bradycardia (if still undelivered)
Then: bloodBleeding from uterus, wound, cannula sites · blood will not clot = DIC
Definition in your exam

Labour or recently delivered + sudden hypoxia + hypotension/collapse + coagulopathy, with no other cause. Sometimes bleeding with DIC is the first sign.

Clinical picture
Classic AFE
Labour / ROM+sudden hypoxia+collapse+then bleeding=AFE
5. Investigations

Send them while resuscitating. They support the picture and exclude look-alikes; none proves AFE.

TestFinding in AFE
ABGHypoxia, metabolic acidosis
FBCWBC ↑ · platelets ↓ · Hb falls with bleeding
ClottingPT, aPTT ↑ · fibrinogen ↓ · D-dimer ↑ · clot test: no clot
U&E, Ca, MgBaseline for kidneys; Ca and Mg affect the heart
Group, cross-match4–6 units; FFP, cryo, platelets ready
CXRPulmonary oedema (bilateral shadows)
ECG · echoIschaemia, right heart strain · dilated right ventricle
6. Diagnosis
Clinical, by exclusion
  • In life: clinical; no test proves it
  • Squames in PA catheter blood or sputum: supportive only
  • Definitive: squames, debris in lung vessels at post-mortem
  • Never wait for tests to treat
