2.6Malaria in pregnancy
Malaria in pregnancy is mostly P. falciparum. Reduced immunity and parasites that bind the placenta make it more severe: it causes anaemia, hypoglycaemia, pulmonary oedema, miscarriage, low birth weight and stillbirth.
1Types and severe features
  • Uncomplicated: symptoms + parasites, no severe feature, can take oral drugs
  • Severe: parasites + any severe feature
  • Placental (silent): parasites without fever; harms the fetus; the target of IPTp
  • Severe features: impaired consciousness, repeated fits, Hb below 7, glucose below 40 mg/dL, temperature over 40 °C, parasitaemia over 5%, jaundice, blackwater urine, pulmonary oedema, renal failure, shock
🩺 Exam scenario
  • No fever, anaemia, small baby → Placental malaria
2Risk factors and pathophysiology
  • Primigravidae (no immunity to placenta-binding parasites), young mothers, non-immune women, HIV, no net or IPTp
  • Sticky infected red cells block small vessels and bind placental CSA
  • Pregnancy adds hypoglycaemia (parasites use glucose; quinine releases insulin) and pulmonary oedema
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Check blood glucose in every case; do not flood her with fluids
3Clinical features and investigations
  • Fever, rigors, sweats, headache, vomiting; can mimic labour pains
  • Pallor, splenomegaly, mild jaundice
  • Many have no fever: anaemia or a small baby may be the only clue
  • Fever in pregnancy in an endemic area: test for malaria first
