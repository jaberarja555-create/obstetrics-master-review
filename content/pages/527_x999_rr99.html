🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Day 4, cries easily, feeds the baby well → Baby blues: reassure
  • Day 5, no sleep, says the baby is evil → Puerperal psychosis
2Aetiology
  • Sudden fall of oestrogen and progesterone after the placenta, plus sleep loss, pain and stress
  • Psychosis is mostly a bipolar episode triggered by delivery
3Baby blues
  • Up to 80%
  • Explain, rest, family support
  • Review if it lasts > 2 weeks: severe or long blues is a risk factor for depression
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Do not start an antidepressant for blues on day 4; still present at 3 weeks → screen for depression
4Physical illness that mimics a mood disorder
  • Sepsis delirium and malaria
  • Eclampsia, stroke
  • Cerebral vein thrombosis (headache, fits, normal BP)
  • Anaemia
  • Postpartum thyroiditis (hypo phase at 4–8 months)
  • Sheehan syndrome
  • Drugs, glucose, sodium
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Day 5, confused, temperature 39 °C → Exclude sepsis and malaria first
  • 5 months, low mood, cold, weight gain → Postpartum thyroiditis
5Postnatal depression
  • Within the first year, usually by 3 months
  • Risk factors: past depression or PND, depression in pregnancy, severe blues, poor support, marital conflict, sick baby or stillbirth
  • Features ≥ 2 weeks: low mood, anhedonia, insomnia even when the baby sleeps, guilt ("bad mother"), suicidal thoughts; mothers hide it
