3.6Intrauterine fetal death
Intrauterine fetal death is death of the fetus in the uterus after the limit of viability: WHO ≥ 28 wk or ≥ 1000 g (UK stillbirth from 24 wk). Earlier loss is managed as a miscarriage. About half have no cause found.
1Causes
  • Unexplained: about 50%, the commonest single group
  • Maternal: pre-eclampsia, diabetes, infection (malaria, TORCH, syphilis, listeria), anaemia, thyroid disease, thrombophilia (APS), Rh isoimmunisation
  • Fetal: anomalies, FGR, TTTS, hydrops, feto-maternal haemorrhage
  • Placenta and cord: abruption, insufficiency, cord accident
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • IUFD with fever and rigors → Malaria: blood film
  • Recurrent IUFD with DVT and miscarriages → Antiphospholipid syndrome
2Diagnosis
  • Reduced or absent movements; breasts soften
  • Uterus small for dates, SFH stops rising
  • Real-time ultrasound with no heart motion is diagnostic (second operator if possible)
  • Later signs: Robert sign (gas in the fetal heart, about 12 h, earliest X-ray sign), Spalding sign (overlapping skull bones, about 7 days), maceration
⚠️ Exam trap
  • No heart on a Pinard or hand Doppler does not prove death: only a real-time scan confirms
3Investigations for the cause
  • Mother now: FBC, clotting + fibrinogen, urine protein, LFT, RFT, glucose/OGTT, malaria film, TORCH, syphilis, blood group (Rh-negative: Kleihauer), TFT if symptoms
  • At 6 wk: thrombophilia, APS
  • Fetus: detailed scan; karyotype
  • After birth: autopsy and placental histology
