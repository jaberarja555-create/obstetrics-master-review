2.6Malaria in Pregnancy

1. Definition & classification

Plasmodium infection in a pregnant woman, proven by blood film or RDT; in Sudan mostly P. falciparum.

TypeMeaning
UncomplicatedSymptoms + parasites, no severe feature, can take oral drugs
SevereParasites + ≥ 1 severe feature (below): high mortality in pregnancy
Placental / silentParasites without fever: harms the fetus quietly; target of IPTp

Severe malaria: any one of

  • Cerebral: impaired consciousness
  • Repeated convulsions
  • Anaemia: Hb < 7 g/dL
  • Hypoglycaemia: RBG < 40 mg/dL
  • Hyperpyrexia: > 40 °C
  • Parasitaemia > 5% of red cells
  • Jaundice · blackwater urine
  • Pulmonary oedema
  • Renal failure: oliguria, ↑ creatinine
  • Algid: shock, systolic < 90
2. Risk factors
  • Primigravida (no immunity yet to placenta-binding parasites) · young mother
  • Non-immune: from a malaria-free area · HIV · no bed net, no IPTp
3. Pathophysiology
THE RULE · Sticky infected red cells block small vessels; in pregnancy they also bind placental CSA.
Exam trap

Pregnancy adds two dangerous complications: hypoglycaemia (parasites eat glucose, insulin response rises, quinine releases insulin) and pulmonary oedema. Check RBG in every case; do not flood her with fluids.
