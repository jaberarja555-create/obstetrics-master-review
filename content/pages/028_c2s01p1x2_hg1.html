4. Clinical features

History: vomiting through the day, cannot keep food or fluid down, weight loss, weakness, vomiting blood (Mallory-Weiss tear). Examination: dehydration (dry tongue, ↓ skin turgor, tachycardia, low BP), weight loss, muscle wasting.

Severity (PUQE score): hours of nausea + episodes of vomiting + retching per day, each scored 1–5.

Mild · ≤ 6Diet, oral drugs
Moderate · 7–11Oral or day-case IV
Severe · ≥ 12Admit
5. Investigations
UrineKetones (dipstick) · MSU to exclude UTI
BloodsFBC (↑ Hct) · U&E (↓ Na⁺, ↓ K⁺, ↓ Cl⁻, ↑ urea) · LFT (↑ transaminases, ↓ albumin) · TFT · glucose
UltrasoundConfirms viability; excludes twins and molar pregnancy
6. Differential diagnosis

UTI / pyelonephritis · gastroenteritis · hepatitis · peptic ulcer · thyrotoxicosis · molar pregnancy · DKA · raised ICP · red degeneration of fibroid · acute abdomen. Onset after 12 wk, pain, fever or headache = not HG.

7. Complications
MaternalWernicke encephalopathy (B1 lack: confusion, ataxia, eye signs) · hyponatraemia (corrected too fast → central pontine myelinolysis) · hypokalaemia · hypochloraemic alkalosis · Mallory-Weiss tear · VTE (dehydration + immobility) · liver and renal failure · malnutrition, depression
FetalLow birth weight / IUGR · fetal death if Wernicke develops
Best next step · low TSH in HG

Low TSH + high free T4 is usually gestational transient thyrotoxicosis: hCG stimulates the TSH receptor. No goitre, no eye signs. No antithyroid drugs; it settles as hCG falls (by about 20 wk).
