6Antiemetic ladder
  • 1st: promethazine 25 mg or cyclizine 50 mg 8-hourly
  • 2nd: metoclopramide 10 mg (no longer than 5 days: dystonia) or domperidone
  • 3rd: ondansetron 4–8 mg
  • Then hydrocortisone 100 mg IV 12-hourly → oral prednisolone
  • Then TPN
  • Termination: last resort when life is at risk
  • Climb one step only when the step below fails
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Fails three antiemetic lines → Hydrocortisone
2.2Anaemia in pregnancy
Anaemia is the commonest medical problem of pregnancy; iron deficiency causes 80–90%. It is Hb below 11 g/dL in the first trimester, below 10.5 in the second and third, and below 10 after birth. It makes haemorrhage, infection and heart failure more dangerous.
1Grades and physiological anaemia
  • Mild 10–10.9
  • Moderate 7–9.9
  • Severe below 7
  • Very severe below 4 g/dL
  • Physiological anaemia: plasma rises faster than red cells; Hb 10.5–11 in the second trimester with normal MCV and ferritin: no treatment
  • Iron need about 1000 mg; folate need doubles
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Hb 10.6 at 24 wk, MCV normal, well → Physiological: reassure
2Causes and risk factors
  • Deficiency: iron (commonest), folate (megaloblastic), B12 (rare)
  • Haemolytic: sickle cell, thalassaemia, G6PD, malaria
  • Blood loss: APH, miscarriage, hookworm, schistosomiasis
  • Chronic disease: TB, HIV, renal disease
  • Risk: poor diet, pica, tea with meals, high parity, short interval, twins, teenage pregnancy, anti-epileptics
