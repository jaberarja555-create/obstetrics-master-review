7. Differential diagnosis · jaundice in pregnancy

Specific to pregnancy (about 10%): hyperemesis · obstetric cholestasis · PE / HELLP · AFLP. Not specific: viral hepatitis (A, B, C, E, EBV, CMV) · gallstones · haemolysis (malaria, sickle cell) · Gilbert's · autoimmune hepatitis · cirrhosis · drugs · malignancy.

TIMING · The trimester is the first clue: hyperemesis early, cholestasis, HELLP and AFLP late; hepatitis at any time.

Exam trap

Hepatitis E is mild in non-pregnant adults but causes fulminant liver failure in the 3rd trimester (death up to 20%). Consider it in jaundice outbreaks (unsafe water).
