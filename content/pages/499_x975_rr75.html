7Failed induction and previous caesarean
  • Failed: no labour after a full course (2 prostins or Foley, then ARM + oxytocin 6–12 h) → rest and second course, or CS
  • One lower-segment CS: hospital with theatre; Foley + ARM + oxytocin; prostin lowest dose; no misoprostol with a live fetus; continuous CTG
  • Rupture risk about 0.5% in spontaneous labour, 2–3 times higher if induced
  • Classical scar, 2 or more CS, previous rupture: elective CS
🩺 Exam scenario
  • One previous CS, 41+3 wk, Bishop 3 → Foley balloon, then ARM + oxytocin
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Scar rupture often shows first as fetal bradycardia, not pain
4.7Breech presentation
Breech is a longitudinal lie with the buttocks or feet presenting and the head in the fundus; the denominator is the sacrum. It is the commonest malpresentation: 25% before 28 wk but 3–4% at term. The danger is the aftercoming head.
1Definitions
  • Lie: long axis (longitudinal, transverse, oblique)
  • Presentation: lowest part
  • Attitude: flexion or extension
  • Denominator: occiput, sacrum, mentum, acromion
  • Malpresentation: not vertex (breech, face, brow, shoulder)
  • Malposition: vertex with the occiput not anterior (OT, OP)
⚠️ Exam trap
  • OP is a malposition, not a malpresentation
2Types and causes
  • Frank 65% (hips flexed, knees extended; commonest in primigravidae)
  • Complete 25% (hips and knees flexed)
  • Footling 10% (highest cord prolapse risk)
  • Causes: idiopathic; prematurity (commonest association); uterine anomaly, fibroid; poly- or oligohydramnios; praevia, cornual placenta; twins, hydrocephalus, anencephaly; contracted pelvis; lax multiparous uterus
