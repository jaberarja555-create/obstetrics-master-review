2.8Epilepsy in Pregnancy

1. Definition

Recurrent unprovoked seizures; the commonest neurological disorder in pregnancy. Most women have normal pregnancies: the risks come from fits and drugs.

2. Effect of pregnancy on epilepsy

Most women: no change (about two thirds). About one third fit more, mostly those with poor control before pregnancy. Why: vomiting and missed doses (fear for the baby), poor sleep, and falling drug levels (bigger blood volume, faster clearance: lamotrigine most). Fits are commonest in labour and the first days after birth.

3. Differential diagnosis of fits in pregnancy
Eclampsia> 20 wk or ≤ 48 h after birth · high BP + proteinuria
Cerebral malariaFever, coma, parasites on film or RDT · BP normal
HypoglycaemiaInsulin, vomiting, sepsis · check glucose in every fit
OthersMeningitis · cerebral venous thrombosis · stroke · low sodium · pseudoseizure
4. Complications: effect of epilepsy on pregnancy
MaternalInjury, aspiration pneumonia during a fit · pre-eclampsia · APH, PPH · anaemia (folate) · postnatal depression · status epilepticus, sudden death (SUDEP)
FetalMiscarriage · congenital anomalies (drugs) · growth restriction, low birth weight · preterm birth · fetal hypoxia in long fits · stillbirth
5. Antiepileptic drugs and anomalies
MAJOR ANOMALY RISK · Risk rises with the dose and the number of drugs: on average about 6–8% with one drug and 8–16% with polytherapy.
