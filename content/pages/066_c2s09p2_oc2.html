8. Management · antenatal
  1. Confirm and exclude. Bile acids, LFT, clotting; rule out other liver disease and PE. Book delivery in a hospital with a neonatal unit.
  2. Ursodeoxycholic acid 8–12 mg/kg/day in 2 doses. Itch eases in 1–7 days; LFT improve. No proven benefit to the fetus.
  3. Water-soluble vitamin K (menadiol) 10 mg oral daily from diagnosis: protects against PPH.
  4. Itch relief: emollients, e.g. aqueous cream with menthol. Chlorphenamine only sedates (helps sleep, not itch).
  5. Monitor: LFT and bile acids weekly (the peak level sets delivery timing); fetal movements; growth and liquor as indicated.
Clinical picture
Treatment
Ursodeoxycholic acid+vitamin K+planned delivery=OC care
9. Management · intrapartum

Timing depends on the peak bile acid level (µmol/L). Why deliver early? Stillbirth risk rises in the last weeks and cannot be predicted by CTG.

Mild · 10–39Deliver at 39 wk
Moderate · 40–99Deliver at 38 wk
Severe · ≥ 100Deliver at 35–36 wk
  • Induction of labour; caesarean only for the usual obstetric reasons.
  • Steroids if birth is planned before 35 wk.
  • Labour: continuous CTG, watch for meconium, neonatal team aware.
  • Active 3rd stage with oxytocin: PPH risk from low vitamin K.
10. Management · postpartum
Resolution

Itch goes within days. Check LFT before discharge or at 6 wk. Still abnormal → look for chronic liver disease. Baby: routine vitamin K at birth.

Recurrence

Comes back in 45–70% of later pregnancies. Avoid oestrogen pills: they can bring the itch back. Use progestogen-only or IUCD. Test LFT early next time.
