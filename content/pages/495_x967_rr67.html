3Complications of prematurity
  • RDS (no surfactant): the commonest neonatal problem
  • Apnoea, chronic lung disease, PDA
  • IVH, periventricular leukomalacia → cerebral palsy
  • NEC, feeding problems, sepsis
  • Hypothermia, hypoglycaemia, jaundice
  • Retinopathy of prematurity, deafness
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Preterm baby with bloody stools and distension → NEC
4Management by gestation
  • Admit, confirm gestation, assess the fetus, tell parents and the neonatal team
  • Under 24 wk: counsel, comfort care, no steroids or tocolysis
  • 24–33⁺⁶ wk: steroids, nifedipine 48 h, GBS cover, MgSO₄ under 32 wk, transfer to a unit with NICU
  • 34 wk or more: no tocolysis, no steroids, let labour go on
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Preterm labour at 30 wk with fever and a tender uterus → No tocolysis: deliver
  • Preterm labour at 35 wk → No tocolysis, no steroids
5Corticosteroids and tocolysis
  • Steroids 24–34 wk: dexamethasone 6 mg IM 12-hourly × 4 or betamethasone 12 mg IM × 2, 24 h apart
  • Work after 24 h, best at 2–7 days; reduce RDS, IVH, NEC, death
  • One course; raise insulin in diabetics; double the dose with enzyme inducers
  • Tocolysis 24–34 wk for 48 h only: buys time for steroids and transfer
  • Nifedipine first line (30 mg oral, then 20 mg 8-hourly; never sublingual)
  • Atosiban: fewest side effects, costly
  • Indomethacin under 32 wk only (ductus closure, oligohydramnios)
  • β-agonists: tachycardia, pulmonary oedema; not with heart disease or diabetes
  • Never two tocolytics
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Preterm labour with mitral stenosis → Nifedipine, not salbutamol
