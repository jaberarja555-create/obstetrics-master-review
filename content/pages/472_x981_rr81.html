4Clinical features and diagnosis
  • Labour going well, membranes rupture (or ARM), then sudden fetal bradycardia or variable decelerations
  • Mother well, no bleeding, clear liquor
  • VE at once after any ROM followed by an abnormal FHR
  • Pulsating cord = fetus alive
  • Still cord: check the heart by Doppler or bedside scan (may be spasm)
  • Diagnosis is clinical: no other test
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • ARM at 5 cm, FHR falls to 80, loop of cord in the vagina → Cord prolapse: call help, push the head up
  • Mother well, fresh bleed after ROM, FHR 70 → Vasa praevia
5Management: the drill
  • Call help, note the time; stop oxytocin
  • Hands off the cord: do not push it back; if outside, tuck it gently into the vagina
  • Elevate the presenting part with two fingers and keep it up until delivery
  • Long transfer: fill the bladder with 500 mL warm saline, clamp; empty it before the uterine incision
  • Position: knee-chest, or exaggerated left Sims for transfer
  • Terbutaline 0.25 mg SC stops contractions
⚠️ Exam traps
  • Never try to replace the cord in the uterus
  • In a moving ambulance use exaggerated left Sims, not knee-chest
6Route of delivery
  • Alive, 1st stage: Category 1 CS, decision to delivery ≤ 30 min (GA, or spinal if quick)
  • Alive, fully dilated, head low: forceps or ventouse in the room
  • Fully dilated, head high or transverse lie: caesarean
  • Second twin: breech extraction or ventouse
  • Dead fetus: vaginal, no CS for the baby
  • Before 24 wk (limit of viability): counsel; managed as a miscarriage (RCOG; local units: 26 wk)
