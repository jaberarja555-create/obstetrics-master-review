  • Incomplete = 1st, 2nd
  • Complete = 3rd, 4th
  • Acute < 24 h (most)
  • Subacute 24 h – 4 wk
  • Chronic > 4 wk (often a fundal fibroid polyp)
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Fundus past the cervix but not the vulva → Second degree (incomplete)
2Aetiology
  • Mismanaged 3rd stage: cord traction before separation, no counter-traction
  • Fundal pressure (Credé) on a relaxed uterus
  • Fundal placenta, short cord
  • Atony
  • Placenta accreta, fundal fibroid
  • Uterine anomaly, previous inversion
3Pathophysiology
  • Relaxed fundus (placental site) dimples in
  • Cord traction or fundal push pulls it down
  • Cervix closes as a constriction ring; the fundus swells
  • Traction on ovaries, tubes and peritoneum: vagal (neurogenic) shock with bradycardia, plus bleeding
4Clinical features
  • Severe pain in the 3rd stage
  • PPH in about 94%
  • Shock out of proportion to the blood loss, slow pulse
  • Abdomen: fundus absent or a cup at the top
  • VE: purple-red mass in the vagina or at the vulva, placenta may be on it
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Cord pulled hard, pulse 52, BP 70/40, little bleeding, red mass at the vulva → Acute inversion, vagal shock
