7.1The OSCE Station

1. What an OSCE is

Objective structured clinical examination: a circuit of short stations. Every student gets the same task, the same patient or model and the same checklist. The examiner watches and ticks. Marks = checklist steps + a global score (attitude, communication, safety).

2. Station types in the obstetrics OSCE
StationWhat you doMarks hinge on
HistoryFocused history: HTN, bleeding, fever, anaemia, diabetes (7.2)Red flags asked, summary, likely diagnosis
ExaminationAbdomen, BP, urine: patient or model (7.3, 7.4)Order, technique, talking to her
ProcedureOn a model: speculum, VE, delivery, episiotomy, instrumental (7.5–7.8)Asepsis, correct steps, safety
DataPartogram, CTG, FBC, OGTT, urine (7.6)Describe first, then conclude and act
SpotterName an instrument, drug or image (7.10)Name → use → one viva fact
CounsellingExplain a diagnosis, options, a plan (7.11)Empathy, no jargon, check understanding
Emergency drillPPH, eclampsia, shoulder dystocia, cord prolapse (7.9)Call for help first, right order, doses
Distinguishing features · The verb decides
"Take a focused history"History only, no examination
"Examine the abdomen"Explain as you go, present findings
"Counsel her"Explain + options, not a history
"Interpret this chart"Describe, then conclude, then act
"Demonstrate on the model"Treat the model as a woman
3. The 5-minute plan
Viva questions
Task: "Take a history from this woman with bleeding at 34 wk." Do you do a PV?
No: history only. Say you would avoid PV until praevia is excluded.
The bell rings at 4 min and you are not done. What now?
Stop, summarise to the patient, thank her, close.
