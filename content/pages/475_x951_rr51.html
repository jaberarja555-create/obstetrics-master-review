3.4Multiple pregnancy
Multiple pregnancy is more than one fetus in the uterus; twins occur naturally in about 1 in 80 (Hellin's law: triplets 1 in 80²). Chorionicity, not zygosity, decides the risk, and prematurity is the main cause of death.
1Types and chorionicity
  • Dizygotic (about two thirds): 2 ova + 2 sperm, either sex, always DCDA
  • Monozygotic (one third, constant): split day decides the type
  • Split by day 3: DCDA; days 4–8: MCDA (commonest MZ); days 8–13: MCMA; after day 13: conjoined
  • Risk factors (DZ): IVF, clomiphene, previous twins, family history, age and parity, African race
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Twins of different sex → Dizygotic, DCDA
2Diagnosis
  • Severe hyperemesis, uterus large for dates, many fetal parts, two hearts
  • Ultrasound: number, viability; chorionicity at 11–13⁺⁶ wk; if late and unclear, treat as MC
  • Lambda (twin-peak) sign → DC; T sign → MC; two placentas or two sexes → DC; no membrane → MCMA
  • Large for dates: wrong dates, polyhydramnios, macrosomia, fibroids, ovarian mass, mole
🩺 Exam scenario
  • One placenta, thick membrane with a triangular wedge at 12 wk → Dichorionic (lambda sign)
3Complications
  • Maternal: hyperemesis, anaemia, pre-eclampsia (about 5 times), GDM, preterm labour, polyhydramnios, praevia, APH, malpresentation, PPH, operative birth
  • Fetal: miscarriage, vanishing twin, anomalies (twice in MC), prematurity, FGR (25%), discordance, stillbirth, cerebral palsy
  • Labour: malpresentation, cord prolapse, hypoxia of twin 2, locked twins
