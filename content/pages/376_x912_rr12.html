🩺 Exam scenario
  • 10 wk, ketones 3+, Na 128, K 2.9 → Hypochloraemic hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis
4Complications
  • Wernicke encephalopathy (thiamine lack: confusion, ataxia, nystagmus)
  • Hyponatraemia; corrected too fast → central pontine myelinolysis
  • Hypokalaemia, Mallory–Weiss tear
  • VTE (dehydration, immobility)
  • Liver and renal failure, malnutrition, depression
  • Fetus: low birth weight; death if Wernicke develops
  • Low TSH with high free T4 and no goitre = gestational transient thyrotoxicosis: no antithyroid drugs; settles by about 20 wk
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • On IV dextrose, now confused and ataxic → Wernicke: IV thiamine
  • Swollen calf on day 4 of admission → DVT: Doppler + LMWH
5Management
  • Mild: small dry low-fat meals, reassure (settles by 12–16 wk), oral antiemetic
  • Admit if she cannot keep down fluid or drugs, ketonuria, weight loss, low K⁺ or Na⁺, or coexisting illness
  • IV 0.9% saline (or Hartmann's) + KCl by daily U&E
  • Thiamine 100 mg IV before any dextrose, then oral; add pyridoxine
  • No hypotonic fluids (worsen low Na⁺)
  • LMWH and stockings while admitted
  • Nil by mouth 24–48 h if vomiting everything; fluid chart, weight, U&E, ketones; psychological support
⚠️ Exam traps
  • No dextrose before thiamine: it can precipitate Wernicke
  • Raise sodium by no more than 10 mmol/L in 24 h
