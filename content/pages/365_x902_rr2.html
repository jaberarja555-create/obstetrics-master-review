3Haematological changes
  • Plasma volume +45%, red cell mass +25%: Hb about 11 g/dL with normal MCV = physiological anaemia
  • Iron need about 1000 mg
  • WBC up to 15 × 10⁹/L (higher in labour); platelets slightly lower
  • ESR normally high: not useful
  • Hypercoagulable: fibrinogen and factors VII, VIII, X rise; protein S falls; less fibrinolysis
  • VTE risk 4–6 times, highest after delivery
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Hb 11.2 g/dL at 30 wk, MCV 88 fL → Haemodilution: physiological anaemia
4Respiratory changes
  • Progesterone drives breathing: tidal volume +40%, minute ventilation +40–50%, respiratory rate unchanged
  • PCO₂ falls to about 30 mmHg (4 kPa): compensated respiratory alkalosis
  • Oxygen use +20%, FRC −20%: quick hypoxia at induction, so pre-oxygenate
  • Physiological breathlessness in up to 70%: at rest or talking, normal SpO₂
  • Breathless on mild effort, at night or with low SpO₂: look for anaemia, heart disease, PE
⚠️ Exam traps
  • A fast respiratory rate is a sign of illness, not of pregnancy
  • A 'normal' pCO₂ of 40 mmHg in an asthmatic pregnant woman means she is tiring
5Renal and urinary tract
  • Renal plasma flow +50–75%, GFR +50%: creatinine, urea and uric acid fall
  • Creatinine above 75 µmol/L suggests kidney disease
  • Glycosuria is common: confirm diabetes with a blood test (OGTT)
  • Protein up to 300 mg/24 h is normal
  • Dilated ureters, right more than left: asymptomatic bacteriuria → pyelonephritis in 20–30% if untreated: screen all women
  • Frequency early and near term
