11. Antibiotics
SituationRegimen (all safe in breastfeeding)
Endometritis, sepsisceftriaxone 1–2 g IV daily + metronidazole 500 mg IV 8-hourly
Alternativeclindamycin 900 mg IV 8-hourly + gentamicin 5 mg/kg IV daily · or ampicillin 2 g IV 6-hourly + gentamicin + metronidazole
Severe sepsis, shockpiperacillin-tazobactam 4.5 g IV 8-hourly or meropenem 1 g IV 8-hourly + clindamycin (stops GAS toxin)
Mastitisflucloxacillin 500 mg orally 6-hourly (or cloxacillin) × 10–14 days · allergy: erythromycin · keep feeding
UTICystitis: cefalexin 500 mg 8-hourly × 7 days · pyelonephritis: ceftriaxone IV · adjust to culture
WoundFlucloxacillin ± metronidazole · open and drain pus

Give IV until fever-free for 48 h and improving; an oral course is not needed after uncomplicated endometritis. Avoid tetracyclines and chloramphenicol (breastfeeding). No response in 48–72 h → look for pus, retained tissue, pelvic vein thrombosis, wrong organism; ask the microbiologist.

12. Supportive and surgical care
  • IV fluids, oxygen, paracetamol, NSAID if no AKI
  • Enoxaparin 40 mg SC daily (sepsis = VTE risk)
  • Correct anaemia; blood if Hb low
  • Breastfeed or express; baby checked (GAS)
  • Ice packs: perineum, mastitis
  • Evacuate retained products gently, after antibiotics start
  • Drain pus: breast, wound, pelvic abscess (US-guided, colpotomy, laparotomy)
  • Debride necrotising fasciitis early
  • Hysterectomy if the uterus is necrotic or gas-forming and she is not improving
