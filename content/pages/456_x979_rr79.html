4Face and brow: management
  • Mento-anterior face: vaginal birth by flexion under the pubis; low forceps allowed
  • Mento-posterior: about two thirds rotate; persistent MP → CS
  • Face: no ventouse, scalp electrode or FBS
  • Persistent brow → CS; no forceps, ventouse or oxytocin
  • Facial bruising and oedema fade in days
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Face, chin at the sacrum, no progress → Caesarean
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Ventouse is never used on a face
5Transverse, oblique and unstable lie
  • Transverse lie presents by the shoulder (acromion); about 1 in 300 at term
  • Unstable lie keeps changing after 36 wk
  • Causes: multiparity with a lax uterus (commonest), polyhydramnios, prematurity, twins, praevia, pelvic tumour, uterine anomaly
  • Wide uterus, fundus below dates, empty pelvis, FHS near the umbilicus
  • Neglected shoulder: ROM → arm or cord prolapse → obstruction → rupture, death, fistula
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Wide uterus, fundus less than dates, empty pelvis → Transverse lie
6Abnormal lie: management
  • Admit from 37 wk so a CS is ready
  • ECV from 37 wk; stable transverse lie → elective CS at 39 wk
  • Early labour, membranes intact: ECV then ARM, otherwise CS
  • Neglected shoulder: caesarean even if the fetus is dead (vertical incision if needed)
  • Second twin transverse: ECV or internal podalic version + breech extraction
  • Compound presentation (hand by the head): leave it, it usually retracts; arrest or cord prolapse → CS
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Multipara at term in labour, transverse lie, ROM → Caesarean
  • Arm out, dead fetus, obstructed → Caesarean, not traction
