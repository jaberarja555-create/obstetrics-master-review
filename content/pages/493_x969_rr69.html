4.4Prelabour rupture of membranes
Prelabour rupture of membranes is rupture before contractions start. At term (PROM) it is physiological; before 37 wk (PPROM) it is pathological and causes about 40% of preterm births. The two dangers are infection and prematurity.
1Definitions and causes
  • PROM: at 37 wk or more, 8–10% of term births
  • PPROM: before 37 wk, about 2% of pregnancies
  • Prolonged ROM: over 24 h before birth
  • Causes: infection (BV, GBS, UTI, STIs), previous PPROM, twins, polyhydramnios, cervical weakness, amniocentesis, APH, smoking
2Diagnosis
  • Sudden gush, then a slow leak, worse on standing
  • Sterile speculum: pool in the posterior fornix or leak on coughing (after lying 20–30 min)
  • Nitrazine turns blue (false positive with blood, semen, BV)
  • Fern test on a dried slide
  • Ultrasound: low AFI supports
  • Differential: urine (acidic), discharge, show; liquor is alkaline (pH 7–7.5)
🩺 Exam scenario
  • 32 wk, gush of clear fluid, no pains → Sterile speculum; no digital VE
⚠️ Exam trap
  • No digital VE unless she is in labour: each VE carries bacteria up
3Complications
  • Mother: chorioamnionitis → sepsis, abruption, endometritis
  • Fetus: prematurity (leading cause of death), sepsis, cord prolapse, lung hypoplasia (ROM before 24 wk)
4Management of PPROM
  • Admit at least 48–72 h
  • Erythromycin 250 mg oral 6-hourly × 10 days (or until labour)
  • Steroids 24–34 wk
  • MgSO₄ under 32 wk if birth expected within 24 h
