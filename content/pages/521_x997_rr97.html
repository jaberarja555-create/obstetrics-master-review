9Sepsis Six: the first hour
  • Give: oxygen, IV fluids, IV antibiotics
  • Take: blood cultures, lactate, urine output
  • Each hour of delay raises the risk of death
  • Call help, MEOWS every 15–30 min; septic shock → ICU, vasopressors
10Antibiotics
  • Endometritis, sepsis: clindamycin 900 mg IV 8-hourly + gentamicin 5 mg/kg IV daily (WHO)
  • Alternatives: ceftriaxone + metronidazole; ampicillin + gentamicin + metronidazole (local units often start ceftriaxone + metronidazole)
  • Severe sepsis: piperacillin-tazobactam or meropenem + clindamycin (stops GAS toxin)
  • Mastitis: flucloxacillin; keep feeding
  • Pyelonephritis: ceftriaxone IV
  • IV until 48 h fever-free; no oral course after uncomplicated endometritis
  • Avoid tetracyclines and chloramphenicol
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Fever, foul lochia, tender uterus after CS → Clindamycin + gentamicin
  • GAS toxic shock → Add clindamycin
11Supportive and surgical care
  • Fluids, oxygen, enoxaparin 40 mg daily
  • Evacuate retained products after antibiotics start
  • Drain pus (breast, wound, pelvic abscess)
  • Debride necrotising fasciitis early
  • Hysterectomy if the uterus is necrotic
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Still febrile after 3 days of antibiotics, bulky uterus → Ultrasound for retained products; evacuate
