2Investigations and first hour
  • FBC, cross-match 4 units, clotting, fibrinogen, clot test (no clot in 7–10 min = low fibrinogen), creatinine, Kleihauer
  • CTG; ultrasound for placental site
  • Help, O₂, two cannulae, fluids, blood; O-negative if collapsed
  • Anti-D 500 IU within 72 h if RhD-negative + Kleihauer (1500 IU if none)
  • Peripheral unit: cannula, fluids, O₂, left tilt, transfer with a sample
🩺 Exam scenario
  • RhD-negative woman with APH at 32 wk → Anti-D 500 IU within 72 h + Kleihauer
3Praevia versus abruption
  • Praevia: painless, bright red, recurrent; soft non-tender uterus; shock matches the loss; high head or malpresentation; diagnosis by ultrasound
  • Abruption: constant pain, dark blood or none; hard woody uterus; shock out of proportion; fetal distress; diagnosis is clinical
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Painless bleed, soft uterus, breech → Placenta praevia
  • Pain, hard uterus, BP 160/110 → Abruption
4Placenta praevia
  • Placenta in the lower segment
  • RCOG terms: low-lying (edge under 20 mm from the os) and praevia (covers the os); older grading minor/major
  • Low at 20 wk → rescan at 32 wk (most move up)
  • Risks: previous CS, previous praevia, multiparity, age over 35, smoking, twins, IVF
  • Bleeds because the stretching lower segment shears the placenta; thin muscle has no living ligature
  • TVS is safe (probe in the fornix); previous CS: Doppler ± MRI for accreta
⚠️ Exam trap
  • A transvaginal scan is safe in praevia; the danger is a finger in the cervix
