4Monochorionic complications
  • TTTS (10–15% of MC): artery-to-vein links pump blood from donor to recipient; untreated mortality up to 80%
  • Donor: anaemic, oligohydramnios ('stuck twin'), FGR, small bladder
  • Recipient: polycythaemic, polyhydramnios, large bladder, heart failure, hydrops
  • Treatment: fetal medicine; fetoscopic laser under 26 wk
  • TAPS: Hb gap, liquor equal (MCA Doppler)
  • TRAP: acardiac twin fed by the pump twin
  • Discordance over 25% needs referral
🩺 Exam scenario
  • MCDA at 20 wk, deep pool 10 cm in one sac, other twin stuck → TTTS: fetoscopic laser
5Death of one twin
  • DC: early loss harmless (vanishing twin); later: preterm labour (90% deliver within 3 wk)
  • MC: the survivor bleeds into the dead twin; up to 25% die or have brain damage at that moment, so rushed delivery does not help
  • Weekly scans, check maternal clotting, aim for about 36 wk
🩺 Exam scenario
  • MC twins, one dies at 30 wk → Do not rush delivery
6Antenatal care
  • Team care, more visits
  • Iron and folic acid; CBC at booking and 28 wk
  • BP and urine every visit (moderate risk factor for PE)
  • Scans: chorionicity 11–13⁺⁶ wk, NT, anomaly scan
  • DC: growth scan every 4 wk from 20 wk
  • MC: every 2 wk from 16 wk (TTTS)
  • Steroids if preterm birth is likely; birth plan by 36 wk
7Timing and mode of birth
  • DCDA 37 wk
  • MCDA 36 wk (NICE NG137; local practice often 38 and 37 wk)
  • MCMA 32 wk by CS (cord entanglement)
  • Triplets 35 wk by CS
