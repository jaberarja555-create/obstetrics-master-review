2.8Epilepsy in pregnancy
Woman with epilepsy on valproate wants a pregnancy → Switch to lamotrigine/levetiracetam, folic acid 5 mg
Fit at 36 weeks with high BP and proteinuria → Eclampsia: MgSO₄, not antiepileptics
Fit lasting more than 5 minutes → Status epilepticus: lorazepam 4 mg IV
Effect of pregnancy
Most no change · a third fit more · fits commonest in labour and early postpartum
Teratogens
Valproate worst (≈ 10%, neural tube defect) · phenytoin (cleft, nail hypoplasia) · lamotrigine least · background 2–3%
Before conception
Fit-free 2 years ideal · single lowest-dose drug · folic acid 5 mg daily
Antenatal
Anomaly scan 18–20⁺⁶ wk · continue drugs (fits harm more than drugs) · drug levels
Labour and after
Avoid pethidine · baby: vitamin K 1 mg IM · breastfeeding allowed · reduce dose back by 10 d
Contraception
Enzyme inducers: copper IUD or DMPA
Exam traps
Never stop antiepileptics suddenly · fit + fever + normal BP = cerebral malaria · status: lorazepam then phenytoin 15–20 mg/kg
2.9Obstetric cholestasis
Itchy palms and soles at 32 weeks, no rash → Obstetric cholestasis: bile acids
Bile acids 120 µmol/L → Deliver at 35–36 weeks
Itch with normal tests → Repeat in 1–2 weeks
Key features
Itch of palms and soles, no rash, 3rd trimester, worse at night · family history in a third
Investigations
Serum bile acids > 10 µmol/L · ALT/AST 2–3× · exclude hepatitis, gallstones · ALP is not useful (placental)
Treatment
Ursodeoxycholic acid 8–12 mg/kg/day · antihistamines only sedate · vitamin K if steatorrhoea
Delivery
Bile acids ≥ 100: deliver 35–36 wk · 40–99: about 38 wk
Risks
Fetal: sudden stillbirth, meconium, preterm birth · maternal: vitamin K ↓ → PPH
Recurrence
45–70% next pregnancy · avoid oestrogen pills
Exam traps
Bile acids above 100 mark high stillbirth risk · itch with rash is another disease · UDCA relieves itch but its effect on stillbirth is unproved
