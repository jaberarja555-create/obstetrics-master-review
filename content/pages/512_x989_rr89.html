3Clinical features
  • Head delivers slowly; face and chin hard to deliver
  • Turtle sign: head retracts against the perineum
  • No restitution
  • Gentle axial traction fails
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Head out, retracts against the perineum, gentle traction fails → Shoulder dystocia: call help, McRoberts
4Complications
  • Mother: PPH (commonest)
  • 3rd/4th degree, cervical and vaginal tears
  • Uterine rupture (from fundal pressure)
  • Symphysis separation
  • Baby: Erb palsy C5–C6 (commonest; waiter's tip, grasp present; under 10% permanent)
  • Klumpke C8–T1 (claw hand ± Horner)
  • Fractured clavicle (no splint) or humerus
  • Hypoxia, HIE, death
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Limp arm, waiter's tip, grasp present → Erb palsy (C5–C6)
  • Claw hand, ptosis, small pupil → Klumpke + Horner
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Plexus injury comes from strong lateral traction, not the manoeuvres; it can follow a normal birth or even a caesarean
5Management: HELPERR
  • Say "shoulder dystocia", note the time, stop her pushing
  • Help: midwives, obstetrician, anaesthetist, paediatrician
  • Evaluate for episiotomy (room for internal manoeuvres only)
  • Legs: McRoberts (hips hyperflexed onto the abdomen)
