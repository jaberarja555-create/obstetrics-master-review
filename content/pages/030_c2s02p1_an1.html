2.2Anaemia in Pregnancy

1. Definition

Anaemia: Hb < 11 g/dL in the 1st trimester · < 10.5 in the 2nd and 3rd · < 10 after birth.

Mild10 – 10.9
Moderate7 – 9.9
Severe< 7 g/dL
Very severe< 4 g/dL
2. Physiological anaemia
HAEMODILUTION · Plasma rises faster than red cells: Hb 10.5–11 g/dL in the 2nd trimester, normal MCV and ferritin, no treatment. Iron need ≈ 1000 mg; folate need doubles.
Clinical picture
Physiological anaemia
Hb 10.5–11+2nd trimester+normal MCV=Dilution · no treatment
3. Classification and causes
DeficiencyIron (commonest, 80–90%) · folate (megaloblastic) · B12 (rare)
HaemolyticInherited: sickle cell, thalassaemia, G6PD · acquired: malaria
HaemorrhagicAPH, miscarriage · hookworm, schistosomiasis
Chronic diseaseTB, HIV, renal disease · marrow failure (rare)
Distinguishing features
Hb 10.6 at 24 wk, MCV normal, wellPhysiological: reassure
Pale, fever, splenomegalyMalaria
Pale, jaundice, from KordofanSickle cell disease
Pale, on phenytoin, large cellsFolate deficiency
4. Risk factors

Poor diet, pica, tea with meals · high parity, short birth interval · twins · teenage pregnancy · worms, malaria · sickle cell, thalassaemia · anti-epileptics.
