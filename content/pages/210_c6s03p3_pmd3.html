Chapter 6 · 6.3 Postpartum Mood DisordersPostnatal Depression210
9. Definition

A depressive episode starting in the first year after birth, in 10–15% of mothers. Onset any time from the first days to months, usually within 3 months. It is a true depression that needs treatment, not a mild state that passes.

10. Risk factors
  • Past depression or previous postnatal depression
  • Depression or anxiety in pregnancy
  • Family history of depression
  • Severe or long baby blues
  • Young age · no partner, marital conflict
  • Low social support, poverty
  • Stressful events in pregnancy
  • Sick baby, stillbirth, unwanted pregnancy
11. Clinical features

Low mood most days for ≥ 2 weeks, loss of interest, crying, irritability, anxiety about the baby, insomnia even when the baby sleeps, exhaustion, anorexia, guilt, feeling a "bad mother", negative feelings to the infant, delayed attachment, suicidal thoughts. Mothers hide it; ask directly.

Clinical picture
Postnatal depression
> 2 wk low mood+anhedonia+guilt, poor bonding=PND
12. Investigations: screening

Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS): 10 questions, each scored 0–3 (max 30), done at booking and at the 6-week postnatal visit. Score ≥ 13 = probable depression → full assessment. Any score on question 10 (self-harm) → assess the same day. Check Hb and TSH: anaemia and thyroiditis mimic depression.

13. Effect on the infant

Poor bonding, insecure attachment, poor feeding and growth, slow milestones, later behaviour and aggression problems, more mental illness in the child.

14. Management
MildEmotional support from husband and family, sleep, practical help · CBT or counselling · review in 2–4 wk
Moderate–severeSSRI: sertraline 50 mg once daily, raise to 100–200 mg if needed (paroxetine is an alternative) · effect in 2–4 wk · continue 6 months after recovery · plus CBT
Suicidal, psychotic, not eatingUrgent psychiatry, admit · ECT if life-threatening
