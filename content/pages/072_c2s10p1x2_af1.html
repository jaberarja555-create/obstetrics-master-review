4. Clinical features
  • Days of nausea, vomiting, malaise, anorexia
  • Abdominal pain (epigastric / RUQ)
  • Jaundice · headache · fever
  • Polyuria, polydipsia (transient diabetes insipidus)
  • Confusion → coma (encephalopathy)
  • Mild hypertension ± proteinuria
Clinical picture
AFLP
3rd trimester vomiting+jaundice+glucose ↓ + INR ↑=AFLP
5. Investigations
GlucoseHypoglycaemia: check often, it can kill quietly
ClottingPT / INR ↑, fibrinogen ↓ (liver cannot make factors) → DIC
LFTBilirubin ↑ · ALT / AST moderately ↑ (usually < 1000)
U&E, urateMarked hyperuricaemia · creatinine ↑ (kidney failure)
FBC, filmWBC ↑ · platelets ↓ (milder than HELLP)
Gases, ammoniaMetabolic acidosis · ammonia ↑ · lactate ↑
UltrasoundBright fatty liver; mainly to exclude other causes. Biopsy rarely (clotting)

Swansea criteria: ≥ 6 of 14 typical clinical, blood and scan features (no other cause) = AFLP.

Distinguishing features
Jaundice + glucose 40 mg/dL + INR 2, 35 wkAFLP
RUQ pain + platelets 40, BP 170/110HELLP
Itch, well, ALT 2 × normalObstetric cholestasis
Vomiting + jaundice at 10 wkHyperemesis / hepatitis
6. Complications
MaternalFulminant liver failure · DIC, PPH · acute kidney injury · hypoglycaemic coma · pancreatitis · maternal death 18% (higher if diagnosis is late)
FetalFetal distress, fetal death 23% · prematurity · baby with LCHAD defect (hypoglycaemia, cardiomyopathy)
