5. Booking investigations

Routine investigations are offered to every woman at the booking visit. Their purpose is to detect conditions that cause no symptoms but can harm the mother or baby, such as anaemia, infections that pass to the newborn, and blood group incompatibility.

TestPurposeAction if abnormal
Complete blood count (CBC)To detect anaemia (haemoglobin below 11 g/dL) and a low platelet countRequest iron studies and a malaria film, and treat the cause (see 2.2). The haemoglobin is repeated at 28 weeks.
Blood group and Rhesus (Rh) factorTo identify RhD-negative women and red cell antibodiesAntibody screen at booking and at 28 weeks; plan anti-D prophylaxis.
Urine examinationProtein, glucose, and asymptomatic bacteriuria (by urine culture)Bacteriuria is treated, because this prevents pyelonephritis and preterm labour.
Blood glucoseTo detect pre-existing diabetes (fasting or random blood sugar)An oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) is done at 24–28 weeks in women with risk factors (at 16 weeks if she had gestational diabetes before).
TestPurposeAction if abnormal
HIV, hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) and syphilisTo detect infections that can pass to the babyHIV: antiretroviral therapy (see 2.12). Hepatitis B: vaccine and immunoglobulin for the newborn. Syphilis: benzathine penicillin.
UltrasoundDating, number of fetuses and viability; an anomaly scan at 18–22 weeksRefer fetal anomalies; plan care for twins (see 3.4).
Selected womenHaemoglobin electrophoresis (sickle cell disease), thyroid function, and a malaria film if she has feverSpecialist care.
6. Supplements and preventive drugs

Several simple and inexpensive drugs are given in pregnancy to prevent common complications. Some are given to every woman, and others only to women at increased risk.

DrugDose and timingIndication and purpose
Folic acid0.4 mg daily from before conception until 12 weeksGiven to all women to prevent neural tube defects (NTD). A higher dose of 5 mg is given to women with epilepsy, diabetes, a previous baby with an NTD, obesity or sickle cell disease.
Iron30–60 mg of elemental iron daily with folic acid, throughout pregnancyGiven to all women to prevent iron deficiency anaemia (see 2.2).
