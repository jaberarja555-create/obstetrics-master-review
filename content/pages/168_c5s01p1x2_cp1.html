4. Clinical features

Typical case: labour going well, membranes rupture (or ARM), then sudden fetal bradycardia or variable decelerations. Mother is well, no pain beyond labour, no bleeding, liquor clear.

Vaginal examination: cord felt in the vagina or seen at the vulva. Pulsating cord = fetus alive. Do a VE at once after any ROM followed by an abnormal FHR.

5. Investigations

Diagnosis is clinical. If the cord does not pulsate, check the heart by Doppler or bedside scan; a still cord may be in spasm, not dead. Do not wait for any other test.

6. Complications

Maternal: emergency caesarean and its risks · instrumental trauma · PPH · psychological trauma. Fetal: asphyxia, fresh stillbirth · hypoxic brain injury, cerebral palsy, periventricular leukomalacia · birth trauma.
