4.5Prolonged pregnancy
A prolonged (post-term) pregnancy reaches 42+0 wk (294 days) or more. The commonest reason a pregnancy only looks post-term is wrong dates. Risks rise after 41 wk, so every woman needs a plan before 42 wk.
1Definitions and dating
  • Post-date: past the EDD but under 42 wk
  • Postmaturity describes the baby, not the weeks
  • 5–10% reach 42 wk (under 5% with early scan dating)
  • Recurs in about 30%
  • CRL at 11–14 wk ± 5 days is the best dating; scan and LMP differ by over 5 days: use the scan
  • Never re-date from a late scan
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Booked at 30 wk, '42 wk' by LMP, irregular cycles → Confirm gestational age first
2Causes and pathophysiology
  • Usually unknown
  • Previous post-term pregnancy, primigravida, obesity, male fetus
  • Low fetal cortisol: anencephaly, adrenal hypoplasia, placental sulphatase deficiency
  • Failing placenta: fetal distress, oligohydramnios, cord compression, meconium
  • Good placenta: macrosomia, shoulder dystocia
3Clinical features and complications
  • Often none
  • Postmaturity syndrome (about 20%): long thin baby, peeling skin, long nails, no vernix, meconium staining
  • Fetal: stillbirth (intrapartum death 4 times after 42 wk), distress, oligohydramnios, meconium aspiration, macrosomia
  • Neonatal: hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, polycythaemia
  • Maternal: more induction, instrumental birth, CS, tears, PPH
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Peeling skin and long nails at 42 wk → Postmaturity syndrome; the same at 37 wk → FGR
