4Complications
  • Fulminant liver failure, DIC, PPH, AKI, hypoglycaemic coma, pancreatitis
  • Maternal death about 18%, fetal death about 23%
  • Baby with LCHAD defect: hypoglycaemia, cardiomyopathy
5Jaundice in pregnancy
  • Trimester is the first clue
  • Hyperemesis: early, vomiting, mild ALT rise
  • Cholestasis: itch, well, bile acids raised
  • HELLP: high BP, low platelets, LDH raised
  • AFLP: ill, vomiting, low glucose, raised INR
  • Hepatitis E: any trimester, ALT over 1000, IgM
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Hepatitis E is mild in non-pregnant adults but causes fulminant liver failure in the third trimester (death up to 20%)
6Management
  • HDU/ICU with a team; inform a liver unit
  • 10–50% dextrose, glucose every 1–2 h
  • Clotting: IV vitamin K 10 mg, FFP, cryoprecipitate, platelets before delivery
  • Strict fluid balance; treat hypertension
  • Deliver once stable, whatever the gestation (usually CS)
  • No regional block if platelets below 80 or clotting deranged
  • Most recover within days; may recur (test parents and baby for LCHAD)
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Ill, jaundiced, vomiting at 36 wk → Check glucose, INR, platelets and BP first
  • AFLP, INR 2.5, for caesarean → General anaesthesia
