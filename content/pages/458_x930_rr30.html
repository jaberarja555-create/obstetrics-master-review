8Peripartum cardiomyopathy
  • Heart failure from 32 wk of pregnancy to 6 months after birth, no other cause, EF below 45%
  • Risks: multiparity, twins, pre-eclampsia, age over 30, African women
  • Treat: furosemide, beta-blocker, hydralazine + nitrate (ACE inhibitor after birth), LMWH if EF below 35%
  • Half recover; recurs in the next pregnancy
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Breathless 2 months after birth, EF 30% → Peripartum cardiomyopathy
2.8Epilepsy in pregnancy
Epilepsy is the commonest neurological disorder in pregnancy. Most women have normal pregnancies; the risks come from fits and from antiepileptic drugs.
1Effect of pregnancy on epilepsy
  • About two thirds: no change
  • About one third fit more, mostly those poorly controlled before
  • Why: vomiting and missed doses, poor sleep, falling drug levels (lamotrigine most)
  • Fits are commonest in labour and the first days after birth
🩺 Exam scenario
  • More fits at 30 wk on lamotrigine → Levels fell: raise the dose
2Fits in pregnancy and complications
  • Differential: eclampsia (over 20 wk or within 48 h of birth, high BP, proteinuria), cerebral malaria, hypoglycaemia, meningitis, cerebral venous thrombosis, low sodium
  • Maternal: injury, aspiration, pre-eclampsia, anaemia (folate), depression, status epilepticus, SUDEP
  • Fetal: miscarriage, anomalies (drugs), FGR, preterm birth, hypoxia in long fits
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Known epileptic fits at 36 wk with BP 160/110 → Treat as eclampsia: MgSO₄
