10Stopping lactation
  • After stillbirth or neonatal death: supportive bra, cold packs, no expressing
  • Cabergoline 1 mg once within 24 h
  • Avoid in pre-eclampsia or hypertension
  • Bromocriptine no longer used
11Breastfeeding problems
  • Engorgement day 3–5, both breasts: feed often, express
  • Cracked nipples: correct the latch
  • Mastitis: red wedge, fever, week 2–3, S. aureus: keep feeding, flucloxacillin 500 mg 6-hourly × 10–14 d
  • Breast abscess: fluctuant mass: ultrasound, aspiration or I&D
12Postnatal contraception
  • LAM: no period + full breastfeeding + baby under 6 months → 98%
  • Progestogen methods (POP, implant, DMPA, LNG-IUS): first choice while breastfeeding
  • COC: from day 21 if not breastfeeding; after 6 months if breastfeeding
  • IUD: within 48 h or after 4 wk
  • Tubal ligation: at CS or within 7 days, else after 6 wk
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Breastfeeding mother at 6 wk asks for the combined pill → Avoid until 6 months; POP, implant or IUD
  • LAM user, baby 4 months, starts solids → Start another method
6.2Puerperal pyrexia & sepsis
Puerperal pyrexia = temperature ≥ 38 °C in the first 14 days after birth. Puerperal sepsis = genital tract infection from ROM or labour to day 42. Sepsis is the leading cause of maternal death in Sudan; infection is usually polymicrobial and ascends to the placental site.
1Definition
  • Puerperal morbidity: ≥ 38 °C on any 2 of the first 10 days, not counting the first 24 h
  • Puerperal sepsis (WHO): ≥ 2 of pelvic pain, fever ≥ 38.5 °C, abnormal discharge, foul smell, slow involution
  • Maternal sepsis (WHO): organ dysfunction from infection in pregnancy, birth, after abortion or to day 42
