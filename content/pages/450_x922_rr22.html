2.5Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy
Hypertension in pregnancy is BP ≥ 140/90 on two readings 4 h apart (one reading if ≥ 160/110). Pre-eclampsia is a placental disease of the whole body: new hypertension after 20 wk with proteinuria, or (current definition) with maternal organ damage or fetal growth restriction. Delivery is the cure.
1Classification
  • Chronic: before pregnancy or before 20 wk, still high at 12 wk after birth
  • Gestational (PIH): after 20 wk without protein; 1 in 4 progress to PE
  • Pre-eclampsia: after 20 wk + proteinuria (≥ 300 mg/24 h or PCR ≥ 30 mg/mmol)
  • Superimposed PE: chronic HTN with new protein or organ damage
  • Eclampsia: PE + fits; may be the first sign, even after birth
  • BP: mild 140/90–149/99, moderate 150/100–159/109, severe ≥ 160/110
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • BP 150/100 + proteinuria at 14 wk → Molar pregnancy
  • New HTN at 34 wk, urine clear → Gestational hypertension
2Risk factors and prevention
  • High risk (aspirin if one): previous PE, chronic HTN, kidney disease, diabetes, SLE or antiphospholipid
  • Moderate (aspirin if two): first pregnancy, age 40 or more, BMI over 30, family history, twins, interval over 10 years
  • Aspirin 75 mg at night, 12–36 wk
  • Calcium 1.5–2 g/day from 20 wk if dietary intake is low
3Clinical features and severe features
  • Often no symptoms: ask about headache, flashes, epigastric pain, vomiting
  • Sudden face and hand oedema, hyperreflexia, clonus, RUQ tenderness, small fundus
  • Severe PE (any one): BP ≥ 160/110
  • Severe headache, visual disturbance, clonus
  • Epigastric or RUQ pain, raised AST/ALT
  • Platelets below 100, haemolysis
  • Oliguria, raised creatinine
  • Pulmonary oedema
  • Severe FGR
