4.6Induction of Labour

1. Definition

Induction of labour (IOL) = starting labour artificially, before it begins on its own, aiming at vaginal birth. Not the same as augmentation, which speeds up a labour that has already started.

2. Indications
GroupIndications
ObstetricProlonged pregnancy (commonest) · PROM at term · pre-eclampsia (severe, after stabilising), eclampsia · unexplained APH at term · chorioamnionitis
FetalUteroplacental insufficiency: FGR, oligohydramnios, abnormal Dopplers, non-reassuring CTG · IUFD · Rh isoimmunisation
MedicalSevere hypertension · uncontrolled diabetes · renal disease with falling function · cholestasis
3. Contraindications
Absolute
  • Placenta praevia, vasa praevia
  • Transverse / oblique lie
  • Cord presentation or prolapse
  • CPD, contracted pelvis
  • Classical CS, ≥ 2 CS, previous rupture
  • Active genital herpes · VVF repair
Relative · with care
  • One previous lower-segment CS
  • Breech · high, free head
  • Grand multipara (rupture)
  • Polyhydramnios · twins
  • Heart disease (fluid load)
  • Asthma (prostaglandins)
4. Before you induce

Clear indication and consent · sure gestational age · lie and presentation, head in the pelvis · pelvis adequate · CTG normal · Bishop score. Success depends on gestational age, parity (multipara does better) and the Bishop score.
