4.8Malpresentations & malpositions
A malposition is a vertex with the occiput not anterior; a malpresentation is any presentation other than the vertex. The presenting diameter decides whether the head can pass: well-flexed vertex 9.5 cm, deflexed OP 11.5 cm, brow 13.5 cm, fully extended face 9.5 cm.
1Occipito-posterior position
  • Occiput posterior; about 25% in early labour, ROP commoner
  • Android or anthropoid pelvis, deflexed head, anterior placenta
  • Backache, long labour, early urge to push, early ROM, swollen anterior lip
  • Flat lower abdomen, limbs in front; anterior fontanelle felt in front on VE
  • Presenting diameter occipito-frontal 11.5 cm
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Primigravida, 6 cm, severe backache, wants to push early → Occipito-posterior
2OP: outcome and management
  • Long anterior rotation about 90%: delivers as OA
  • Short posterior rotation about 6%: face to pubis, born by flexion, episiotomy
  • About 4% no full rotation: deep transverse arrest (sagittal suture transverse at the spines, no descent) → manual rotation, Kielland or ventouse if skilled, else CS
  • Oxytocin only for weak contractions without CPD
  • Complications: prolonged labour, third-degree tear, PPH
3Face and brow
  • Face: fully extended, denominator mentum, about 1 in 500, submento-bregmatic 9.5 cm; feel mouth with gums, nose, malar bones
  • Brow: midway, about 1 in 2000, mento-vertical 13.5 cm; feel anterior fontanelle, orbital ridges, no chin
  • Causes: anencephaly, neck mass, cord coils, multiparity, contracted pelvis, polyhydramnios, prematurity
  • Head high with a deep groove between head and back
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Anterior fontanelle, orbital ridges, no chin → Brow: caesarean
