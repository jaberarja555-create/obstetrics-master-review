⚠️ Exam trap
  • One painful swollen leg in pregnancy = DVT until proven otherwise
3Investigations
  • Start treatment-dose LMWH first, then investigate (unless bleeding)
  • Baseline FBC, U&E, LFT, clotting
  • Compression duplex: negative but suspicion high → keep LMWH, repeat on day 4 and 7
  • Iliac DVT: Doppler of the iliac vein or MRI venography
  • D-dimer not useful (raised in normal pregnancy)
  • PE: ECG commonest sinus tachycardia (S1Q3T3 suggestive only); CXR often normal; ABG low O₂ and CO₂
  • Legs first: a DVT on duplex proves VTE
  • Normal CXR → V/Q scan; abnormal CXR → CTPA; fetal radiation risk almost nil
🩺 Exam scenario
  • Day 4 after CS, breathless, SpO₂ 91% → Treatment-dose LMWH before imaging
4Differential diagnosis and complications
  • DVT: cellulitis, ruptured Baker's cyst, muscle strain, physiological oedema
  • PE: pneumonia, pneumothorax, pulmonary oedema (peripartum cardiomyopathy), aortic dissection, amniotic fluid embolism
  • Complications: PE, recurrence, post-thrombotic syndrome, venous gangrene
  • Of treatment: bleeding, heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, osteoporosis
5Antenatal treatment
  • LMWH (does not cross the placenta): enoxaparin 1 mg/kg twice daily by booking weight (50–69 kg 60 mg, 70–89 kg 80 mg twice daily)
  • For the rest of pregnancy and at least 6 wk after birth (3 months in total)
  • Stockings, leg elevation, early mobilisation
  • LMWH needs no routine monitoring; UFH (APTT 1.5–2.5 times) for massive PE, high bleeding risk or near delivery
  • Reversal: protamine (complete for UFH, partial for LMWH)
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Warfarin crosses the placenta: embryopathy at 6–12 wk and fetal brain bleeds later; avoid in pregnancy, safe after birth
