Medical Disorders in Pregnancy
2.1Hyperemesis gravidarum
Hyperemesis gravidarum is vomiting through the day in early pregnancy, severe enough to cause dehydration, ketonuria, electrolyte upset and weight loss over 5%. Morning sickness (about half of women, 4–12 wk) and emesis gravidarum (vomiting but hydrated) are milder forms.
1Aetiology and risk factors
  • Cause unclear; main theory is high hCG (peaks at 9–12 wk, like the vomiting)
  • Multiple and molar pregnancy
  • Primigravida, previous HG
  • Thyrotoxicosis
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Vomiting at 9 wk, uterus large for dates → Molar pregnancy or twins: scan
  • Vomiting that starts at 24 wk → Not HG: find the cause
2Clinical features and severity
  • Vomiting all day, cannot keep food or fluid down, weight loss, weakness
  • Blood after retching: Mallory–Weiss tear
  • Dehydration: dry tongue, poor skin turgor, tachycardia, low BP
  • PUQE score (hours of nausea, vomits, retching): ≤ 6 diet and oral drugs; 7–11 oral or day-case IV; ≥ 12 admit
3Investigations
  • Urine ketones; MSU to exclude UTI
  • FBC (raised Hct), U&E (low Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, raised urea), LFT, TFT, glucose
  • Ultrasound: viability, twins, molar pregnancy
  • Acid–base: hypochloraemic hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis
  • Differential: pyelonephritis, gastroenteritis, hepatitis, peptic ulcer, thyrotoxicosis, DKA, raised ICP, red degeneration
  • Onset after 12 wk, pain, fever or headache = not HG
