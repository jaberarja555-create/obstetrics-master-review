4Oligohydramnios: definition and causes
  • AFI under 5 cm or DVP under 2 cm (5–8 borderline); anhydramnios = no pool
  • Fluid leaks out: ROM (commonest)
  • Less urine: FGR, placental insufficiency, post-term, renal agenesis (Potter)
  • Urine blocked: posterior urethral valves in boys
  • Drugs: NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, ARBs
  • Mnemonic DRIPP: Demise, Renal, IUGR, Post-term, PROM
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Boy, large bladder, keyhole sign → Posterior urethral valves
  • 42 wk, liquor low, CTG normal → Post-term: induce
5Oligohydramnios: features and investigations
  • Watery leak, reduced movements
  • Uterus small for dates, fetal parts easily felt, often breech
  • Sterile speculum (pool), FBC, CRP, HVS if ROM
  • Scan: AFI, growth, kidneys, bladder; UA Doppler if growth is poor
6Oligohydramnios: complications and management
  • From the cause: chorioamnionitis, preterm birth, stillbirth
  • From low volume: pulmonary hypoplasia (before about 22 wk), limb contractures, talipes, Potter facies, cord compression
  • ROM at or after 34–36 wk: induce
  • ROM before 34–36 wk: erythromycin 250 mg 6-hourly × 10 days, dexamethasone if under 34 wk, watch for infection
  • FGR: timing by Doppler and CTG
  • Renal anomaly: fetal medicine
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Breech at 37 wk, AFI 3 cm → ECV is usually not offered
  • Newborn with flat nose, low-set ears, talipes, respiratory failure → Potter sequence (renal agenesis)
⚠️ Exam trap
  • ACE inhibitors are banned in pregnancy: fetal renal failure, oligohydramnios, hypocalvaria
