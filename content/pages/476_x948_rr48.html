3.3Postpartum haemorrhage
Primary PPH is blood loss ≥ 500 mL after vaginal birth or ≥ 1000 mL at caesarean within 24 h, or any loss that makes her unstable. It is a leading cause of maternal death; uterine atony causes most cases.
1Definition and classification
  • Primary: within 24 h
  • Secondary: abnormal bleeding from 24 h to 12 wk
  • Minor 500–1000 mL, stable
  • Major over 1000 mL or shock
  • Placental bed takes 500–800 mL/min; crossing fibres act as living ligatures
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Loss is underestimated (clot in a soft uterus); tachycardia comes first, low BP means ≥ 30% of volume is lost
2Causes: the 4 Ts
  • Tone (about 70%): overdistension (twins, polyhydramnios, macrosomia), grand multipara, fibroids, long or fast labour, chorioamnionitis, MgSO₄, nifedipine, anaemia
  • Trauma (20%): instrumental birth, precipitate labour, macrosomia, VBAC
  • Tissue (10%): retained placenta, accreta, succenturiate lobe
  • Thrombin (under 1%): DIC (abruption, PE, HELLP, IUFD, sepsis, AFE), anticoagulants
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Uterus soft, above the umbilicus → Tone: atony
  • Uterus hard, steady bright bleeding after forceps → Trauma: tear
  • Twins, 18 h on oxytocin, 900 mL loss → Uterine atony
