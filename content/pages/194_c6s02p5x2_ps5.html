12. Supportive and surgical care
  • IV fluids, oxygen, paracetamol, NSAID if no AKI
  • Enoxaparin 40 mg SC daily (sepsis = VTE risk)
  • Correct anaemia; blood if Hb low
  • Breastfeed or express; baby checked (GAS)
  • Ice packs: perineum, mastitis
  • Evacuate retained products gently, after antibiotics start
  • Drain pus: breast, wound, pelvic abscess (US-guided, colpotomy, laparotomy)
  • Debride necrotising fasciitis early
  • Hysterectomy if the uterus is necrotic or gas-forming and she is not improving
Clinical scenario
Endometritis, 3 days of antibiotics, still febrile, bulky uterus. Next step?
Ultrasound for retained products; evacuate.
13. Complications
ComplicationClue and action
Septic shock, MODSLow BP despite fluids, lactate ≥ 4 · AKI, ARDS, liver failure → ICU, vasopressors
DICBleeding from drips and wounds, low platelets and fibrinogen → treat sepsis, FFP, cryoprecipitate, platelets
Pelvic abscessSwinging fever, pelvic mass, diarrhoea, tenesmus (pouch of Douglas) → drain
Septic pelvic thrombophlebitisUsually ovarian vein, after CS · about 1 in 3000 births · spiking fever despite antibiotics, patient looks well · CT/MRI; a diagnosis of exclusion → add therapeutic heparin
PeritonitisDistension, guarding, ileus · resuscitate, laparotomy
Necrotising fasciitisWound pain out of proportion, dusky skin, crepitus · urgent debridement
Secondary PPHInfected placental site or retained tissue (24 h – 6 wk)
LateTubal block → infertility, ectopic, chronic pelvic pain, wound hernia · death
BabyNeonatal GAS or GBS sepsis; feeding interrupted
Exam trap

Fever that persists on antibiotics is rarely "resistant bacteria" first: look for pus (abscess, wound), retained products, pelvic vein thrombosis, DVT and malaria.
