2Causes and day of onset
  • Endometritis is the commonest cause
  • Order by day: chest (day 1 after GA) → urine and womb (day 2–5) → wound (day 5–7) → breast and legs
  • Also retained products, infected haematoma, cannula site, epidural abscess, drug fever, malaria or typhoid in endemic areas
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • Day 1 after CS, crackles → Atelectasis, aspiration
  • Day 3, both breasts hard, 37.9 °C → Engorgement
3Organisms
  • Group A strep: most lethal; from a sore throat via hands; looks well, then sudden toxic shock
  • Anaerobes (Bacteroides): commonest in endometritis
  • E. coli, Klebsiella: UTI, endotoxic shock
  • Staph. aureus: mastitis, wound
  • Clostridium: after unsafe abortion
4Risk factors
  • Caesarean section (5–20 times the risk)
  • Long labour, ROM > 24 h, many VEs
  • Retained placenta, manual removal, PPH
  • Anaemia, diabetes, obesity, HIV
  • Sore throat in the family (GAS)
5Clinical features
  • Fever ≥ 38 °C or < 36 °C, rigors, tachycardia
  • Severe: low BP, confusion, mottled skin, little urine, pain out of proportion (GAS, necrotising infection)
  • Endometritis: foul lochia, tender bulky uterus
  • Breast: red wedge
  • Urinary: loin pain, rigors
  • DVT: tender swollen calf
