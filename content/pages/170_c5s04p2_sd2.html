8. Management: the drill
HELPERR · 30–60 s per manoeuvre; after L + P, the posterior arm is often the best next step.
StepHow and why
H · HelpMidwives, obstetrician, anaesthetist, paediatrician; one calls out the time, records
E · EvaluateEpisiotomy only to make room for internal manoeuvres; it does not free the shoulder
L · LegsMcRoberts: lie flat, hips hyperflexed and abducted, thighs to the abdomen → flattens the sacrum, rotates the symphysis up. Then routine gentle traction
P · PressureSuprapubic (Rubin I): assistant pushes down and sideways from the fetal back side, on the back of the anterior shoulder → shoulder adducts into the oblique. Steady, then rocking
E · EnterRubin II: push the back of the anterior shoulder toward the chest · Woods screw: add pressure on the front of the posterior shoulder · reverse Woods: rotate the other way
R · RemovePosterior arm: hand behind, flex the elbow, take the hand, sweep it across chest and face → shoulder-to-axilla is narrower
R · RollAll fours (Gaskin): pelvis opens; gentle traction on the now-upper posterior shoulder, repeat internal steps
