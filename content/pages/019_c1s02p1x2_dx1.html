Amenorrhoea is not always pregnancy

Other causes of amenorrhoea include stress, weight loss, heavy exercise, breastfeeding, polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), thyroid disease, high prolactin, injectable contraception (DMPA) and the menopause. In addition, vaginal bleeding does not exclude pregnancy: it may be implantation bleeding, a threatened miscarriage or an ectopic pregnancy.

Pseudocyesis (false pregnancy)

In pseudocyesis the woman firmly believes that she is pregnant. She may have amenorrhoea, nausea, feel "movements" and even have an enlarged abdomen from fat, gas or lordosis. The hCG test is negative and the uterus is empty on ultrasound. The condition is often related to pressure from infertility. The diagnosis should be explained gently, the family involved, and the woman referred for counselling.
