5. Investigations
Bile acids> 10 µmol/L (fasting). The key test; ≥ 100 = high fetal risk
LFTALT, AST, GGT 2–3 × normal. ALP is not useful (made by the placenta)
ClottingPT: vitamin K deficiency
Exclude othersUS liver and biliary tree · viral serology (hepatitis A, B, C, E, CMV, EBV) · antimitochondrial and anti-smooth muscle antibodies
Best next step

Itching + normal tests: repeat LFT and bile acids every 1–2 wk. The itch can come weeks before the blood tests turn abnormal.

Clinical scenario
32 wk, itchy palms at night, no rash, normal LFT and bile acids. Next?
Repeat LFT and bile acids in 1–2 wk.
6. Differential diagnosis

Itch: eczema, scabies, polymorphic eruption, pemphigoid gestationis (all have a rash). Abnormal LFT: PE / HELLP (BP, platelets) · AFLP (vomiting, low glucose) · viral hepatitis · gallstones · primary biliary cholangitis (AMA +) · autoimmune hepatitis (ASMA +) · drugs.

Distinguishing features
Itch, no rash, high bile acidsObstetric cholestasis
Itch with blisters round the navelPemphigoid gestationis
Raised LFT + BP 160/110, low plateletsHELLP
High ALP only, no itch, 34 wkNormal (placental ALP)
7. Complications
MaternalDistress from itch, poor sleep · vitamin K deficiency → PPH (fat-soluble vitamins not absorbed) · anxiety. No lasting liver damage
FetalSudden stillbirth (small risk, rises with bile acids ≥ 100) · meconium-stained liquor · preterm birth (spontaneous or induced) · neonatal respiratory distress
Exam trap

A normal CTG does not exclude sudden fetal death in cholestasis: the death is sudden (bile acids on the fetal heart), not from slow placental failure. Plan delivery by the bile acid level.
