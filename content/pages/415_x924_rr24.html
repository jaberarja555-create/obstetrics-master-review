  • BP 170/115 at 33 wk with headache → IV hydralazine (or labetalol) + MgSO₄

7Magnesium sulphate
  • Prevents and treats eclamptic fits
  • Loading 4 g IV over 5–15 min, then 1 g/h for 24 h after birth or the last fit
  • Toxicity in order: loss of knee jerk → respiratory depression (RR below 12) → cardiac arrest
  • Keep urine ≥ 30 mL/h (kidneys clear it)
  • Antidote: 10 mL of 10% calcium gluconate IV slowly
  • Fit on MgSO₄: extra 2 g IV
  • Fluids ≤ 80 mL/h: leaky capillaries cause pulmonary oedema
🩺 Exam scenarios
  • On MgSO₄, reflexes absent, RR 16 → Stop the infusion, check the level
  • On MgSO₄, RR 8 → Calcium gluconate + O₂
⚠️ Exam trap
  • MgSO₄ prevents fits; it does not lower BP
8Eclampsia and HELLP
  • Eclampsia: call for help, left lateral, airway, O₂; do not hold her down
  • MgSO₄ 4 g IV then 1 g/h (diazepam only if no magnesium)
  • Lower BP if ≥ 160/110; check glucose and malaria; catheter
  • Deliver once stable (within 6–12 h)
  • HELLP: haemolysis (LDH raised), elevated liver enzymes, platelets below 100; BP may be only mildly raised; treat as severe PE and deliver; risks DIC, liver rupture
⚠️ Exam traps
  • Epigastric pain and vomiting at 34 wk = HELLP until proven otherwise, not gastritis
  • HELLP is severe whatever the BP
9Delivery and postpartum care
  • Gestational HTN or PE without severe features: 37 wk (NICE); local units often 38 wk
  • Severe PE, eclampsia, HELLP: deliver once stable, at any gestation; steroids under 34 wk
  • Induction preferred; CS if the cervix is unfavourable and birth is not near, or fetal distress
  • Continuous CTG; epidural if platelets over 80
