7Complications
MaternalDistress from itch, poor sleep · vitamin K deficiency → PPH (fat-soluble vitamins not absorbed) · anxiety. No lasting liver damage
FetalSudden stillbirth (small risk, rises with bile acids ≥ 100) · meconium-stained liquor · preterm birth (spontaneous or induced) · neonatal respiratory distress
Exam trap

A normal CTG does not exclude sudden fetal death in cholestasis: the death is sudden (bile acids on the fetal heart), not from slow placental failure. Plan delivery by the bile acid level.

8Management · antenatal
  1. Confirm and exclude. Bile acids, LFT, clotting; rule out other liver disease and PE. Book delivery in a hospital with a neonatal unit.
  2. Ursodeoxycholic acid 8–12 mg/kg/day in 2 doses. Itch improves in 1–7 days; LFT improve. No proven benefit to the fetus.
  3. Vitamin K (water-soluble, menadiol 10 mg oral daily) only if the prothrombin time is prolonged or there is steatorrhoea (RCOG 2022).
  4. Itch relief: emollients, e.g. aqueous cream with menthol. Chlorphenamine only sedates (helps sleep, not itch).
  5. Monitor: LFT and bile acids weekly (the peak level sets delivery timing); fetal movements; growth and liquor as indicated.
