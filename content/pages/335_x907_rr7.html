2.10Acute fatty liver of pregnancy
Third trimester vomiting, jaundice, hypoglycaemia, raised INR → Acute fatty liver of pregnancy
Twin pregnancy, male fetus, mild PE, then liver failure → AFLP
Jaundice with ALT above 1000 → Consider viral hepatitis (hepatitis E)
Cause
Fetal LCHAD deficiency · microvesicular fat · twins, male fetus, PE
Features
Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain · jaundice · hypoglycaemia · encephalopathy · polydipsia
Investigations
Glucose low · INR ↑, DIC · urate markedly ↑ · ALT moderate · Swansea criteria ≥ 6
AFLP vs HELLP
AFLP: glucose ↓, INR ↑ · HELLP: haemolysis, platelets ↓, hypertension
Treatment
HDU/ICU · dextrose infusion (glucose checks 1–2 h) · vitamin K, FFP · stabilise then deliver
Prognosis
Maternal death 18% · hepatitis E in the 3rd trimester: about 20% mortality
Exam traps
No regional block if platelets < 80 · test the newborn for LCHAD · AFLP needs delivery, not waiting
2.11Thyroid disease in pregnancy
Weight loss, tremor, goitre, eye signs at 10 weeks → Graves' disease: PTU
Hyperemesis, low TSH, no goitre or eye signs → Gestational thyrotoxicosis: no antithyroid drug
Painless palpitations 3 months after birth → Postpartum thyroiditis: β-blocker
Tests
Use free T4 · TSH low in 1st trimester (hCG) · TRAb in Graves'
Hyperthyroidism
PTU in 1st trimester (carbimazole embryopathy: aplasia cutis, choanal atresia) · carbimazole from 2nd trimester · PTU: liver failure
Contraindicated
Radioiodine · surgery if needed in the 2nd trimester
Fetus
Maternal TRAb cross the placenta → fetal/neonatal thyrotoxicosis (≤ 10%)
Hypothyroidism
Increase levothyroxine 25–30% when pregnant · TSH < 2.5 in 1st trimester
Postpartum thyroiditis
3–4 months · hyper phase: β-blocker only · hypo phase 4–8 months · recurrence 70%
Exam traps
Do not treat gestational thyrotoxicosis with antithyroid drugs · carbimazole up to 15 mg/day and PTU up to 150 mg/day are safe in breastfeeding
