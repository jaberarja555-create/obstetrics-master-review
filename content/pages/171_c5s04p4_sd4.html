10. Management: after delivery
Mother
  • Expect PPH: active 3rd stage + oxytocin 40 IU/500 mL over 4 h
  • Examine and repair the whole genital tract (cervix, vagina, perineum: 3rd/4th degree)
  • Bladder: catheter if injury suspected
Baby
  • Paediatrician at delivery: resuscitation often needed
  • Cord blood gases (pH < 7.25 = acidosis)
  • Examine arms, clavicles, humerus; watch for HIE
Document & debrief

Time of head and body delivery, which shoulder was anterior, each manoeuvre in order and who did it, baby's condition and gases. Explain to the parents; debrief the team.

Clinical scenario
After SD with McRoberts, what must you prepare for in the mother?
PPH and 3rd/4th degree tears.
11. Prevention
  • Elective caesarean if EFW > 4.5 kg in a diabetic or > 5 kg in a non-diabetic woman
  • Diabetes: good sugar control; deliver at 38–39 wk if well controlled
  • Previous SD: recurs in about 1 in 10; discuss caesarean or vaginal birth with an experienced team
  • Known risk: deliver in hospital, experienced person present, no mid-cavity instrumental delivery for a macrosomia
Why prediction fails

Scan weight can be wrong by 10–15%; about half of SD occur in babies under 4 kg, and most macrosomic babies deliver normally. So most cases cannot be predicted: every labour ward drills HELPERR.

Hospital protocol

Weigh the risk in the antenatal card (diabetes, macrosomia, previous SD) and book delivery in a hospital with theatre. In a peripheral unit, where caesarean is far, symphysiotomy remains a life-saving last resort.
