12. Management of the other Ts
TissueRetained placenta = not out 30 min after active management (60 min if physiological). Oxytocin, empty bladder → manual removal under anaesthesia in theatre + antibiotics. Will not separate: suspect accreta.
TraumaGood light, speculum: repair cervical, vaginal, perineal tears · rupture → laparotomy. Inversion (shock > loss, no fundus felt): push it back at once, then oxytocin.

13. Secondary PPH
  • Causes: endometritis, retained products; rarely choriocarcinoma
  • Clues: crampy pain, uterus too large, open os, tissue passed, fever, smelly lochia
  • Tests: FBC, cultures (HVS, blood), scan, β-hCG if it persists
  • Treat: IV antibiotics (cephalosporin + metronidazole) · uterotonics · evacuation if tissue retained
14. Prevention
  • Antenatal: treat anaemia · high-risk women deliver where blood and theatre exist
  • Labour: partograph, avoid prolonged labour
  • Active management of the 3rd stage in every birth:
  1. Oxytocin 10 IU IM within 1 min of birth · no oxytocin: misoprostol 600 µg orally
  2. Delayed cord clamping (1–3 min) · controlled cord traction once the uterus contracts, guarding it
  3. Uterine massage after the placenta, then check it is complete
Hospital protocol

A ready PPH box: oxytocin (fridge), ergometrine, misoprostol, TXA, cannulae. Watch every mother for the first 4 h: fundus, pulse, BP, pad.
