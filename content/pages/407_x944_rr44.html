13Molar pregnancy (GTD)
  • Swollen villi and trophoblast overgrowth with very high hCG
  • Complete mole: empty ovum + sperm, 46,XX; no fetus; large for dates; malignant change about 15%
  • Partial mole: 69,XXY triploid; abnormal fetus; malignant change about 0.5%
  • Features: bleeding, grape-like vesicles, hyperemesis, pre-eclampsia before 20 wk, hyperthyroidism, theca lutein cysts
  • Scan: snowstorm, no fetus
  • Bloods: hCG, TFT; chest X-ray for lung spread
🩺 Exam scenario
  • BP 160/110 at 14 wk, uterus large for dates, no fetal heart → Complete mole
14Molar pregnancy: management
  • Suction evacuation; no misoprostol before; oxytocin only once the uterus is emptying
  • Histology, then serial hCG until normal
  • No pregnancy until follow-up ends: condoms or the pill; no IUCD while hCG is raised
  • HCG plateau or rise = persistent GTN: FIGO score, methotrexate if ≤ 6
  • Haemoptysis with rising hCG after a mole → choriocarcinoma
3.2Antepartum haemorrhage
Antepartum haemorrhage is bleeding from the genital tract from 24 wk (28 wk in local definitions) until the end of the second stage; about 3–5% of pregnancies. The main causes are placenta praevia and abruption; vasa praevia and uterine rupture are rare but deadly.
1Causes and assessment
  • Placental: praevia, abruption, marginal sinus bleed
  • Non-placental: vasa praevia, uterine rupture, show, cervical polyp or cancer
  • Unexplained: still raises preterm birth, FGR, stillbirth
  • History: amount, colour, pain, trigger, movements, previous CS
  • No digital PV until a scan excludes praevia; speculum then safe
