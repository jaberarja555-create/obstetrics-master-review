1.2Diagnosis of Pregnancy

1. Introduction

The diagnosis of pregnancy is made in three steps. Pregnancy is first suspected from the history, it is then confirmed by detecting the pregnancy hormone human chorionic gonadotrophin (hCG), and finally its site, number and age are established by ultrasound.

Pregnancy should be considered in every woman of reproductive age who presents with amenorrhoea, vomiting, abdominal pain or vaginal bleeding. It must also be excluded before an X-ray, before starting a drug that may harm the fetus (a teratogenic drug), and before any operation.

2. Clinical features

The symptoms and signs of pregnancy are traditionally divided into three groups according to how strongly they prove pregnancy. Presumptive features are noticed by the woman herself and can have other causes. Probable features are found by the examiner but are still not proof. Only the positive features, which come from the fetus itself, prove that the woman is pregnant.
