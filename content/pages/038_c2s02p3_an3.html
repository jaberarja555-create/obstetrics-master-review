8. Complications
MotherHeart failure · PPH tolerated badly · APH · sepsis · VTE
BabyPreterm · IUGR · stillbirth · low iron stores · NTD (folate lack, not iron)
9. Prevention
  • Folic acid 0.4 mg daily from before conception to 12 wk. High risk (previous NTD, anti-epileptics, diabetes, obesity, sickle cell, thal, twins): 5 mg daily.
  • Iron + folate daily, booking to 3 months after birth (30–60 mg elemental). Hb at booking, 28, 36 wk. Treat malaria, hookworm (after 1st trimester); bed nets; space births.
10. Oral iron
DrugDoseKey point
Ferrous sulphate200 mg (65 mg Fe) 2–3× dailyFirst choice · black stool
Ferrous fumarate200 mg once or twice dailyAlternative
Ferrous gluconate300 mg (35 mg Fe) 2–3× dailyGentler, less iron
  • How: 1 h before food, with vitamin C. Not with tea, milk or antacids.
  • Response: reticulocytes ↑ at 7–10 days · Hb ↑ about 1 g/dL per week. Recheck at 3 wk. Continue 3 months after Hb is normal.
11. Parenteral iron
  • Indications: intolerant of oral iron, non-adherent, malabsorption, or Hb 7–9.9 after ~34 wk. Not in the 1st trimester.
  • Iron sucrose 200 mg IV over 30 min, 2–3×/wk: safest. Iron dextran: total dose over 4–6 h after a test dose; adrenaline ready (anaphylaxis).
12. Blood transfusion
SituationAction
Hb < 7, before 36 wk, no failureAdmit, find the cause, oral or IV iron, treat malaria and worms
Hb < 7 after 36 wkTransfuse: no time for iron
Heart failure or Hb < 5Transfuse at any gestation
Bleeding, sepsis, labour or CS dueTransfuse
  1. Sit her up, O₂, IV line. Cross-match 2 units of packed cells (less volume).
  2. Slowly: 1 unit over 3–4 h, furosemide 20–40 mg IV with the unit if overload.
  3. Watch RR, pulse, BP, temperature, chest, JVP every 15 min at first. Breathless, crackles or fever: stop.
  4. Treat the cause, then iron. Target is safety (Hb about 8), not full correction.
