# Kassala guideline — pages 101–114
## p101–103 Severe malaria (cont.)
- Artemether 80 mg/mL or 40 mg/0.5 mL: wt × 2 units insulin syringe. e.g. 10 kg, 40 mg/mL: 40 units now, again after 12 h, then daily for 6 days.
- Treatment: IV quinine or artesunate inj.
- Quinine infusion 10 mg/kg/dose 8-hourly for 7 days (renal impairment 5–7 mg/kg/dose); 600 mg/2 mL. Dilute 1 mg : 1 mL in D10, dextrose 1/2 saline or DNS over 4 h. e.g. 15 kg: 150 mg in 150 mL D10 over 4 h 8-hourly; or in dextrose saline + 7.5 mL D50 (1/2 mL/kg of D50).
- Severe anaemia: give quinine IM before transfusion, same dose, diluted to 60 mg/mL with water or saline, divided into both anterior thighs. Persistent hyperparasitaemia on quinine → consider exchange transfusion.
- Uncomplicated malaria 1st line: Coartem (artemether 20 mg + lumefantrine 120 mg paediatric; adult 80/480). 5–24 mg/kg artemether. Doses at 0 and 8 h on day 1 then BD days 2–3: 5–14 kg 1 tab (total 6); 15–24 kg 2 tabs (12); 25–34 kg 3 tabs (18); > 34 kg 1 adult tab (6); < 5 kg adjust by weight.
- 2nd line (failure: fever/parasitaemia persists or recurs within 4 weeks): DHA-piperaquine (paediatric 20/160, adult 40/320), 4 mg/kg DHA + 24 mg/kg piperaquine, once daily 3 days same time. Table: 20/160: 5–7 kg 1/2 tab daily; 7–<13 kg 1 tab; 12–<24 kg [blank]. 40/320: 24–<36 kg 2 tabs; 36–<75 kg 3 tabs; ≥ 75 kg 4 tabs. [table partly illegible/inconsistent; WHO: 13–<24 kg 1 × 40/320]
- 3rd line: quinine tab 300 mg, 10 mg/kg 8-hourly 7 days (10 kg → 100 mg = 1/3 tab dissolved in 3 mL, give 1 mL 8-hourly).
## p103 Congenital malaria
- Neonate with fever; mother had malaria in 9th month (most important). Febrile, pale ± jaundiced. BFFM +ve (confirm MAT), thrombocytopenia. ttt as severe malaria (quinine infusion).
- IV artesunate alternative: 2.4 mg/kg; 3 mg/kg if < 20 kg. Vials 30/60/120 mg: reconstitute with sodium bicarbonate 0.5/1/2 mL then NS 2.5/5/10 mL → 10 mg/mL.
## p104 Vivax malaria
- Dormant liver stage (relapse); can be severe. BFFM, MAT (rapid antigen test). Antimalarial (Coartem; artemether) followed by primaquine.
- Primaquine tab 7.5 mg (15 mg not available): 0.25 mg/kg/day once daily 14 days (protocol); references 0.15 mg/kg/dose BD 14 days [as printed]. e.g. 10 kg: Coartem 1 tab now, after 8 h, then BD 2 days; then primaquine 2.5 mg daily 14 days (1/2 of 15 mg tab in 3 mL, give 1 mL daily).
- Not < 6 months; absolutely contraindicated in G6PD deficiency. P. ovale same. Severe vivax as falciparum.
## p105–109 Sickle cell disease
- Common in Messiria and Hausa. Symptoms after 6 months (HbF falls). Dactylitis (early infancy); splenic sequestration (infants/young children: rapidly progressive pallor and enlarging spleen — life-threatening: ABC + transfusion; weigh, O2, IV line, group & cross-match, NS 4 mL/kg/h until blood); painful crisis (any age: joints, back, abdomen; hand-foot swelling; crying infant); priapism; severe anaemia; acute chest syndrome (chest pain, distress, hypoxia); stroke (adolescence).
- Painful crisis Inv: CBC + retic (aplastic crisis retic low; hyperhaemolytic high) ± BFFM/MAT if febrile.
- ttt: analgesia + antibiotic (ceftriaxone ± benzylpenicillin) + IV fluid (maintenance, 1.5× or double) + zinc (reduces crises).
- Analgesia ladder: ibuprofen 20 mg/kg/day (double dose possible; tab 200 mg > 20 kg; 400 adolescents) → diclofenac inj 1–3 mg/kg/dose (75 mg/3 mL) for severe pain → IV fluid → pethidine 1–2 mg/kg/dose (50 mg/mL) or morphine 0.1–0.2 mg/kg/dose (10 or 15 mg/mL) → further morphine → blood transfusion (whole blood, no furosemide; packed cells increase viscosity) → exchange transfusion.
- Fluids: ideal double maintenance (half orally, half IV); commonly 1.5× maintenance.
- Order: admit; cannula 24; diclofenac … mg IM now; ceftriaxone … mg 12-hourly; dextrose 1/5 (1/2) saline … mL 8-hourly; ibuprofen … 8-hourly; folic acid 1 tab daily; BFFM (MAT), CBC; ± fresh blood wt × 20 mL if Hb < 5 g/dL.
- No IV fluid before transfusion in severe/symptomatic anaemia (anaemic or impending heart failure, Hb < 5).
- SCD osteomyelitis (Salmonella): hot tender limb swelling; CBC, CRP, film ± ESR, X-ray (no changes < 2 weeks). Ceftriaxone 80 mg/kg/dose ± Ampiclox or benzylpenicillin for 6 weeks; or vancomycin 60 mg/kg/day 6 weeks; analgesia ± fusidic cream.
- SCD severe anaemia (anaemic/impending heart failure; Hb < 5 + retic): transfuse wt × 20 mL without furosemide (increases viscosity, aggravates crisis).
- SCD pneumonia: atypical (Mycoplasma) → erythromycin 125 wt/2, 250 wt/4 mL or tab 250 6-hourly.
- SCD stroke: headache, convulsion, weakness → exchange transfusion every 3 months + hydroxyurea. Exchange also for severe acute chest syndrome, priapism, severe crisis not responding to simple transfusion, sickle hepatopathy.
- Supportive: folic acid; penicillin V (Ospenin) prophylaxis under 5: < 2 y 125 mg (2.5 mL) BD, > 2 y 250 mg (5 mL) BD (tab 500); Halorange (antioxidant/omega-3) < 6 months 2.5 mL, > 6 months 5 mL, adult 10 mL daily; zinc.
## p110–111 Scorpion sting
- Pain, vomiting, sweating; check pulse and BP. Shock is anaphylactic or neurogenic (not hypovolaemic): IV fluid may spread toxin; if needed 1/3–1/2 maintenance.
- Plan: ABC + local analgesia + antihistamine (> 1–2 y 1/4 mL, 2–6 y 1/2 mL, > 6 y 1 mL [chlorphenamine inj]) + antiscorpion + hydrocortisone 4–5 mg/kg. Anaphylactic shock → IM adrenaline first. Antiscorpion IV or IM by clinical grade:
  - history only, no symptoms → no antivenom; antihistamine + hydrocortisone, observe 6 h, discharge;
  - local pain/paraesthesia → 1–2 vials;
  - local + cranial/autonomic/somatic neuromuscular dysfunction → 5 vials in 100 mL NS over 1/2 h.
- Calcium gluconate evidence (smooth muscle relaxation); check pulse first. Shock/hypotension (infants) → adrenaline infusion; hypertension (adolescents) → nifedipine 0.25–0.5 mg/kg/dose BD/TDS (tab 10/20). Dopamine for persistent hypotension.
- Dopamine: 2–5 mcg/kg/min renal (vasodilator, β), 5–10 cardiac (vasoconstriction, α) [as printed]. mg per 24 h = wt × dose × 60 × 24 / 1000 (usually 10); ampoule 200 mg/5 mL (1 mL = 40 mg; printed "50"). e.g. 10 kg × 10 → 144 mg ≈ 150 mg (printed 3 mL) in 100 mL NS over 24 h.
## p112–113 Shock
- Inadequate tissue perfusion: lethargic, rapid weak pulse, cold extremities, CRT > 3 s, oliguria, hypotension or unrecordable BP.
- Well nourished: NS or RL 20 mL/kg bolus (wt × 20) over 5 min; reassess; repeat up to 3 times; then septic shock → broad-spectrum antibiotic (ceftriaxone) + transfusion; NS 4 mL/kg/h until blood ready.
- Malnourished: D10 5 mL/kg + O2 + RL (or NS) 15 mL/kg over 1 h; improved → repeat then ReSoMal; not improved → transfuse. e.g. 10 kg: D10 50 mL now + O2; RL 150 mL over 1 h.
- Notes: tachycardia earliest sign, hypotension late; fluids first for most shock; hypovolaemic commonest; warm septic shock (fever, wide pulse pressure, flushed); cardiogenic with CHD/heart failure; neonates and cardiac patients smaller boluses 5–10 mL/kg.
- Drugs: dopamine (cardiogenic), adrenaline (septic), hydrocortisone (vasopressor-resistant, purpura, adrenal insufficiency), prostaglandin E 0.05–0.1 mcg/kg/min in neonatal shock (duct-dependent).
## p114 PEM / SAM
- Marasmus, kwashiorkor, marasmic kwashiorkor; wasting, oedema, skin changes, low anthropometry.
- Admit: SAM + complication (dehydration, anaemia, hypoglycaemia…), general danger signs, bilateral oedema ++ or more.
- Dehydration commonest complication; signs: tachycardia, hypotension, oliguria/anuria, history of loss. Skin pinch and sunken eyes unreliable; no some/severe grading in SAM.
- Plan SAM + dehydration: ReSoMal + F75 + antibiotics ± vitamin A ± metronidazole + zinc.
- ReSoMal 5 mL/kg every 1/2 h for 2 h (or 20 mL/kg over 2 h), then alternate ReSoMal and F75 5–10 mL/kg hourly for 10 h [printed "10 mL/kg hourly"]; reassess: still dehydrated → repeat; hydrated → ReSoMal after each motion 15–30 mL/kg (30 in kwashiorkor) [printed; WHO 50–100 mL per stool].
