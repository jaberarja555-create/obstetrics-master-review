# Kassala guideline — pages 31–44
## p31 Viral myocarditis & dilated cardiomyopathy
- Commonest cause of heart failure without murmur. Hx URTI/viral illness, no CHD. O/E heart failure (tachycardia, tachypnoea, tender liver) or gallop. Inv CBC (may be unremarkable), ESR, CXR (cardiomegaly). ttt as heart failure (O2, furosemide ± spironolactone).
## p32–34 Rheumatic fever
- 5–15 y (rare < 3 y). Fever for weeks, joint pain & swelling, chorea, ± SOB (RHD); Hx recurrent tonsillitis. O/E unwell, febrile, tachycardia disproportionate to fever or persisting (carditis) ± murmur.
- Inv: BFFM, CBC, urine; ESR, ASO titre/CRP; CXR, ECG, echo.
- Jones: 1 major + 2 minor or 2 major. 2015: subclinical (echo) carditis = major; monoarthritis & polyarthralgia major in high-risk areas (Kordofan, Darfur, White Nile, Al Jazirah); recurrent RF: 3 minor diagnostic; ESR ≥ 60 low-risk, ≥ 30 high-risk area.
- ttt: aspirin + PPI (omeprazole; aspirin → erosive gastric ulcer) + benzathine penicillin inj (after sensitivity test).
- Benzathine penicillin: 600,000 IU < 7 y (< 27 kg) [printed "6000,000"; 600,000 not available → use 1.2 MU vial: dissolve in 4 mL, give 2 mL]; 1.2 MU > 7–12 y; 2.4 MU > 12 y. IM every 3 weeks for 25 years (no carditis) or lifelong (RHD) [as printed; RHD protocol]. Vial powder + 4 mL water; lidocaine may reduce pain.
- Penicillin allergy: erythromycin < 7 y 250 mg BD, > 7 y 500 mg BD for 10 days and for the duration of secondary prophylaxis.
- Aspirin 75 mg/kg/day for 4 weeks then taper, with PPI. e.g. 20 kg → 1500 mg/day = 5 × 300 mg tabs (2 after breakfast, 2 after lunch, 1 after supper) or 15 × 100 mg (5-5-5) + PPI BD.
- Prednisolone if aspirin not tolerated or no improvement: 2 mg/kg/day ÷ 6–8-hourly for 2 weeks, then taper by 5 mg/day every 2–3 days; at start of taper begin aspirin 60 mg/kg/day for 6 weeks.
## p35 Rheumatic heart disease
- > 5 y; recurrent tonsillitis → RHD → heart failure. SOB ± cough ± LL oedema; ill, distressed, ± ↑JVP, murmur. Inv as RF.
- O2 if distressed + benzathine penicillin (doses above) ± aspirin + PPI ± furosemide 1–2 mg/kg/dose; cardiac bed; may need spironolactone & captopril.
## p36 Rheumatic (Sydenham's) chorea
- Often isolated, latent up to 6 months, more in females. Abnormal movement, slurred speech, bad handwriting; darting tongue, milking sign, ± murmur, hypotonia. Inv ASO, CRP, ESR ± CXR ± echo.
- Haloperidol 0.01–0.03 mg/kg/day for 3–4 months (tab 0.5 mg); or chlorpromazine (Largactil) 0.5 mg/kg/dose for 4–6 months (tab 50–100 mg).
## p37–43 Neonatology; neonatal sepsis
- Neonate < 28 days. Common: sepsis, jaundice, birth asphyxia, seizures, haemorrhagic disease of newborn.
- Sepsis: fever / refusal of feeds / umbilical discharge (most important) but any system: diarrhoea, vomiting, cough, SOB, convulsion. Risk: PROM > 18 h, home delivery, maternal pyrexia.
- Inv: CBC (high TWBC, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia), CRP > 10, RFT; septic screen by symptoms (CXR if distressed, urine, stool).
- Ideal: cefotaxime + Ampiclox/ampicillin (benzylpenicillin if no ampicillin) + antipyretic + vitamin K ± IV maintenance or NG feeds. (Cefotaxime misses Listeria → ampicillin.) Ceftriaxone contraindicated in neonates, especially first 2 weeks (jaundice).
- Simple sepsis 1st line: benzylpenicillin + gentamicin ± antipyretic ± vitamin K ± IV maintenance/NG feeding. 2nd line (severe/not responding): cefotaxime + ampicillin, ± vancomycin if severely ill.
- Cefotaxime (vial 500 mg, 1 g): 100–150 mg/kg/day; wt × 50 BD first week, wt × 50 TDS from 2nd week; meningitis 200 mg/kg/day (wt × 50 6-hourly).
- Ampiclox/ampicillin inj (vial 500 mg): 25 mg/kg/dose 6-hourly usual; 50 mg/kg/dose BD first week. Both cefotaxime and ampicillin BD (wt × 50) in first week.
- Gentamicin 80 mg/2 mL: nephrotoxic, check RFT first. 2.5 mg/kg/dose 8-hourly; first week or < 2 kg: 2.5 mg/kg BD or 4 mg/kg once daily.
- Benzylpenicillin vial 1 MU: 100,000 IU/kg/dose 6-hourly usual; 1/4 MU BD first week or < 2 kg; 1/4 MU 6-hourly 2nd week.
- Vitamin K (10 mg/mL): prophylaxis 0.2 mg/kg (practically 1 mg = 10 units insulin syringe) IM once to every neonate; haemorrhagic disease 5 mg daily IV for 3–5 days (3 doses).
- Milk/IV volume: day 1 50–60 mL/kg/day; day 2 80–90; day 3 100–120; day 4 130–150; day 5 to 1 month 150. Divide by 12 (2-hourly) or 8 (3-hourly). e.g. 2.5 kg day 1: 150 mL/day → 12.5 mL 2-hourly. IV same amounts; some limit IV to 100 mL/kg/day (WHO). IV absolutely indicated: marked abdominal distension, preterm < 30 weeks, NEC.
- Preterm: day 1 80, day 2 100, day 3 120, day 4 150, day 5 180 mL/kg/day (max; up to 200–220 if tolerated). Excess fluid → PDA. No breast feeding < 30 (34) weeks (NEC).
- IV fluid type: first 48 h D10; day 3 to 6 months D5 1/5 saline; or give Na 2–3 mmol/kg/day as NS (0.15 mmol/mL) or 3% NaCl (0.5 mmol/mL) and the rest D10.
- NPO from day 3: add K 1–2 mmol/kg/day (0.5–1 mL/kg/day KCl; preterm 2–4 mmol = 1–2 mL/kg), Ca 0.5 mmol/kg/day (2 mL/kg/day Ca gluconate 10%; preterm 0.5–1 mmol = 2–4 mL/kg), Na 2–3 (preterm 2–4) mmol/kg/day. First 48 h: D10 only.
- Example 3 kg day 3 NPO: 360 mL/day D5 1/5 saline + 3 mL KCl (wt × 1) + 6 mL Ca gluconate (wt × 2) over 24 h = 15 mL/h. Alternative: Na 9 mmol = 60 mL NS (or 18 mL 3%) + 300 mL (or 342 mL) D10 + 3 mL KCl + 6 mL Ca over 24 h.
- Antipyretic in neonate: paracetamol suppository 125 mg (1/2–1/4) 6-hourly; infusion 1 mL/kg/dose (10 mg/kg); Adol drops 100 mg/mL 10 mg/kg (5 mg/kg if jaundiced) = 0.1 mL/kg (most accurate).
- Summary plans (Dr Hatem): Simple (1st line): admit nursery, IV cannula 24; benzylpenicillin 1/4 MU 6-hourly (12-hourly first week); gentamicin wt × 2.5 mg 8-hourly (12-hourly first week) or wt × 4 once daily first week; vitamin K 1 mg IM (or 5 mg IV daily 3 days if bleeding); D10 (or D5 1/5 saline) … mL over 24 h (or ÷3 8-hourly); or NG tube size 5 with breast milk … mL hourly. Severe (2nd line): cefotaxime wt × 50 8-hourly (12-hourly first week) + Ampiclox/ampicillin wt × 25 6-hourly (wt × 50 12-hourly first week) (or benzylpenicillin 1/4 MU 6-hourly); vitamin K; fluids/NG as above.
- Ampicillin better for Listeria; trial of Ampiclox syrup if tolerated.
- Neonate with fever or cough, clinically and lab normal (TWBC normal, CRP −ve): no admission; oral antibiotic (amoxicillin syrup 125: wt/4 mL 8-hourly; cefpodoxime syrup; cefixime wt × 0.4 once daily 6 days; Ampiclox syrup wt/4 6-hourly) + Adol drops/paracetamol syrup ± NS nasal drops 6-hourly ± Mini Guava 5 drops 8-hourly.
## p44 Dehydration fever
- First 3 days after delivery (little colostrum, hot weather). Fever, otherwise well, may refuse feeds; normal exam; CBC & CRP normal. ttt: IV maintenance or formula until breast milk adequate + antipyretic, or D10 orally alternating with breast milk.
