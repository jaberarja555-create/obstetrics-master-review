# Kassala guideline — pages 73–86
## p73–76 Gastritis / peptic ulcer / H. pylori
- Epigastric pain ± poor feeding, vomiting (or haematemesis); epigastric tenderness.
- Inv: H. pylori ICT (not accurate, may be done in young children), stool antigen (more accurate), urea breath test (recurrent pain + past H. pylori, if available), endoscopy (ulcer; expensive), BFFM/MAT & SG (exclude malaria, giardiasis).
- Persistent vomiting or excessive loss → admit: cimetidine (or ranitidine) inj, or omeprazole/pantoprazole inj + IV maintenance.
- Cimetidine inj 100 mg/mL (2 mL = 200 mg), max one ampoule: 5–10 mg/kg/dose; neonate 5–10 mg/kg/day ÷ BD–TDS; infant & child 20–40 mg/kg/day ÷ TDS.
- Omeprazole inj 40 mg powder: superior to H2 blockers; not < 1 y; 0.5–1.5 mg/kg/dose. Pantoprazole (Pantodac) 40 mg inj 2.5 mg/kg/dose [as printed; flag].
- Order (Dr Hatem): admit; cannula 24; dextrose with … saline every 8 h; cimetidine … mg in the last 100 mL of the drip; BFFM, stool H. pylori, SG.
- Ranitidine inj 50 mg/mL [printed; usual 25 mg/mL]: neonate 0.5–1 mg/kg every 6–8 h; 1 month–18 y 1 mg/kg every 6–8 h, max 50 mg; slow IV (dilute 2–5 mg/mL, over 3 min) or infusion 25 mg/h.
- Ranitidine tab (150/300; 75 rarely): GERD, CP. Neonate 2 mg/kg TDS (max 3 mg/kg TDS); 1–6 months 1 mg/kg TDS; 6 months–3 y 2–4 mg/kg BD; 3–12 y 2–4 mg/kg BD max 150 mg (up to 5 mg/kg max 300 mg BD in severe GERD); 12–18 y 150 mg BD or 300 mg at night.
- Omeprazole tab/caps 20 mg: < 20 kg 10 mg (1/2), > 20 kg 20 mg.
- H. pylori triple therapy (omeprazole + clarithromycin + amoxicillin; no metronidazole — that is regimen 2 with bismuth + tetracycline if failure): amoxicillin 125 wt/2 or 250 wt/4 mL (or caps 250) 8-hourly 1 week; clarithromycin 125 wt/3, 250 wt/6 mL (or tab 250) BD 1 week; omeprazole 20 mg 1/2 tab BD 1 week then 1 tab daily for 1 month. > 45 kg/adolescent: amoxicillin 500 mg 2 caps BD, clarithromycin 500 mg BD, omeprazole 20 mg BD — 1 week; then omeprazole daily before supper 1 month.
## p77–79 Abdominal distension
- Mostly wrong breast-feeding technique (neonates). Others: giardiasis (gas), hypokalaemia (profuse D/V), constipation, NEC (preterm, coffee-ground vomit), ascites, peritonitis.
- O/E: tenderness (peritonitis), organomegaly; hyper-resonance → gaseous → SG most important.
- Inv: SG; serum K (only if frequent D/V); abdominal X-ray erect & supine if obstruction suspected; US if peritonitis/other obstruction.
- Giardiasis → metronidazole ± simethicone (Deflate drops 40 mg/mL, 20–40 mg/dose: < 2 y 0.5 mL = 10 drops, > 2 y 1 mL = 20 drops after meals and at bedtime).
- Hypokalaemia (K < 3; normal 3.5–5.5). KCl ampoule 20 mmol/10 mL (2 mmol/mL).
  Common hospital equation (no evidence base): [(4 − K) × wt/2] + (1 or 2 × wt) mmol per 24 h; ÷ 2 = mL; given in the maintenance IV over 24 h or ÷ 3 8-hourly, or in 30/50/100 mL NS if no IV fluid; sometimes orally.
  Evidence-based (PFCCS): K 3–3.5 → wt × 0.25 mmol over 1 h; 2.5–3 → wt × 0.5 mmol over 2 h; < 2.5 → wt × 0.75 mmol over 3 h. Rate not > 0.4 mmol/kg/h (some 0.5–1 mmol/kg over 1–2 h; or 1 ampoule per 500 mL drip). Oral Slow-K tab 8 mmol: 2–4 mmol/kg/day.
  Example 10 kg, K 2: (4−2)×5 + 10 = 20 mmol = 10 mL KCl in 1000 mL dextrose 1/2 saline over 24 h, or 3.5 mL in 330 mL 8-hourly, or 3.5 mL in 50 mL NS over 4 h 8-hourly.
## p80–81 Intestinal worms
- Enterobius (threadworm): commonest, autoinfection; anal itching at night; white worms; SG does not detect; sticky-tape test at night. Mebendazole 100 mg (5 mL syrup or 1 tab) BD for 3 days, repeat after 2 weeks (2.5–3 mg/kg/dose theoretically; 100 mg regardless of age/weight) ± betamethasone cream BD for itching. Treat the whole family.
- Ascaris & Ancylostoma: worms in stool ± abdominal pain/distension; pallor with hookworm (0.2–0.5 mL blood/worm/day). SG eggs; CBC microcytic hypochromic anaemia, eosinophilia. Mebendazole as above.
## p82–84 UTI
- Upper (pyelonephritis) / lower (cystitis). Crying on micturition, ± fever, irritability, vomiting; neonate: prolonged jaundice, weight loss, apnoea, collapse; older: loin pain (pyelonephritis), urgency/frequency, suprapubic pain (cystitis).
- Inv: ideal urine nitrite or culture; UG pus cells > 10 (concentrated samples mislead); adhesive bag in infants; US for recurrent UTI in infants (anomaly) or significant/uncountable RBC (stone). Uncountable RBC in menstruating adolescent may be normal — ask.
- ttt: increase water intake (most important) + antibiotic ± antipyretic/analgesic/antispasmodic.
- Options: co-trimoxazole wt/2 mL BD (cheap, free in emergency); cefixime wt × 0.4 mL once daily 6 days; nalidixic acid 50 mg/kg/day ÷ 6-hourly (10 kg → 1/4 tab 6-hourly or 1 tab dissolved in 4 mL, give 1 mL 6-hourly); cefpodoxime (Cefodox 50 or 100 mg/5 mL) 5 mg/kg/dose BD 7 days: 50 → wt/2, 100 → wt/4 mL.
- Recurrent UTI → urine culture & sensitivity. Admit with danger signs (febrile convulsion, vomiting everything, refusing feeds): ceftriaxone ± IV maintenance ± antipyretic/analgesia.
## p84–85 Nephrotic syndrome
- Periorbital swelling worse at night/morning; generalised oedema, ascites, pleural effusion.
- Inv: UG heavy proteinuria (++/+++; + not significant), RBC, casts; serum albumin < 2.5 g/dL; RFT; urine albumin:creatinine > 200 mg/mmol.
- Management: renal chart (p89, most important); antibiotic if infection (ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg/day "renal dose"); daily UG; BP 6-hourly; intake/output; prednisolone after infection treated and BP controlled: 60 mg/m²/day (or 2 mg/kg/day) in 3–4 divided doses, max 80 mg [printed; standard max 60], until remission, then 40 mg/m² (1.5 mg/kg) alternate days (max 40 mg) for 1 month, then taper and stop. Salt restriction; encourage movement (DVT).
## p86 Acute (post-streptococcal) glomerulonephritis
- Coca-Cola haematuria, oliguria, hypertension ± periorbital oedema; tonsillitis or skin infection 2–3 weeks earlier.
- Inv: UG uncountable RBC, RBC or granular casts, mild protein; ASO +ve (may be −ve after skin infection); ESR ↑; C3 low (not routine).
- Admit: renal chart; bed rest until hypertension and gross haematuria resolve; benzylpenicillin 6-hourly (procaine penicillin or penicillin V ideal; else benzylpenicillin or amoxicillin) to eradicate group A strep [printed "group B"]; BP 4-hourly, control with furosemide ± antihypertensive; restrict salt & fruit juice; fluid restriction if urine < 400 mL/m²: intake = 400 mL/m² + previous day's urine output.
