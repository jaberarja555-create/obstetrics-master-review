# Kassala guideline — pages 87–100
## p87 Acute kidney injury
- ↓ urine/anuria, oedema, hypertension (salt & water retention), lethargy/convulsion/coma (uraemic encephalopathy), signs of the cause (dehydration, hypotension).
- Inv: RFT + electrolytes (↑urea, creatinine; dilutional hyponatraemia [printed "↑Na"]), CBC (anaemia in HUS or CRF; ↓platelets in HUS), US (obstruction).
- Supportive: admit + renal chart; treat cause; fluid challenge NS 20 mL/kg over 1 h + furosemide 1 mg/kg; monitor urine; repeat if no urine; still anuric → restrict fluid to insensible loss + urine output; no response → dialysis.
- Dialysis: symptomatic uraemia not responding (uraemic pericarditis, encephalopathy), urea > 250, creatinine > 8 mg/dL.
## p88 Haemolytic uraemic syndrome
- Commonest cause of AKI in children; bloody diarrhoea days–weeks before; hypertension, haemolytic anaemia, AKI; E. coli O157.
- Inv CBC (anaemia, thrombocytopenia), film (fragmented RBC), RFT. ttt supportive: STOP antibiotics (most important; increase incidence and severity); renal chart.
## p89 Renal chart
- Daily: date, weight, BP, fluid input/output, UG (protein, pus cells, RBC, casts), urea, creatinine, Na, K, signature. UG daily early morning; RFT per situation (alternate days / every 2 days / daily if impaired); weight daily; BP 6-hourly. BP percentiles 90th/95th/99th.
- Surface area = √(wt × height(cm) / 3600). Oliguric phase = SA × 300; polyuric = SA × 3000; insensible loss = SA × 400 mL/day.
## p90–91 Febrile convulsion
- 6 months–5 (6) y; fever + convulsion from an extracranial cause (tonsillitis, otitis, pneumonia, viral, cold). Meningitis, encephalitis, cerebral malaria are not febrile convulsions.
- Inv: BFFM (MAT), CBC, RBG; UG, SG not routine; LP when meningitis suspected at any age; electrolytes not needed.
- Plan: ABC (side position, suction, O2 — airway before IV line); antipyretic (paracetamol suppository 125/250 mg 10 mg/kg; tepid sponging; diclofenac suppository 12.5/25 mg 1–3 mg/kg; Voltaren inj 75 mg 1–5 mg/kg [as printed]); diazepam beside the patient; investigations.
- Diazepam 10 mg/2 mL: 0.3 mg/kg IV (= wt × 6 units insulin syringe) or 0.5 mg/kg rectally (= wt × 10 units).
- Order: admit; cannula 24; paracetamol suppository 125 mg (1/2 = 50 mg) 6-hourly (or Fendol/ibuprofen syrup 6–8-hourly; or diclofenac inj 1–3 mg/kg IM now if high); diazepam beside patient wt × 6 units if convulses; BFFM, MAT, CBC, RBG.
- Do not start antibiotics before investigations (LP), so dexamethasone can precede antibiotics by 30 min if CSF suggests meningitis.
## p92–93 Meningitis
- Fever, convulsion, coma (rare), rash (meningococcaemia). Bulging fontanelle (< 18 months); meningism > 2 y (neck stiffness, Kernig, Brudzinski).
- Inv: CBC (neutrophilia), RBG (vs CSF glucose), CSF: pus cells > 5, glucose < 1/3 (printed "↓ 1/3") of blood glucose.
- ttt: dexamethasone + ceftriaxone ± benzylpenicillin (neonate: cefotaxime + ampicillin); or vancomycin + ceftriaxone (neonate vancomycin + cefotaxime) if very ill / not responding; antipyretic (paracetamol, diclofenac inj); IV 2/3 maintenance or NG feeds.
- Ceftriaxone meningitic dose 100 mg/kg/day once or ÷ BD, max 1 g BD [as printed; standard max 4 g/day].
- Dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg/dose 6-hourly for 4 days, 30 min before antibiotic.
- Benzylpenicillin meningitic 150,000 IU/kg/dose 6-hourly (vial 1 MU).
- Neonate: cefotaxime 200 mg/kg/day ÷ 6-hourly (wt × 50) + ampicillin 50 mg/kg/dose 6-hourly (wt × 50), or cefotaxime + benzylpenicillin.
- Order: admit; cannula 24/26; paracetamol suppository … mg 6-hourly (or diclofenac … mL IM now if high); diazepam … mg beside patient; dexamethasone … mg 6-hourly; ceftriaxone … mg daily (12-hourly) (cefotaxime … mg 6-hourly); ampicillin (benzylpenicillin) 6-hourly; dextrose saline … mL over 24 h.
## p94–95 Epilepsy
- Afebrile convulsion, normal before attack, ± aura; exam normal except CP. Investigations normal (RBG, Ca, electrolytes); diagnosis by EEG.
- By type/EEG: generalised → sodium valproate (all types); focal → carbamazepine (Tegretol); neonates → phenobarbitone maintenance 5 mg/kg/day.
- Valproate syrup 200 mg/5 mL, tab 200/500: start 5 mg/kg/day for 1 week, then 15 mg/kg/day ÷ BD (usual maintenance; increase with weight); not controlled → 20 → 25 → up to 40 (some 60) mg/kg/day max. LFT (hepatotoxic).
- Carbamazepine (not carbimazole; write Tegretol) syrup 100 mg/5 mL, tab 200: start 5 mg/kg/day, increase 2.5 mg/kg every 5 days to 10 (usual 15) mg/kg/day; max 20–25 mg/kg/day. Infantile spasms → prednisolone.
## p96–99 Afebrile convulsion
- Causes: hypoglycaemia, hypocalcaemia, hypo/hypernatraemia, epilepsy, hypertensive encephalopathy. Symptoms of cause (refusal of feeds in hypoglycaemia, carpal spasm in hypocalcaemia). Signs of CP (spastic, frog position), squint. Measure BP.
- Inv: RBG, Ca ± Mg, Na (if D/V) ± RFT; all normal → EEG ± MRI/CT. Treat cause; hypertension → hydralazine or nitroprusside.
- Hypoglycaemia: < 46 mg/dL well-nourished; < 54 malnourished; < 80 diabetic infant; < 70 older diabetic (mmol × 18 = mg/dL). Preterm may be lower in first 3 days.
  ttt D10 2–5 mL/kg, recheck in 1–2 h; still low → D10 maintenance 100 mL/kg/day. Malnourished conscious: D10 50 mL orally regardless of weight; lethargic/unconscious: D10 5 mL/kg IV then ReSoMal [printed; feeds per SAM].
- Hypocalcaemia (Ca < 8 mg/dL): Ca gluconate 10% 2 mL/kg loading then 1 mmol/kg/day (4 mL/kg/day) maintenance; infuse slowly over 30 min diluted 1:4 in D5 (rapid → bradycardia, cardiac arrest); monitor pulse.
  e.g. 5 kg, Ca 6: 10 mL in 40 mL D5 over 1/2 h; then 20 mL/day (7 mL in 28 mL D5 over 1/2 h 8-hourly, or in the maintenance drip over 24 h / 8-hourly bags).
  Persistent → check Mg (MgSO4 0.1–0.2 mL/kg diluted in D5 over 1/2 h) and PTH. Neonate evidence: after 2 mL/kg loading, 8 mL/kg/day for 48 h then 4 then 2 mL/kg/day. Permanent cause → discharge on oral calcium: Ca carbonate tab 500 mg 50 mg/kg/day ÷ BD–TDS; Calcicare syrup (10 mL: Ca 500 mg, Mg 150 mg, Zn 4 mg) 1–4 y 1/2 tsp BD, 5–12 y 1 tsp BD, adult 2 tsp BD; Osteocare syrup 150 mg Ca/5 mL.
## p100 Severe malaria
- Fever, convulsion, persistent vomiting; 14 features of severity (repeated convulsions, prostration …). Very ill, prostrated, febrile, pale ± jaundiced (haemolysis), hypotension.
- Inv: BFFM (hyperparasitaemia; negative does not exclude unless 4–6-hourly for 3 days), MAT more sensitive, ICT not reliable; CBC thrombocytopenia, normocytic anaemia, retics ↑, TWBC normal with monocytes; RBG (hypoglycaemia).
- Drug of choice: quinine or IV artesunate (new protocol); artemether inj not in new protocol but still used.
- Artemether 1.6 mg/kg/dose (forms 40 mg/0.5 mL, 40 mg/mL, 80 mg/mL — common last two). 40 mg/mL: wt × 4 units on insulin syringe, or wt/2.5 on a 3 mL syringe [printed; check — 1.6 mg/kg of 40 mg/mL = wt × 0.04 mL = wt × 4 units ✓]. 40 mg ampoule up to 25 kg; 80 mg above 25 kg.
